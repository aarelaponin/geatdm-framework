# E8 — The investment case for Progressa's roadmap, in four sheets

**Form of this example.** The four-sheet layout is the team's template and names no country. The values are Progressa's and are invented; none is derived from any real country's figures. Every table of figures is illustrative. All amounts are in thousands of US dollars (USD thousand), at constant prices.

## What this step does

The roadmap (E7) says what will be done and when. The investment case says what it will cost, who will pay, and what the country gets back. It costs every roadmap component, so that a minister can see the price of each decision and a partner can see where its money goes. Each line gives a low and a high estimate rather than one figure, because the cost of a shared system depends heavily on how it is bought: the World Bank found that procurement strategy can change the overall cost of an identity system by "25 percent to over 100 percent" (2018, page 7).

**Why identity costs so little.** The identity line (INV-07) is under 2 per cent of the total because Progressa reuses PNIA instead of building an identity for learners. For an order of magnitude of what building identity costs: a study the World Bank cites (Gelb and Diofasi Metz, Center for Global Development, 2018) puts the cost of a foundational identity system in a low-income country at "about $4–11 investment per registrant for enrolment and credential issuance" (*Understanding Cost Drivers of Identification Systems*, 2018, page 1). The World Bank warns that few data points stand behind that figure. Applied to Progressa's 11.5 million people of school age alone, not its whole population, the figure gives USD 46 million to USD 126.5 million, against USD 150 to 220 thousand for the link to PNIA. The comparison gives an order of magnitude only: the figure is the cost per person registered of a national identity system, not of a learner register.

Two rules shape this sheet. The Universal DPI Safeguards Framework asks for "diversified, phased and sustainable financing models" (principle O8, page 25); hence three groups of funders and a recurrent line before the partner money ends. And PAERA section 4.5, in its part on the budget (page 74), warns that a budget application "can take 2-3 years before funds are received"; hence the recurrent line is requested in wave 2 (C-05) for the budget year 2029.

## Sheet 4 — Expected returns over ten years (illustrative)

| Category | How the return arises | Low | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enrolment paperwork removed | Schools and district offices stop compiling annual returns and paper registers | 2,500 | 4,000 |
| Scholarships paid to the right learner | Each payment is matched to one verified learner, so duplicate and ineligible payments fall | 3,000 | 6,000 |
| Spreadsheet exchanges replaced | Statistics and examination staff stop re-entering data | 1,500 | 2,500 |
| Reuse by later services | Each new education service uses PLR and PNIA instead of building its own register and identity check | 4,000 | 7,000 |
| Planning on learner-level data | Teachers and school places allocated on real numbers | not valued | not valued |
| **Total valued returns** | | **11,000** | **19,500** |

**Return on the investment.** Over ten years, the valued returns are between about 0.9 times the cost (lowest return against highest cost: 11,000 against 11,720) and about 2.6 times the cost (highest return against lowest cost: 19,500 against 7,610). The case does not rest on the top of that range. It rests on reuse: the second and later services pay for neither a register nor an identity check.

## What the case does not do

It does not rank the components against one another by return; that ranking was made in step 6 by impact, urgency and feasibility, and the roadmap follows it. No public source gives a method for weighing a block's cost against the number of services that reuse it; the way this case reads reuse is the team's own experience.
