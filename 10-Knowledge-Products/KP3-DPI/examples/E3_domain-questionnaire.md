# E3 — The Digital Data questionnaire: the blank instrument and a completed extract for Progressa

**Form of this example.** The blank questionnaire, its two guides and the respondent's process are the team's instruments and name no country. The completed extract is rebuilt for Progressa.

## What this step does

The team sends one questionnaire to each domain's lead body, as the frame page of E1 assigns them. Each questionnaire comes with a guide for the respondents and a guide for the facilitator. PAERA section 5.3 asks one short question per domain; the questionnaire turns that question into sections and question topics that ask for evidence. The expansion is the team's own.

## The blank questionnaire

Every questionnaire follows the same order of sections, so that a respondent who has answered one can read another: first the domain's policy and governance, then its institutions or components, then its standards, then how it runs and connects, then security and trust, then how it is measured, and last its readiness for the future.

The Digital Data questionnaire has seven sections:

| Section | Title | Question topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data policy and governance | 1.1 to 1.4 |
| 2 | State registers and their keepers | 2.1 to 2.4, with the register inventory |
| 3 | Standards and identifiers | 3.1 to 3.3 |
| 4 | Data operations and integration | 4.1 to 4.4 |
| 5 | Data protection, security and trust | 5.1 to 5.4 |
| 6 | Measuring data quality and use | 6.1 to 6.3 |
| 7 | Innovation and future readiness | 7.1 to 7.2 |

Each question topic opens with one short lead question, followed by prompts that say what to describe, what to indicate and what to provide. Section 2 in full:

**Section 2 — State registers and their keepers**

**2.1 Which registers does your body keep, or rely on, for education services?**
Describe each register in a sentence. Indicate which body keeps it in law and which body keeps it in practice, if they differ. Provide the law or regulation that creates it.

**2.2 Is there one authoritative record of each learner?**
Describe where a learner's identity and enrolment are recorded today, from pre-primary to the end of secondary school. Indicate whether the record is on paper, in a spreadsheet or in a system. Provide a blank copy of the form or screen used.

**2.3 How are records in different registers matched to the same person or school?**
Describe the identifiers used. Indicate whether two registers share an identifier. Provide an example of a matching rule, if one exists.

**2.4 Who may read or change each register, and how is that recorded?**
Describe the rules of access. Indicate whether each access is logged. Provide the rule or procedure.

**The register inventory** (answer one row per register):

| Register | Kept by | Exists | Degree of digitisation | Interface for other systems | Real-time access by other bodies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | yes / partly / no | paper / spreadsheet / system | none / file export / service on the exchange | yes / no |

**Completing the questionnaire.** Answer every topic, even where the answer is "we do not have this". Name the document behind each statement. Where an answer is an estimate, say so. The head of the responding unit signs the completed questionnaire.

## The respondent's guide (outline)

The guide has ten parts: an introduction with the time required; the purpose of the assessment and its link to PAERA; how to work through the questionnaire as a team; how to answer (be specific, give evidence, say plainly what is missing, give dates); guidance section by section, saying what is assessed, which documents to look at, whom to consult, what a strong answer looks like and the common errors; the key terms; evidence and documents; common difficulties; a quality checklist; and where to get help.

One of its reference tables tells respondents what each stage of the scale looks like for data, so that they can describe where they are and where they aim to be. Respondents describe; they do not score. Scoring is done in step 5.

| Stage (UNDP Digital Development Compass) | What it looks like for education data | Your target, and by when |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | Learner and school records on paper; totals counted by hand | |
| Opportunistic | Some records digitised, each body with its own files; exchange by spreadsheet | |
| Systematic | Machine-readable registers with named keepers; data shared online between some bodies | |
| Differentiating | Registers linked by shared identifiers; data protection applied in practice; data used for decisions | |
| Transformational | Registers reused by every service that needs them; open data where the law allows | |

## The facilitator's guide (outline)

The facilitator's guide covers the facilitator's role and the link to PAERA; planning before the assessment, with a map of the bodies involved, a schedule and a session plan; how to engage each kind of body; how to run a session, ask questions and test evidence; a question-by-question guide; how to collect and validate data, including what to do when sources disagree; difficult situations; quality checks and the scoring rubric; what happens after the questionnaires come back; and the templates. Its templates are shown, blank, in E4.

## Progressa's completed extract (rebuilt, illustrative)

**Responding unit:** MoEYS statistics unit, with PNEA for examination records. **Submitted:** week 6 of the assessment.

**2.1** MoEYS keeps PEMIS, the school records and the teacher records. It relies on the PNIA national identity register and on PNEA's examination candidate records. The education act of 2011 names PEMIS as the source of education statistics; it names no learner register. *Documents: education act of 2011, section on statistics; PEMIS user manual 2023.*

**2.2** There is no single record of a learner. PLR, the learner registry, holds enrolment records for some learners and serves them over the exchange to PNEA alone; no law names it the learner register, and it holds no record for most learners. Primary schools keep class registers on paper. Secondary schools in four provinces keep learner lists in spreadsheets. Each school sends totals by grade and sex to its district office once a year; the district enters the totals in PEMIS. PEMIS holds no record of individual learners. *Documents: blank annual school return form; PEMIS data dictionary.*

**2.3** There is no shared identifier. PEMIS, PNEA and the school records each use their own school codes, and about one school in ten has a different code in PEMIS from its code at PNEA. Learners are matched at examinations by name, date of birth and school. *Document: PNEA candidate registration rules 2024.*

**2.4** District officers can change PEMIS totals for their own district. Changes are not logged. *Document: none.*

**Register inventory, as completed:**

| Register | Kept by | Exists | Degree of digitisation | Interface for other systems | Real-time access by other bodies |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| National identity register | PNIA | yes | system | service on the exchange (a read of a person by national number) | PNEA only |
| PEMIS (education statistics) | MoEYS statistics unit | yes | system holding district totals; school returns on paper | file export | no |
| School records (8,200 schools) | MoEYS | partly | spreadsheet | none | no |
| Teacher records | MoEYS human resources | partly | system for payroll only | file export | no |
| Examination candidates | PNEA | yes | system for secondary examinations | file export | no |
| Learner registry (PLR) | PLR, with no basis in law | partly | system holding enrolment records for some learners; paper class registers in primary schools | service on the exchange (enrolment) | PNEA only |

These answers go to step 4, where each one is checked against documents and against what the systems show.
