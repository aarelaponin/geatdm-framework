## Executive summary (outline)

**Current state.** All five domains are at the Opportunistic or Systematic stage (E5). Identity and the national exchange run; the ministry of education uses neither, and PLR, the learner registry, is not yet the authoritative register of learners. **Target state.** By the end of 2030, every learner has one record in the Progressa Learner Registry (PLR), linked to the national identity and reused by every education service. **Approach.** Three horizons; the first builds the foundation in four waves, each opened by a governance track and each closed by a service people can see. **Investment.** USD 7.6 to 11.7 million over seven years, of which USD 2.5 to 3.8 million in the first horizon (E8, illustrative).

PAERA section 5.7 puts identity and payment in its first phase and digitises the state registers in a later phase. Progressa already runs identity (PNIA) and payment (PayPro), so its first horizon connects them rather than builds them. It brings the learner register forward into the first horizon on purpose: every education service in Progressa's plans depends on it, and the gap register places it in the first band (G-05), after the three quick wins that share its score of 8. The legal basis (C-02) also opens the first horizon, although its gap, G-01, is in the second band. An amendment to the education act is new law, which E6's rule rates lowest on feasibility. The learner register depends on it, so E6's rule for dependencies across bands schedules it no later than the register, and starts it in the first wave because it is the slowest to pass.

## Part 3 — The first horizon in detail (in full)

The first horizon runs for 24 months, from January 2027 to December 2028, in four waves of six months. Every wave opens with a governance track, because the domain tracks cannot start lawfully or be paid for without it. Every wave closes with a wave summary that says what must be true before the next wave starts. Each component moves through the five life-cycle stages of the Universal DPI Safeguards Framework (conception and scoping, strategy and design, development, deployment, operations and maintenance), and the governance track checks the safeguards at each stage.

### Wave 1 — January to June 2027: decide, and join what already runs

**Governance track.** The joint board of MoEYS and PDGA is set up with written terms and meets monthly (C-01). The legal unit of MoEYS drafts the amendment to the education act and the regulation on children's data, and the board sends them to cabinet (C-02).

**Domain tracks.**
- *Data.* MoEYS, PNEA and the statistics unit agree one school identifier and map the 8,200 schools to it; each school's connectivity is recorded against the same identifier (C-03).
- *Interoperability.* MoEYS joins Linkup as a member and sets up its access point with PDGA (C-04).

**Beacon.** None yet. The quick wins of this wave (C-01, C-03, C-04) are the visible results.

**Wave summary.** Before wave 2 starts: the board has met at least four times; the school identifier is in use in PEMIS and at PNEA; MoEYS can call a test service on Linkup; the draft amendment is with cabinet.

### Wave 2 — July to December 2027: build the register

**Governance track.** MoEYS asks the finance ministry for a recurrent budget line from the budget year 2029, for running PLR and the other shared systems (C-05). The board approves the data-quality rules and names the data steward.

**Domain tracks.**
- *Data.* PLR is made the authoritative register by loading learners' records through Giga's five tiers: raw, the records as received; bronze, where every row is checked against the quality gates and a row that fails is set aside with its reason; staging, where a person reviews the rows that passed; silver, which receives only the rows that person approves; and gold, one trusted record per learner. Until the amendment of C-02 is in force, PLR serves as the authoritative register in one pilot province only, with parents' consent under the data protection act (C-06).
- *Identity.* PLR and the registration service are registered as clients of PNIA's sign-in. A parent, and a learner who holds an identity number, is checked by signing in with the person present: the sign-in happens on PNIA's own screens and does not pass over Linkup. PLR keeps the identifier PNIA gives to it for that person, its sector token, never the national number. PNIA's read of a person by national number on Linkup stays with PNEA. Children below the age of issue receive a provisional learner identifier, joined to the identifier PNIA gives once their identity number is issued (C-07).

**Beacon.** None yet; the register is tested with the records of one district.

**Wave summary.** Before wave 3 starts: PLR holds the records of one district, and at least 95 per cent of them pass the quality gates; a test parent and a test learner over the age of issue can sign in through PNIA, and PLR keeps the identifier PNIA gives for each.

### Wave 3 — January to June 2028: the first service people can see

**Governance track.** The board reviews the safeguards of the registration service before it opens to parents: what data it asks for, who sees it, how a parent corrects a mistake.

**Domain tracks.**
- *Data.* The learner registration service opens in the pilot province. It writes each new learner to PLR (C-08).
- *Interoperability.* PEMIS receives learner counts from PLR over Linkup in the pilot province; schools there stop sending the annual return (C-09).
- *Access.* School and district offices in the pilot province register children for parents who have no smartphone, using the same service (C-10).

**Beacon B1 — register once.** A parent registers a child once. The parent signs in through PNIA, the service keeps the identifier PNIA gives it, writes the learner to PLR, and the school sees the record the same day. No form asks for the same fact twice.

**Wave summary.** Before wave 4 starts: at least 20,000 learners registered through the service in the pilot province; PEMIS totals for the province come from PLR; the assisted channel open in every district of the province.

### Wave 4 — July to December 2028: pay, and measure

**Governance track.** The board starts monitoring data quality and use of the shared blocks, and reports quarterly (C-13). The amendment of C-02 is expected in force by the end of this wave; if it is not, the second horizon waits (Part 5).

**Domain tracks.**
- *Interoperability.* The Payments block joins Linkup as a member and offers a payment-status service any member can call. PayPro does not join Linkup: the block reaches it through its payer bank (C-11).

**Beacon B2 — scholarships paid to the right learner.** In the pilot province, scholarship eligibility is read from PLR and the payment is made through the Payments block, whose payer bank pays through PayPro. Each payment is matched to one verified learner (C-12).

**Wave summary, and the end of the first horizon.** The foundation runs: a register, linked to identity, fed by a registration service, read by statistics, and used by a payment. The targets for the end of 2028 in E5 are checked before the second horizon starts.

## Part 4 — The later horizons (outline)

**H2, 2029–2030.** PLR and the registration service reach all 24 districts (C-14). PNEA registers examination candidates from PLR instead of school lists (C-15). Credentials from the Progressa Digital Credentials Authority (PDCA) are prepared for, not yet issued.
**H3, 2031–2033.** Parents and learners see their own records and the log of who has read them, and give or withdraw consent (C-16).
