# The scoring criteria

**Part of the KP3 assessment toolkit.** This is a blank instrument. It names no country, no institution and no person.

## What the scoring criteria are for

In method step 5 the assessment team places every sub-component of the five domains at one stage of a five-stage scale, and from those places computes a score and a stage for each domain. The criteria below say, for every sub-component, what places it at each stage. They are written down so that two assessors who read the same verified answers reach the same stage, and so that anyone who doubts a stage can see the criterion it rests on and the evidence quoted against it.

The five domains and their codes are those of the question bank (`question-bank.md`, beside this file): governance and policy with the legal framework (GOV), Access (ACC), Digital Data (DAT), Interoperability (INT) and Digital Identity (IDN). Each sub-component below lists the questions whose answers inform it.

## The scale: the five stages of the UNDP Digital Development Compass

The scale is the five stages of the UNDP Digital Development Compass, under the Compass's own names: **Basic, Opportunistic, Systematic, Differentiating and Transformational.** The Compass places scores on a range from 0 to 5, one unit to each stage, and gives no zero scores. The Compass's methodology page (https://digitaldevelopmentcompass.undp.org/methodology, Table 3) describes each stage for its row on digital public infrastructure in these words:

| Stage | Range of scores on the Compass | The Compass's description of the stage for digital public infrastructure |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 0 to 1.0 | "Paper IDs, traditional payments, siloed data." |
| Opportunistic | 1.0 to 2.0 | "Basic IDs, early digital payments, limited sharing." |
| Systematic | 2.0 to 3.0 | "Digital IDs, mobile banking, online data sharing." |
| Differentiating | 3.0 to 4.0 | "Integrated IDs, digital wallets, accessible data." |
| Transformational | 4.0 to 5.0 | "Beneficial ID/banking, widespread payments, open data." |

The Compass scores countries; it does not use PAERA's five domains. The way the scale is applied to the five domains and their sub-components, and every criterion below, are the team's own.

## The scoring rule

1. Each sub-component has assessment criteria written at three reference levels: the lowest stage (Basic), the middle stage (Systematic) and the highest stage (Transformational).
2. The facilitator places each sub-component at one of the five stages, using only verified positions from step 4. A sub-component that meets part of the Systematic criterion is Opportunistic; one that meets part of the Transformational criterion is Differentiating.
3. Each stage scores the middle of its unit: Basic 0.5, Opportunistic 1.5, Systematic 2.5, Differentiating 3.5, Transformational 4.5.
4. A domain's score is the mean of its sub-components' scores, to one decimal place. Its stage is the unit the score falls in: below 1.0 Basic, from 1.0 Opportunistic, from 2.0 Systematic, from 3.0 Differentiating, from 4.0 Transformational.

**An example of the arithmetic.** A domain of five sub-components placed at Systematic, Systematic, Opportunistic, Opportunistic and Opportunistic scores (2.5 + 2.5 + 1.5 + 1.5 + 1.5) ÷ 5 = 1.9. The score falls between 1.0 and 2.0, so the domain's stage is Opportunistic.

**Only verified positions are scored.** A first answer drafted by the AI assistant from the public record in step 2 tells the team where to look; it is never scored. A sub-component is placed only from answers that the questionnaires of step 3 collected and the verification of step 4 confirmed.

## How to read the criteria

For each sub-component there is one table of five rows, one row for each stage, with the score that stage gives. The rows for Basic, Systematic and Transformational are the three reference criteria of rule 1. The rows for Opportunistic and Differentiating say which part of the criterion above them is met, as rule 2 sets out. Read each table from Basic upward and stop at the highest row whose description the verified evidence fully meets. Where a row says "as Systematic, and …", every part of the Systematic criterion must be met as well.

A country may add a sub-component or merge two, as long as it keeps the five domains and their codes. A sub-component that is added needs its own three reference criteria, written before any answer is scored against them.

## GOV — Governance and policy, with the legal framework

### G1 Leadership and coordination

Informed by: GOV-Q03, GOV-Q04, GOV-Q05.

| Stage | Score | What places the sub-component at this stage |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 0.5 | No body decides on the digital infrastructure education shares with other sectors; each system is decided inside the project that pays for it, and no one in the education ministry is responsible for digital transformation |
| Opportunistic | 1.5 | Part of the Systematic criterion is met: a body or a responsible post exists, but the body has no written mandate over education's systems, meets irregularly or reviews only some systems, or the ministry's digital unit is not staffed to plan and run systems |
| Systematic | 2.5 | A body with a written mandate brings the education ministry and the national digital authority together and reviews every new education system before it is funded; the ministry has a named senior post for digital transformation with a staffed unit |
| Differentiating | 3.5 | As Systematic, and part of the Transformational criterion is met: the body's decisions are followed in the budget and in procurement for most systems, and its reviews are recorded and published |
| Transformational | 4.5 | Every system that education depends on is decided by that body against a plan for the whole of government; its decisions bind the budget and procurement; reuse of shared components is checked and reported every year |

### G2 Strategy and policy

Informed by: GOV-Q01, GOV-Q06, GOV-Q07.

| Stage | Score | What places the sub-component at this stage |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 0.5 | No approved national digital strategy names education, and the education sector has no plan for its digital systems |
| Opportunistic | 1.5 | Part of the Systematic criterion is met: a national strategy exists but has no education chapter or is not approved, or a sector plan exists only in draft or without costs, or there is no rule on reusing shared components |
| Systematic | 2.5 | An approved national strategy with an education chapter and a named lead body; an approved sector plan with costed actions traced to it; a written rule that new systems use the shared national components |
| Differentiating | 3.5 | As Systematic, and part of the Transformational criterion is met: most of the sector plan's actions are funded and under way, and the reuse rule is applied in most procurements |
| Transformational | 4.5 | The sector plan is funded, carried out and revised on a set cycle against measured results; the reuse rule is applied to every new system, and every exception is justified in writing |

### G3 Legal framework

Informed by: GOV-Q02, GOV-Q08, GOV-Q09.

| Stage | Score | What places the sub-component at this stage |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 0.5 | No law provides for education registers or for sharing data between public bodies, and education services require paper documents and personal visits by rule |
| Opportunistic | 1.5 | Part of the Systematic criterion is met: a general law on electronic government exists but names no education register, or data can be shared only under an agreement negotiated for each exchange |
| Systematic | 2.5 | The law names the main education registers, their keepers and who may read them; one general rule lets public bodies exchange data on a legal basis without a separate agreement for each exchange |
| Differentiating | 3.5 | As Systematic, and part of the Transformational criterion is met: most rules that required paper documents or personal visits for education services have been changed |
| Transformational | 4.5 | The law makes the digital record the authoritative one and provides that data is given once and reused; no rule requiring paper or a personal visit remains without a stated reason |

### G4 Funding and budgeting

Informed by: GOV-Q10, GOV-Q11, GOV-Q12.

| Stage | Score | What places the sub-component at this stage |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 0.5 | Digital systems are paid for only by projects; no budget line pays for running them once a project ends |
| Opportunistic | 1.5 | Part of the Systematic criterion is met: some running costs are in the state budget, but systems built with partners' money have no plan for their running costs after the project, or investments are approved without a written appraisal |
| Systematic | 2.5 | A recurrent budget line pays for shared digital infrastructure and is held by the body that runs it; running costs are planned before a project closes; every investment is appraised in writing |
| Differentiating | 3.5 | As Systematic, and part of the Transformational criterion is met: most appraisals count reuse and the cost over the system's whole life, and funding for the shared components is in the medium-term budget |
| Transformational | 4.5 | Every shared component is funded for its whole life in the medium-term budget; each investment is appraised for reuse and whole-life cost, and the result is checked after the system is built |

### G5 Rights and safeguards

Informed by: GOV-Q13, GOV-Q14, GOV-Q15.

| Stage | Score | What places the sub-component at this stage |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | 0.5 | There is no data protection law, or no body enforces it, and a person has no right over the data public bodies hold about them |
| Opportunistic | 1.5 | Part of the Systematic criterion is met: a data protection law and an authority exist, but the law has no rules for children's data, or the authority has too few staff to enforce it, or there is no published procedure for a person to use their rights |
| Systematic | 2.5 | The law is in force and enforced by an authority with staff; it has rules for children's data held by public bodies; a person can see, correct and complain about their data through a published procedure |

| Transformational | 4.5 | Every shared system is reviewed before use and whenever it changes; the reviews are published; complaints and reviews lead to recorded changes |

## The scoring prompt: an assistant proposes, a person confirms

**The problem it solves.** Placing twenty-six sub-components by hand, each against five rows of criteria and many pages of verified answers, is slow, and a tired assessor can skip a criterion. An AI assistant can read the answers against the criteria and propose a stage for each sub-component, quoting the evidence for every criterion it counts as met or not met. The assessor then checks each proposal against its quotations, which is faster and leaves a record of why each stage was chosen.

**The prompt.** The team runs it once for each domain, and pastes the domain's criteria from this file and its verified answers where the prompt says so.

```text
You are helping an assessment team score one domain of a country's digital
public infrastructure. You propose; a person decides.

DOMAIN: <domain name and code, for example "Digital Data (DAT)">

CRITERIA: <paste this domain's tables from the scoring criteria: every
sub-component with its code, its name and its five rows, Basic,
Opportunistic, Systematic, Differentiating and Transformational>

VERIFIED ANSWERS: <paste the domain's answers. For each: the question code,
the answer, the evidence attached to it, and the verification record that
confirmed it. Mark any answer that was not verified as UNVERIFIED.>

Rules:
1. Use only verified answers. List every answer marked UNVERIFIED under
   "Answers not used" and do not score from it.
2. For each sub-component, read its rows from Basic upward. Propose the
   highest stage whose description the evidence fully meets. A row that
   begins "As Systematic, and" also needs every part of the Systematic row.
3. For every part of a criterion you count as met or as not met, quote the
   evidence word for word and give its question code. A claim with no
   quotation behind it is not allowed.
4. If the evidence is not enough to place a sub-component, write
   "cannot place" and name the question that would settle it. Do not guess.
   Never fill in an answer that is missing.
5. Give each proposed stage its score: Basic 0.5, Opportunistic 1.5,
   Systematic 2.5, Differentiating 3.5, Transformational 4.5. The domain
   score is the mean of the sub-component scores, to one decimal place. The
   domain's stage is the unit the score falls in: below 1.0 Basic, from 1.0
   Opportunistic, from 2.0 Systematic, from 3.0 Differentiating, from 4.0
   Transformational. If any sub-component is "cannot place", do not compute
   the domain score.

Write four parts:
A. A table with one row per sub-component: code and name; proposed stage;
   score; the parts of the criterion met, each with its quotation and
   question code; the parts not met, each with its quotation and question
   code, or "no evidence".
B. The domain score with its arithmetic written out, and the domain's stage;
   or, if a sub-component could not be placed, what is missing.
C. Answers not used, and why.
D. Every point where two pieces of evidence disagree, quoted side by side.

Everything you write is a proposal for the assessor to confirm or change.
```

**Its inputs and outputs.** In: the domain's criteria, copied from this file, and the domain's answers as verified in step 4, each with its evidence and its verification record. Out: a proposed stage and score for every sub-component, each criterion quoted against the evidence, the proposed domain score and stage, the answers left out, and the conflicts found. The proposal goes to the assessor, who fills in the record of confirmation below.

**The safeguard.** The prompt proposes and the assessor decides. A stage with no quoted evidence behind it is not accepted. Where the assessor changes a proposed stage, the reason is written in the record, so that a reviewer can follow it. The answers describe systems and bodies, not people: no person's personal data is given to the assistant.
