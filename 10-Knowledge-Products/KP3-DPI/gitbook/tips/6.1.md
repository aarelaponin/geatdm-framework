## Example input

**[country X]:** Progressa.

**[paste the findings]:** the 15 findings of Progressa's findings register, F-GOV-1 to F-IDN-2, each with its sub-component and evidence. For example: F-GOV-1, GOV, G3: the education act gives no basis for a learner register or for sharing learner data (AF-GOV-02; the education act of 2011). F-DAT-4, DAT, D3: PEMIS, PNEA and the school records use different school codes for about one school in ten (questionnaire answer 2.3). F-ACC-1, ACC, A1: the connectivity of most schools is not recorded, and connectivity data and school records share no school identifier (AF-ACC-01; session V-04).

**[paste the criteria]:** impact, urgency and feasibility, each rated 3, 2 or 1 as in the rule of the gap report; the ratings added to a score of 3 to 9; first band 8 or 9, second band 6 or 7, third band 5 or less; a gap another depends on comes first inside its band, and across bands is scheduled no later than the gap that depends on it, keeping its own band.

## Reading the output

### 1. The trace from gap to finding holds

Every gap names its findings, and the two merges are visible: F-DAT-1 with F-DAT-2 in G-05, and F-ACC-1 feeding both G-06 and G-14. Feasibility 1 for G-01 rests on the rule itself, since an amendment passed by parliament is new law. That rating is earned.

### 2. "Quick win" is a promise about six months

The run marked G-06 a quick win because MoEYS can issue an identifier alone. Mapping 8,200 schools to it, with PNEA's codes differing for one school in ten, is real work. The run also reproduced the worked example closely, which shows that it follows the rule, not that the rule was applied rightly. Put each quick win to the workshop as a question.

### 3. Check the dependencies the run did not explain

G-03, the recurrent budget, depends on G-02, and the shortened table drops it. Check that every dependency has a reason a workshop can test. Then confirm each rating in the workshop and record every change with its reason.
