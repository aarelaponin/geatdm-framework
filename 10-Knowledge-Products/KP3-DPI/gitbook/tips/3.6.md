## Example input

**[paste] Counts of one load (invented: the term list of one pilot district).** Rows received in raw: 4,812. Passed at bronze: 4,590. Failed at bronze: 222. Approved at staging: 4,571. Rejected at staging: 19.

**[paste] Record count.** Before the load: 4,410. After the load: 4,576.

**[paste] Change log extract for the load.** Records added: 166. Records changed: 4,398.

## Example output

**Reconciliation note (draft)**

Line one: 4,812 rows received against 4,590 passed plus 222 failed, which is 4,812; it balances. Line two: 4,571 rows approved against 166 records added plus 4,398 changed, which is 4,564; it does not balance, by 7 rows. The record count rose by 166, which equals the records added. Staging also balances: 4,571 approved plus 19 rejected is 4,590 passed. The next load waits until the 7 rows are found and the cause written down.

**Differences to investigate**

- 7 approved rows have no matching entry in the change log. They may have been refused by the register when written, for example by its rule on school codes or on a unique identifier.
- They may be two approved rows for one learner, merged into one record at gold.
- They may be rows identical to records already in the register, written without a logged change.
- The change-log extract may cover a shorter time than the load.

## Reading the output

### 1. The arithmetic is right and shown

Both lines are computed with the numbers on the page, and the difference is stated as 7, not "about 0.2 per cent". The run added two cross-checks, staging and record count, that make the 7 rows easier to place.

### 2. The causes are places to look, not answers

The list is plausible, but none of it is known. The third item is the dangerous one: "identical rows, no change logged" is the explanation a tired team will accept, and it would hide a write that failed. Each place must be checked in the logs before it is ruled in or out.

### 3. Look for where the 7 rows went

A sample of the 4,571 approved rows should be confirmed in the register with its "exists" operation, and the 7 named row by row. Present the note as the team's own practice: no published source describes this reconciliation.

## What next

The note goes to the data steward and stops the next load until the 7 rows are explained. The share of rows passing bronze, 95.4 per cent here, is the measure the [roadmap](../examples/E7_roadmap.md) sets at 95 per cent before wave 3; the checks behind it are in [3.4](./3-4.md).
