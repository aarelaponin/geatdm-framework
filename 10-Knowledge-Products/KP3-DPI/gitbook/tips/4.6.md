## Example input

**[the sector]:** education in Progressa.

**[the inventory]:**

- Learner registration — MoEYS. Needs: who the person is, to keep a register, to exchange data. Owner agreed at the joint board.
- Education statistics (PEMIS fed from PLR) — MoEYS planning unit. Needs: to keep a register, to exchange data.
- Scholarship pilot — MoEYS with the Ministry of Finance. Needs: who the person is, to pay, to keep a register, to exchange data.
- Examination registration — PNEA. Needs: to keep a register, to exchange data.

**[the shared blocks]:** the identity block (PNIA's sign-in); the Payments block; the Registration block; the learner register (PLR); the data exchange (Linkup).

## Reading the output

### 1. The register's count matches the plan

Four services on the learner register is the same count the table of what to fund first uses, and the Payments block's single service is the scholarship pilot. Both come straight from the inventory.

### 2. Only one service is confirmed

Of the twelve uses counted, four rest on an owner's agreement, all from the same service. Presented as a count of four, the register's row overstates what is known. The finance ministry should see the "confirmed" column first.

### 3. The scholarship's need for identity is an assumption

The inventory says the scholarship pilot needs to know who the person is. It may instead rely on learners already verified in PLR, and never sign anyone in. Ask MoEYS and the Ministry of Finance; if so, the identity block's count falls to one.

This table becomes the reuse column of [what to fund first](../module-6/6-2.md), where each block's cost is set against it. The order that follows is the [roadmap](../examples/E7_roadmap.md)'s own.
