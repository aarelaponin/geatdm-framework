## Example input

**[paste] The charter.** The draft charter of 3.1: PLR owns the register; the registration service writes after a PLR officer approves; each reader sees only the fields it needs; a parent sees their own child's record.

**[paste] The schema.** learner_number, given_name, family_name, date_of_birth, sex, school_code, grade, enrolment_date, status, pnia_service_identifier, guardian_pnia_service_identifier.

**[paste] The services and their needs.** Learner registration: create and update records. PEMIS: learner counts by school and grade. PNEA: candidates for examination, with names and date of birth for the certificate. Scholarship pilot: whether a learner is enrolled, and in which school and grade. A parent: their own child's record.

**Test cases (one allowed, one refused)**

- Registration: create a learner, allowed; delete one, refused.
- PEMIS: read grade and school, allowed; read a learner's names, refused.
- PNEA: read names and date of birth, allowed; update a grade, refused.
- Scholarship pilot: read status, allowed; read date of birth, refused.
- Parent: read their child's record, allowed; read another child's record, refused.

**Marked choices.** PEMIS reads the learner number to count without double counting. The scholarship pilot reads PNIA's identifier to match a payment to one verified learner. The parent is matched on the guardian identifier.

## Reading the output

### 1. Write access sits in one place

Only the registration service creates and updates, as the charter and the schema specimen set. Every other row is read only, and every row has a test that must be refused.

### 2. PEMIS gets more than it needs

PEMIS needs counts, but the table lets it read one row per learner. A count service, or a read of totals only, would match the charter better. The scholarship pilot's read of PNIA's identifier is also a judgment, and an identifier given to one service should not be passed to another.

### 3. Check the law on minors, and the report of reads

Parental consent and guardianship must be checked against the data protection act before anything is configured. The table also says nothing about a learner over 16 with their own PNIA identifier, or about the report that shows a parent who read the record.
