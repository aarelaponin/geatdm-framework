## Example input

**[country X]:** Progressa.

**[paste the inventory]:** services planned for 2027 to 2031, with the blocks each needs.

- Learner registration: learner register (PLR), school identifier, PNIA sign-in link.
- Statistics from PLR (PEMIS fed over Linkup): PLR, school identifier, MoEYS on Linkup.
- Scholarship pilot: PLR, MoEYS on Linkup, Payments block on Linkup.
- Examination registration from PLR: PLR, school identifier, MoEYS on Linkup.
- Parent and learner view, with consent: PLR, consent service.

PLR needs the school identifier and MoEYS on Linkup; the PNIA link needs PLR; the Payments block's place needs MoEYS on Linkup.

**[paste the ranges]:** USD thousand, all illustrative, from the draft investment case. School identifier 150–250; MoEYS on Linkup 80–120; PLR 600–900; PNIA sign-in link 150–220; Payments block on Linkup 120–180. Consent service: no range.

## Reading the output

### 1. The order is set by dependencies, as asked

MoEYS on Linkup comes first because two blocks wait for it. PLR, the most expensive block, still comes third, ahead of two cheaper ones, because the PNIA link waits for it: cost does not set the order. That follows the rule in the prompt and agrees with the roadmap's waves. The consent service stays without a figure, which is what the rule requires.

### 2. Counting a service as "using" a block is a judgement

The run counted the PNIA link for registration only. A scholarship pilot that pays verified learners may need a sign-in too, and the count would change. Each count rests on a reading of the inventory; ask the owner of each service to confirm the blocks it needs.

### 3. The consent service is the gap to cost next

It has no figure, so it drops out of the order quietly. It still needs a costing before it can enter a budget. Running costs are missing too: every range here is a one-off cost.
