## Example input

**[the block or service]:** the learner register (PLR): the product and its first load, outsourced.

**[country X]:** Progressa.

**[paste the text]:** extracts of a draft contract, invented for this example.

- **4.2** The system shall comply with international interoperability standards and the GovStack Digital Registries specification.
- **7.1** At the end of the contract, the Supplier shall deliver to the Ministry an export of all learner records in CSV format.
- **7.3** Exports requested during the contract are charged at the Supplier's standard rates.
- **9.1** The Ministry is granted a non-transferable licence to use the Software for the term of this Agreement.
- **11.4** On expiry, the Supplier shall co-operate in good faith with any successor.

## Example output

| Check | Clause | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Open standards | 4.2 | weak | Names the Digital Registries specification but no edition; "international standards" names nothing |
| 2. Export | 7.1, 7.3 | weak | Records only, not the schema or the configuration; only at the end; charged during the contract |
| 3. Help with leaving | 11.4 | weak | "Co-operate in good faith" sets no period, no tasks and no price |
| 4. Licence | — | missing | 9.1 ends the licence with the contract; no clause lets the Ministry keep running the system |

**Questions for the lawyer and the procurement officer:**

- Which edition of the Digital Registries specification should 4.2 name?
- Can 7.1 cover the schema, the quality rules and the load configuration, and allow an export at any time at no charge?
- Should the contract set a transition period, the supplier's tasks in it and its price, in place of 11.4?
- Does an annex or the supplier's standard terms say anything more on the licence?

## Reading the output

### 1. The weak exit clause is plainly read

Clause 11.4 names no period, no work and no price, and the run says so in those terms. A ministry that relied on "good faith" would learn its meaning on the day it tried to leave. That reading is supported by the text.

### 2. "Missing" is a reading of the extract, not of the contract

The run marked the licence missing from five pasted clauses. An annex or the supplier's standard terms may say more, and calling a check missing is close to a legal view. The prompt rules that out; the lawyer reads the whole contract.

### 3. The data the ministry must own is wider than records

The export check looks at learner records. Leaving also needs the schema, the quality gates of the five tiers and the set-aside rows with their reasons. Check that the contract names the data steward as the ministry's own and the load configuration as the ministry's property.
