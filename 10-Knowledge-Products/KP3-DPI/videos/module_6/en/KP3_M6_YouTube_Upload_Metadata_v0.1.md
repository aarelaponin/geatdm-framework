**Title:** From assessment findings to a ranked gap register: impact, urgency, feasibility, dependencies

**Description:**

```text
Gather every gap into one register and rank each by impact, urgency, feasibility
and what depends on it, so the list you take to your minister is short and
ordered.

Education DPI Roadmap · Module 6 · 6.1 — From findings to priorities: the gap register

An assessment ends with many findings across five domain reports. Turn each
finding into a gap — the distance between what exists and what the country
needs — and let each gap name the findings it comes from. Rate impact, urgency
and feasibility from 1 to 3, add them into three bands, and let dependencies set
the order inside a band. Shown on the illustrative register of Progressa, a
fictional country: four gaps in the first band, three of them quick wins, and the
patterns across domains that explain the order to a minister.
```

### 6.2 — What to fund first: cost against reuse

**File:** `video/KP3_M6_6.2_Video_v0.9.mp4` · **Subtitles:** `audio/KP3_M6_6.2_Audio_v0.9.srt`

**Title:** What to fund first: weighing a shared block's cost against the services that will reuse it

**Description:**

```text
A shared block is paid for once and used by many, so judge each investment by its
cost against the number of services that will use it, a sum only the whole
government can do.

Education DPI Roadmap · Module 6 · 6.2 — What to fund first: cost against reuse

Every ministry asks for money for its own system, and each request looks
reasonable on its own. List each candidate block with a low and a high cost,
beside the services that will use it and the blocks that depend on it, and fund
first what others depend on and many services use. Shown on the illustrative
table of Progressa, a fictional country — and on identity, reused instead of
rebuilt: connecting to the identity authority's sign-in against building
identity for 11.5 million people of school age, an order of magnitude only.
```

```text
For each block decide what your own teams build, what you outsource and what you

Education DPI Roadmap · Module 6 · 6.5 — Sourcing each block without lock-in

Lock-in is rarely one bad decision; it is written into contracts, clause by
```

**Description:**

```text
Shared blocks stay shared when it is written down who decides money and policy,
who coordinates the programme, and who runs each block and sets its technical
rules.

Education DPI Roadmap · Module 6 · 6.6 — Governance that keeps shared blocks shared

Without someone with the power to keep a block shared, each new project builds its
own copy and pays for it again. Write governance on one page in three layers:
political, programme and technical. Two published examples show roles divided
this way — Estonia's data exchange and Brazil's instant payment system, Pix.
Then who may act for a learner: delegated access and consent, which do not
replace the law. Shown on the illustrative governance page of Progressa, a
```

```text
A roadmap becomes the government's own only when the people it binds have
commented, every comment has a written answer, and an authority has adopted it.

Education DPI Roadmap · Module 6 · 6.7 — Validate, revise, adopt

Plan the path to adoption from the start: six months from consultation, through
workshops, a drafting sprint and written comments, to validation and adoption.
```

```text
After launch, indicators read every month, quarter and year, quality checks and

Education DPI Roadmap · Module 6 · 6.8 — Keep the foundation healthy and safe
```

```text
At every step of the method and of the build there is a task an AI assistant can
draft and a check only a person can make, and no decision is handed over.

Education DPI Roadmap · Module 6 · 6.9 — The AI plays, step by step

A play is one task at one step, with a fixed input, a fixed output and a
safeguard — in the assessment, in planning, in governance and in the build. Every
play follows the same rules: the assistant drafts and a person decides; every
claim quotes its evidence; a missing answer is reported as missing, never filled
in; and no one's personal data goes into a prompt. Before you trust a play, run
it where the answer is already known: the scorer, given the verified answers of
a domain of Progressa, a fictional country, without the result, must reproduce
the validated table.
```
