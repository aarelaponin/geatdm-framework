### 1.3 — The once-only promise

**File:** `video/KP2_M1_1.3_Video_v0.20.mp4` · **Subtitles:** `audio/KP2_M1_1.3_Audio_v0.20.srt`

**Title:** Once-only: the one principle that tells you if your interoperability actually works

**Description:**

```text
The state never asks a citizen for the same information twice. That is the
outcome interoperability is for — and the clearest test of whether your
framework works.

GIF · Module 1 · 1.3 — The once-only promise

Without once-only, the citizen carries the same proof to the identity office,
the school and the clinic. Once-only is not a feature you switch on: it needs a
trusted source for each fact, a lawful basis and the citizen's consent, and a
real-time way to fetch the fact at the moment of service. A worked example from
Progressa, a fictional demonstration country: a learner applying for a
credential is asked once, while the examination authority fetches identity and
enrolment over the bus. And why once-only is the right north star: it is
measurable, and it keeps the framework honest.
```

**Title:** The two-page document to write before you build any interoperability

**Description:**

```text
Name the mandate, the scope and the principles once, in writing, before any
wiring — it is what every later decision is checked against.

GIF · Module 1 · 1.4 — The Strategic Foundation Document

One short document, before a single system is connected. Four questions every
later decision is tested against: mandate, scope, principles and success. The
principles to name — whole-of-government, once-only, reuse-first and
```

**Description:**

```text
Turn your integration map into a ranked catalogue of exchanges worth building —
and start with the one that removes the most burden from citizens.

GIF · Module 1 · 1.5 — The Use-Case Catalogue

A map of everything connecting to everything cannot be built. The Use-Case
```

### 1.6 — Mapping your stakeholders

**File:** `video/KP2_M1_1.6_Video_v0.15.mp4` · **Subtitles:** `audio/KP2_M1_1.6_Audio_v0.15.srt`

**Title:** Interoperability is an agreement problem: how to map the agencies you need

**Description:**

```text
A bus with no members carries nothing. Sort the agencies into Champions, Early
Adopters and Observers, and you know who to onboard first and who to convince.

GIF · Module 1 · 1.6 — Mapping your stakeholders

Interoperability is at least as much an agreement problem as a technical one.
Every exchange has two sides with different incentives: the consumer wants it,
the provider carries the cost and the risk. Map both sides of every exchange,
then work the map — pair a Champion consumer with a willing provider for the
first exchange, use the first win to move the Early Adopters, bring Observers
along with evidence, and lock the Champions in with a signed commitment letter.
And why a bus with no owner decays in its second year.
```

```text
You are not the first country to build this. The pattern is proven and the

GIF · Module 1 · 1.7 — What the world already proved

Estonia's X-Road shows the pattern lasts: a shared bus, distributed registries,
once-only in law — a proof, not a template to copy line for line. The EU's
```
