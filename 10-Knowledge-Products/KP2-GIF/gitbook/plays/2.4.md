## Example input

Country: Progressa. The three brackets:

- **Which article:** the once-only article.
- **Published model:** EU Single Digital Gateway Regulation (EU) 2018/1724, Article 14. The learner pastes the article's text. Its core: a competent authority obtains evidence directly from the authority that issued it, through a technical system; only the evidence requested is exchanged; the user may preview it; use of the system is not obligatory for users and happens only at their explicit request, unless Union or national law provides otherwise.

Pasted with it, as context:

- **B9:** the once-only article sits in the Draft Articles Package, after mandatory connection.
- **B5, first-wave rows 1 and 2:** PNEA issuing a certificate fetches identity from PNIA and enrolment from PLR, over Linkup. The identity leg returns six fields, as in the build pack: NIN, given name, family name, date of birth, sex, region.

## Example output

**Article 5 — Once-only**

1. Where a designated body needs, for a procedure listed in [confirm: annex of first-wave procedures], evidence of a person's identity or enrolment that another designated body holds in electronic form, it shall request that evidence directly from the issuing body through [confirm: the legal name of the national data-exchange layer and its operator].
2. Only the evidence necessary for the procedure shall be exchanged.
3. The person concerned may see the evidence before it is used in the procedure.
4. The exchange shall take place at the person's request, unless [confirm: the provision of Progressa law that allows the exchange without a request].

**[confirm] items**

- The annex listing first-wave procedures and designated bodies.
- The legal name of Linkup and of PDGA as its operator.
- The provision that allows exchange without a request.

**Flagged terms**

- "Evidence", "issuing body", "designated body" — need definitions in the scope article.
- "Necessary" — must match the Data Protection Act 2023's wording.
- "Person's request" — for a minor, who makes it: the parent or the learner?

## Reading the output

### 1. Paragraph 4 is the honest part

The model is opt-in: the user asks, the system fetches. The run kept that and put the switch to statutory exchange in a [confirm], rather than inventing a Progressa law that removes the request. That is rule (2) of the prompt working. It also exposes the real policy choice: request-based or statutory. The build pack runs the education exchanges as statutory, so for Progressa that [confirm] is the one that decides the article.

### 2. Paragraph 1 reads as a duty the model does not create

In the model, obtaining evidence directly is tied to the user's request. Paragraph 1 reads as a free-standing "shall", and only paragraph 4 pulls it back. Anyone quoting paragraph 1 alone would cite an obligation the model never imposed, which is what rule (3) forbids. Merge the two paragraphs, and check the draft line by line against the pasted text of Article 14.

### 3. The field list is not in the article

The identity leg returns six fields, but the article only says "necessary". Either the decree carries per-exchange field lists in an annex, or it delegates them to a regulation. Decide which before [2.6](./2-6.md) checks scope, or nothing will hold the field list.
