## Example input

Country: Progressa. The brief (the bracket):

The Minister of ICT sponsors the framework; PDGA would act as the interoperability authority and Linkup operator. First wave: PNIA (identity provider), PLR (enrolment provider, owned by MoEYS, planned with World Bank funding of USD 6.5m over three years), PNEA (consumer), PDGA (operator). First exchange: PNEA certificate issuance, fetching identity from PNIA and enrolment from PLR.

Pasted with it:

- **B1 (summary):** interoperability is required by tender clauses, the 2021 framework and the draft Roadmap, but delivered by no catalogued service. The 2022 tax link and the 2016 social register are procured one-offs.
- **B3 (summary):** first exchange as above; build the PNIA leg first, for candidates aged 16 and over; PLR leg when PLR holds records.
- **A0 §7 (extract):** Data Protection Act 2023 (legal basis for minors' data; parental consent for non-statutory use). PDGA's Roadmap is draft and its mandate coordinates, not binds. No e-transactions act. PNIA issues IDs only at 16.

## Example output

**Strategic Foundation Document — Progressa Government Interoperability Framework (draft for consultation)**

**1. Mandate.** This framework is issued under the authority of the Minister of ICT. It authorises PDGA to set interoperability standards, operate Linkup, admit members and publish the service catalogue. Members commit to exchange data over Linkup rather than by direct link.

**2. Scope.** First wave: PNIA, PLR, PNEA and PDGA. First exchange: PNEA certificate issuance with identity from PNIA (candidates 16 and over) and enrolment from PLR once it holds records. Out of scope for now: health, agriculture, social register, payments and cross-border exchange.

**3. Principles.**
- *Whole-of-government:* one shared bus. A new direct link needs a written exception.
- *Once-only:* a fact held by an authoritative source is fetched, not re-asked.
- *Reuse-first:* published standards and existing building blocks before anything bespoke.
- *Cross-border readiness:* identifiers and credentials follow international standards.

**4. Success measures (18 months).**
- PNEA and PLR admitted as Linkup members (PNIA already is).
- 50% of certificate applications from candidates aged 16 and over carry no paper identity proof.
- Service catalogue published, with every live service listed.
- The 2022 tax link migrated to Linkup.

*Signed: Minister of ICT.*

## Reading the output

### 1. Scope carries the caveats forward

The scope line keeps B3's limits: candidates 16 and over, PLR leg only once PLR holds records. The out-of-scope list is concrete. A reader can see what is not promised, which is half the value of the section.

### 2. The mandate claims authority PDGA does not have

"Authorises PDGA to set standards and admit members" reads well, but A0 §7 says PDGA coordinates and does not bind. A ministerial signature on this page does not change that; a decree does. Mark the mandate section as pending Module 2, or the first resisting agency will point it out.

### 3. The 50% target is invented

No baseline or pilot sizing supports it. Keep the measure and leave the figure blank until PNEA's candidate numbers are in hand. The tax-link migration measure is also outside the stated scope, which a careful reader will notice.

### 4. What is missing: the Data Protection Commission

The legal basis for minors' data is the hardest constraint in the input, and the draft names no role for the regulator. Add the Commission as a consulted party in the mandate section.
