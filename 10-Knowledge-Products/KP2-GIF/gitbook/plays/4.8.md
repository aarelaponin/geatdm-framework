## Example input

**The exchange, from the Use-Case Catalogue (B5), the first two rows:** a learner applies to PNEA for a senior-secondary certificate. PNEA pre-fills identity (given name, family name, date of birth, sex, region) from PNIA and enrolment (school, level, year, status) from PLR. The learner gives one field: the NIN. Many applicants are 16 or 17, so minors.

**The decree article, from the operative article draft (B11)** — the decree is a draft, not enacted. *Article 5 (Once-only):* for a procedure listed in [annex], a designated body requests evidence of identity or enrolment directly from the body that holds it, through [the data-exchange layer]; only the evidence necessary is exchanged; the person may see it first; the exchange takes place at the person's request, unless [a provision of Progressa law] allows it without one.

**Also in force:** Data Protection Act 2023, which requires a legal basis to share a minor's data.

## Example output

**Lawful basis: NOT AUTHORISED (yet).** Article 5 would cover this exchange once enacted, if the certificate procedure is in its annex. Today the decree is a draft, so no article authorises it. Two further gaps: Article 5 runs on the person's request, and for a 16-year-old it does not say who requests; and the PLR, being planned, has no act of its own in A0 §7.

**Draft Letter of Interest (PNIA to PNEA; a second one for PLR to PNEA)**

```text
Parties:   PNIA (provider), PNEA (consumer)
Purpose:   pre-filling a senior-secondary certificate application
Data:      nin, given_name, family_name, date_of_birth, sex, region
Channel:   Linkup; identity-api; PNEA:EXAMS only
Basis:     Interoperability Decree Art. 5 [on enactment; annex entry]
Request:   the applicant's, shown before use [confirm: parent for a minor?]
Retention: PNEA keeps the data for 5 years [confirm]
Review:    yearly, or when the purpose changes
```

**Data protection by design**

- The contract returns six fields; mother's name, birth registration number and address stay at PNIA.
- The access-control list grants PNEA:EXAMS only; PLR is denied.
- Every call is logged by both security servers.
- Retention of PNEA's copy and of the message log: [set by the decree or the Data Protection Commission].
- Sign-off: PNEA's and PNIA's data-protection officers.

## Reading the output

### 1. The NOT AUTHORISED flag is the right answer

The exchange runs in the build pack, and the run still refused to call it lawful. Article 5 fits the exchange, but a draft authorises nothing, and its request rule is unsettled for minors. The pack is honest about this too: its lawful-basis lines are marked as illustrative wording, not real statutes.

### 2. The five-year retention was invented

Nothing in the input sets a period. The run put "5 years" in the Letter of Interest and only then marked it [confirm], while its own checklist says retention is for the decree or the regulator. A number in a signed letter tends to stick. Leave the field blank until the law sets it.

### 3. The sign-off has nobody to do it yet

The build pack records the application gate, which asks for a data-protection officer wherever personal data flows, as a named absence for every member. The Data Protection Commission has six staff and no enforcement record. Name the officers before the envelope goes for signature, or it stays the architect's opinion.
