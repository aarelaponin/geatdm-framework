## Reading the output

### 1. The access list is exact

One entry, PNEA's EXAMS subsystem, and PNIA left out on purpose. That is what `configs/member-plr/plr.yaml` holds and what `onboarding/plr/05-registration.md` records as granted. The approval column also matches the pack's gate table: the Steering Committee admits, the Operating Authority does the rest.

### 2. The run restated what the pack keeps in one place

The model wrote PLR's member class, code and subsystem into the member file. The pack keeps them once, in `manifest.yaml`, and its member file deliberately leaves them out, because two copies can drift apart. The `[confirm]` marks fit a bus you are joining. On Linkup, which you operate, the manifest records the identifiers as verified against the live registry, and the join validator refuses a bad member key before anything reaches the bus.

### 3. Check the refusal, not only the grant

The safeguard asks you to prove that an unauthorised caller is refused. In the pack, the live refusal check calls PNIA's `identity-api`, not this service. For PLR, the check confirms that the granted list equals the config. Add a refused call against `enrolment-api` in the sandbox. Note also that the pack registered PLR by hand: its application and admission are recorded as not implemented in the demo.
