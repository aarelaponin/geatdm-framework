## Example input

The bracket *[relevant bodies and roles]* was replaced with this, from A0 §4 and B14:

- **Political:** Minister of ICT (sponsor of the Digital Transformation Roadmap). Ministers of Education, Finance and Interior own the ministries the first exchanges touch.
- **Digital-government head:** PDGA Director-General, coordinating mandate, reports to the Minister of ICT.
- **Sector CIOs:** MoEYS ICT Director; Ministry of Health ICT Director; Social Protection Agency IT manager. Heads of IT at PNIA, PNEA and the Progressa Learner Registry (PLR, being established).
- **Other:** Data Protection Commission (2023, 6 staff); Ministry of Finance budget department.
- **Existing governance:** no cross-government board. An ICT Steering Committee met twice in 2024 and not since. The 2021 framework's committee no longer meets.
- **Operating Authority (B14):** PDGA, with a Standards and Compliance Unit (regulator) and a Linkup Operations Unit (operator).

**Gaps [confirm]**

- No Strategic Council exists, and a council chaired by the Minister of ICT cannot bind the Minister of Education. It needs a cabinet decision or the decree.
- The Steering Committee should be the revived 2024 ICT Steering Committee, with new terms.

**If Progressa keeps it small.** Keep the Council and the Committee separate. In year one, run two Working Groups instead of four: Security with Platform Operations, and Semantics with APIs.

## Reading the output

### 1. Reviving a body beats inventing one

The model mapped the Steering Committee onto the ICT Steering Committee that already met twice. Members recognise a body they once sat on, and it needs new terms, not a new decree. That is earned from the input.

### 2. Nobody has agreed to any of this

Every minister in the Council row was placed there by the model. The safeguard applies in full: until each named body accepts its seat and scope, this table is a diagram. The Minister of Education above all, whose ministry gains the most and gives up the most.

### 3. The gap it flagged is the one that matters, but its fix is open

The output is right that a council chaired by a peer minister cannot bind another ministry. It offers "a cabinet decision or the decree" without choosing. That choice decides whether the whole structure has teeth. Take it to the decree outline before you circulate the table.

### 4. The Data Protection Commission as observer needs a check

The Commission is independent; a seat that looks like a vote would compromise it. "Observer" is the right instinct, but confirm with the Commission what it is willing to attend, and whether it prefers to be consulted in writing.
