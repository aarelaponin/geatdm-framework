## Example input

Country: Progressa. Services and what each asks for at intake, built from A0 §2 and §9:

1. Primary school enrolment (school, keyed into district EMIS) — child's birth certificate, parent's National ID, proof of address.
2. Transfer to secondary school — previous school's leaving record, birth certificate.
3. Examination registration (PNEA) — National ID or birth certificate, school letter proving enrolment.
4. Certificate issuance (PNEA) — National ID, proof of enrolment.
5. Child social grant (Social Protection Agency) — parent's National ID, child's birth certificate, proof of enrolment, proof of income.
6. Scholarship application (MoEYS, planned platform) — National ID, proof of enrolment, examination results, proof of household income.
7. University admission — examination certificate, National ID.

Context: PNIA's National ID covers 78% of adults and is issued only from age 16; e-KYC API documented; PNIA is on Linkup. PLR is planned, not started. Birth registration is 71%, paper-first. The social register is held by a 2016 vendor system.

**First exchange to build:** certificate issuance at PNEA, with identity fetched from PNIA and enrolment from PLR. It removes the two most re-asked facts at one counter, the identity source is already on Linkup, and PNEA is a single consumer with a clear service. Build the PNIA leg first; add the PLR leg as soon as PLR holds records.

## Reading the output

### 1. The top two rows are earned

Identity and enrolment are re-asked by four or more services each, and the input says so line by line. Picking a single consumer, PNEA, rather than "all services" keeps the first build small. It is the same exchange the course's build pack runs.

### 2. The interaction counts are invented arithmetic

Nothing in the input gives a figure per service. The model derived them from one assumed cohort, and that holds for ranking, not for a budget. A real estimate needs PNEA's candidate numbers and the grant programme's caseload.

### 3. The safeguard bites twice

The prompt's safeguard says an incomplete registry is not yet a trusted source. PLR holds nothing today. PNIA holds no one under 16, so a primary-leaving candidate cannot be fetched at all. The recommendation survives only if it is scoped to candidates aged 16 and over, and the PLR leg waits.

### 4. What to check next: birth facts

Birth facts sit third and are marked High, which is right for a paper-first register at 71%. They are also the only identity anchor for under-16s. Ask the Civil Registration Department whether a digital birth index is planned, since it decides when once-only can reach a primary school.
