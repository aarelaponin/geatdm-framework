## Example input

The bracket *[candidate body or bodies and its current mandate]* was replaced with this, from A0 §6 and B4:

**Candidate body: Progressa Digital Government Authority (PDGA).** A unit under the Ministry of ICT. Its mandate coordinates digital government but does not bind other bodies. It runs Linkup (X-Road 7.x, pilot since 2025, four members: PNIA, the business register, the tax authority, PDGA itself) and has a documented member-onboarding procedure but no service catalogue. Architecture unit: one senior architect on a donor-funded contract and two junior analysts. PDGA published the 2021 e-Government Interoperability Framework, whose governance committee no longer meets.

**From the Strategic Foundation Document (B4):** issued under the authority of the Minister of ICT; it authorises PDGA to set standards, operate Linkup, admit members and publish the service catalogue. First wave: PNIA, the Progressa Learner Registry (PLR), the Progressa National Examination Authority (PNEA) and PDGA. First exchange: PNEA issues certificates with identity from PNIA and enrolment from PLR, without asking the family again.

Other facts: the Ministry of Finance runs an annual budget with no multi-year ICT envelopes.

## Reading the output

### 1. The split fits the evidence

Two units inside PDGA, not two agencies, is the right call for a country whose architecture unit is three people. The model also kept the hats apart in practice, not only on paper: the head of PDGA does not vote on PDGA's own exceptions. That line is worth keeping in the final mandate.

### 2. The staff numbers are the course's range, not Progressa's

"10 now, 30–40 by 2030" sits inside the range the course gives for any country. Nothing in the input supports it, and today's architecture unit is three people, one of them on a donor contract. Treat the numbers as a question for the Ministry of Finance, and price them before anyone quotes them.

### 3. PDGA is also a member of its own bus

The input says PDGA is one of Linkup's four members, and the model did not notice. A regulator that sanctions members while its own operator unit is a member needs a rule for who judges PDGA's own breaches. Add it as a sixth gap: the Steering Committee, not the regulator unit, decides any case against PDGA.

### 4. The Data Protection Commission is missing

The first exchange carries minors' data. Under the Data Protection Act 2023, the Commission, not PDGA, rules on lawful basis. The mandate should say where the regulator's power stops and the Commission's begins, or two regulators will claim the same breach.
