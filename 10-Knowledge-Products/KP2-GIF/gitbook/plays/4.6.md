## Example input

**Source:** Giga's school data for Progressa's ~8,200 schools, in Giga's published school-master schema: a Giga school id, the government school id, school name, latitude and longitude, education level, and a connectivity flag [confirm field names against the schema]. Giga's qos data adds measured download speeds per school.

**What we know locally:** MoEYS keeps its school census in district EMIS files. Each district numbers its own schools. Many rural schools have uneven connectivity.

**From the semantic map (B23):** PLR's enrolment record holds `school` as a free-text name. We want a school code it can point to instead.

## Example output

| Stage | What happens | Kept or published |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bronze | Giga school-master and qos extracts, and the MoEYS census files, loaded unchanged with load date and source | Kept for audit; never served |
| Silver | Coordinates checked inside Progressa; education level mapped to one code list; names normalised; duplicates flagged where two rows share the de-duplication key `district code + government school id` [confirm: verify uniqueness] | Internal; flagged rows go back to districts |

| Onto the bus | OpenAPI contract for `GET /schools/{code}`; X-Road service description; security servers at both ends; school data needs no personal-data basis | Registered by the school-data provider |

**Confirm before go-live**

- That `district code + government school id` is unique across all districts.
- Gold row count against the census count, district by district.
- Which body provides the gold service on the bus.
- That no field carries personal data (head teacher names, phone numbers).

## Reading the output

### 1. The de-duplication key is earned

The input says each district numbers its own schools. The run did not trust the government school id on its own; it prefixed the district code and still marked it [confirm]. That is the safeguard applied properly. Two schools with the same local number in different districts are the most likely silent merge here.

### 2. "No personal-data basis" is said too quickly

School records usually look impersonal. But census files often carry a head teacher's name and phone number, and the run declared the basis unnecessary before seeing a single row. Its own checklist then asks the question. The bus row should say "to be checked", not "not needed".

### 3. Nobody is named to provide the gold service

The school list is MoEYS data, and MoEYS is not a Linkup member (A0 §3). The build pack does not carry it either; it retired MoEYS. Until a provider is chosen and onboarded, the gold dataset has nowhere to go. Raise it as an onboarding decision, not a technical one.

## What next

This plan ends its chain, but its gold school code is what lets PLR's `school` field in the semantic map ([4.4](./4-4.md)) become a code, not a name. The same pattern carries to the next sector in [5.10](../module-5/5-10.md). In your framework workbook this is artefact **B25**.
