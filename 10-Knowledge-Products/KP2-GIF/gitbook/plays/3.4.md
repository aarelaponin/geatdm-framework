## Example input

The bracket *[country X]* was replaced with Progressa, and this context was added from B16 and B6:

**Members (B6).** PNIA provides person identity; the Progressa Learner Registry (PLR) provides enrolment; PNEA consumes both to confirm a candidate without asking the family again.

**From the RACI (B16).** The PDGA Linkup Operations Unit (operator) runs onboarding. The PDGA Standards and Compliance Unit (regulator) is Accountable for suspension, but holds no power to suspend until the interoperability decree exists. The Steering Committee admits members.

**Known service-level expectation.** 99.5% monthly availability; support Monday to Friday, 08:00–18:00; a priority-one incident acknowledged within one hour.

**Law.** The Data Protection Act 2023 requires a legal basis to share a minor's data, and parental consent for any non-statutory use.

## Example output

**Linkup Member Agreement (draft)** — between PDGA, as Operating Authority, and the member agency.

**1. What every member commits to.** Connect and stay connected. Meet the agreed service levels. Keep its data accurate and current. Answer the queries the framework obliges it to answer, so a citizen gives data once. Protect what it receives. Follow Linkup's published standards. Name a Technical Focal Point and, wherever personal data flows, a Data Protection Officer. Pay any contribution the Council sets; none is set for the pilot phase **[confirm with the Council]**.

**2. If you provide data (PNIA, PLR).** Keep your service available at 99.5% a month. Correct a reported error within five working days. Give five working days' notice of a planned change. Carry the query load the catalogue entry states.

**3. If you consume data (PNEA).** Use the data only for the purpose in the catalogue entry: confirming a candidate's identity and enrolment. Do not store more than you need. Do not pass it on to any other body.

**4. What the framework gives a provider in return.** The operator monitors your service and handles first-line support for consumers. Service levels are agreed with you, not for you. The Steering Committee refuses an exchange that would overload you.

**5. Breach.** A breach is missing a service level three months running, an unanswered obligatory query, or any use beyond the stated purpose. The operator writes to the member and agrees a fix within 30 days. If the breach persists, the regulator may suspend access **[confirm: depends on the decree]**.

Signed: Head of the member agency; Director-General, PDGA.

## Reading the output

### 1. The provider-consumer balance is earned

Section 4 is what makes PNIA likely to sign: it names what PNIA gets for carrying the load. The input made PNIA the only champion, and the agreement treats it as the agency most worth keeping. Keep section 4 when you edit; it is the first thing a provider's CIO reads.

### 2. The 99.5% was set for the providers, not with them

The figure came from the input, and it matches the signed service levels in the build pack. But the safeguard says to agree the number with each provider. PNIA's e-KYC runs today and can say what it meets; PLR is still being established and cannot yet promise anything. Ask before you print it.

### 3. Minors' data needs more than "purpose limitation"

The consumer section names the purpose but not the legal basis. Every record in the first exchange is a learner's, and most learners are minors. Add a line: the consumer states the statutory basis for each query, and the Data Protection Officers of both members confirm it before the first call.

### 4. There is no way out

The agreement says how to join and how to be suspended, not how to leave. Add an exit clause: notice period, removal from the catalogue, and how long message logs are kept after a member leaves.
