## Example input

Country: Progressa. The bracket "paste it or summarise" was filled with the Strategic Foundation Document (B4), summarised, and the headline of the legal-readiness assessment (B8):

- **Mandate.** Issued by the Minister of ICT. Authorises PDGA to set standards, operate Linkup and admit members (A0 §7: today PDGA's mandate coordinates and does not bind).
- **Scope, first wave.** PNIA, PLR, PNEA and PDGA. PNEA fetches a learner's identity from PNIA and enrolment from PLR to issue a certificate, so the learner brings no paper. Identity for candidates aged 16 and over; the PLR leg once the PLR holds records. Out of scope for now: health, agriculture, social register, payments, cross-border exchange.
- **Principles.** Whole-of-government, once-only, reuse-first, cross-border readiness.

- **B8 headline.** PNIA → PNEA permitted on a narrow reading; PLR → PNEA unclear because the PLR has no founding instrument; minors' data needs a statutory basis under the Data Protection Act 2023.

## Reading the output

### 1. The articles list follows the gaps

The run added a PLR provision that the prompt's list does not contain. It came straight from B8's "unclear" row. That is the outline doing its job: the gaps in current law decide which articles the decree must carry.

### 2. PDGA's powers are assumed, not given

The outline gives PDGA powers to admit, suspend and enforce, because B4 claims them. A0 §7 says PDGA coordinates and does not bind, and a ministerial foundation document cannot change that. Even a decree may not be able to grant binding powers over other ministries; that may need primary law. Mark the article as a question for the Ministry of Justice, not a given.

### 3. Check the route before drafting

Two items — founding the PLR and binding powers for PDGA — may each push the package from a decree to an act of parliament. Ask the Ministry of Justice about the route now, before 2.3 and 2.4 draft text for the wrong instrument.
