## Example input

**[country X]:** Progressa. **[new sector]:** health.

**From the implementation plan (B28), in short:** four phases over 24 months. The bus is built once in Foundation. Next sectors come in Optimisation (months 19–24).

**From the standards portfolio (B22), in short:** X-Road 7.7.0; OpenAPI 3.0.3; TLS; CEDS for person data; OneRoster for enrolment; W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 (planned).

**From the semantic map (B23), in short:** person (NIN, given name, family name, date of birth, sex, region), anchored on CEDS; enrolment (NIN, school, level, year, status), anchored on OneRoster. Records are linked by NIN.

**About health, from the country pack:** a Digital Health Records pilot runs in three provinces (USD 3.2m, Global Fund). It needs patient identity, a facility registry, consent and exchange with civil registration. The Ministry of Health's patient index uses its own identifier. PNIA issues IDs only at 16; birth registration is at 71%.

## Example output

**Reused unchanged**

- The four-layer model: legal, organisational, semantic and technical questions are the same in every sector.
- The decree pattern and the governance: tiers, RACI, Operating Authority. None of it names a sector.
- The bus: Linkup, its trust anchor and PNIA's `identity-api`, already published.
- The standards portfolio's technical rows (X-Road, OpenAPI, TLS). CEDS and OneRoster are education-specific and do not carry.

**New for health**

- Vocabularies: HL7 FHIR R4 for patients, encounters and facilities; ICD-11 for diagnoses; LOINC for laboratory results. *[confirm versions]*
- Exchanges: patient identity from PNIA (a new grant and lawful basis, not a new service); a facility registry; birth notifications to civil registration; consent records.

**Business case for the minister:** "Health reuses the bus, the law and the governance we have already paid for in education. It pays only for its own vocabularies, three new services and one new permission on an existing one. So health costs a fraction of what the first sector cost."

**Wave:** health usually belongs in the first wave: foundational, high-volume reference data that other sectors read. In Progressa it comes second only because education went first. It should be the first sector added after education.

## Reading the output

### 1. Reusing the identity service is earned

PNIA's `identity-api` is already on the bus, with a contract and an access list. Health needs a new grant and its own lawful basis, not a new service. That is the clearest proof in the map that the second sector is cheaper.

### 2. One "unchanged" claim is too broad, and the cost has no figure

The run caught the education vocabularies in the portfolio, which is the leak the safeguard warns about. But it still lists the decree pattern as unchanged. Health data is more sensitive, much of it is about children, and the Data Protection Act asks for a legal basis to share a minor's data. Health will likely need its own article. "A fraction of the cost" also has no figure behind it. Add the cost of the three new health services from your implementation plan before it reaches a minister.

### 3. The linking key does not carry

Education links records by NIN. In health, newborns and children under 16 have no National ID, and the patient index uses its own identifier. Matching patients is a new semantic problem, not a reused one. Settle it with the Ministry of Health's data owners and the Civil Registration Department before anyone promises the platform carries over.
