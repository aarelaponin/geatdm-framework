# E2.1 — Progressa's catalogue of higher-education services, and the blocks they rest on

**What it shows.** Rows of Progressa's catalogue of higher-education services owed by PHEQA and MoEYS, each naming the body that owes it, its state today and the blocks it rests on, so that the column of the register of institutions shows six services resting on one register.

**Form of this example.** The columns of the catalogue are the team's blank instrument and name no country. The rows are filled for Progressa.

## What the catalogue is for

Before anyone specifies one service, the sector lists every service its bodies owe, once, with what each rests on. The catalogue is written for the whole sector, not for one project. It is the only document in which a manager can see that one building block, paid for once, carries many services.

## The rows

"Rests on" means the service cannot run without the block. ● it rests on the block; ○ it will rest on the block when the block is in place; — it does not need it.

## What the register's column shows

Read the column of the register of institutions from top to bottom. The first service writes the register. Every one of the other six rests on it: five now, and the credential later. If PHEQA builds the register once, as a block that others read across Linkup, six services stand on it. If each service keeps its own list of institutions, Progressa pays for seven lists and spends years reconciling them, as the institution in subtopic 1.1 found.

Read the column of PNIA's sign-in the same way: six services rest on it now and the seventh will, and none of them needs to build its own way of knowing who a person is.

## Planning enables re-use

Inside one project, building your own list of institutions is always faster than agreeing to read somebody else's. That is not a failure of discipline; it is what a project is paid to do. The sum that makes re-use worth it, one register paid for and six services carried, exists only at the level of the whole sector. Only someone who plans for the sector, and keeps this catalogue, can see it. The UNDP describes digital public infrastructure as foundational systems that many services connect to; PAERA adds that the infrastructure shapes what can be built on top of it. The catalogue is where a sector decides which foundation it will build on.

**What to look for in your own sector.** Ask for the catalogue before you approve the first project. Look down each column of a block. A block with one service in its column is a project's tool. A block with many is the sector's infrastructure, and it should be planned and funded as such.
