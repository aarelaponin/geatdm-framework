**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name, date and figure in it is invented.

# E3.6 — The review of the provisional-licence screens at PHEQA

**What it shows.** The review of the provisional-licence screens at PHEQA, with the owner of the screens, the supplier's builder and the head of the registration desk, one hour, the walk-through on the screen, and the record of the meeting as a list of open lines, each with an owner and a date.

**Form of this example.** The three seats, the questions put and the form of the record are the method's and name no country. The meeting and its record are filled for Progressa.

## The three seats

| Seat | Who sits in it at PHEQA | Why that seat |
|---|---|---|
| The owner | The supplier's analyst who wrote the goal and its screens | Answers for what the documents say |
| The builder | The supplier's builder who will generate the application | Says whether he can build from it without asking anything |
| The person who will live with the result | The head of the registration desk of PHEQA | Says whether the screens are her work as she does it, and what an applicant will actually do |

The Registrar of PHEQA convenes the meeting and accepts its record. She does not take one of the three seats.

## The meeting

Thursday 19 November 2026, 10:00 to 11:00, at PHEQA. One screen on the wall, showing the walk-through of version 1.2 of the screens. The Registrar opens with the four questions every review of screens puts:

1. Does every screen serve a step or a failure of the story, and is every step and failure served?
2. Does every value on every screen say where it comes from?
3. Could the builder build each screen without asking anybody anything?
4. Would the person who will use it recognise it as her work?

The head of the registration desk holds the mouse and clicks through, page by page. At each page the owner says what the screen is for, the builder says whether he can build it, and the head of the desk says what is wrong with it. Nobody leaves the room with a decision in their head that is not in the record.

## The record: the open lines

| Line | What is open | Owner | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Show the fee due on S2, beside the standards, as well as on S4 (from the walk-through) | The supplier's analyst | 26 November 2026 |
| 2 | Is a name held by an institution whose licence was cancelled still held, for the check on S3? | The Registrar of PHEQA | 3 December 2026 |
| 3 | S3 asks for the governing board as one block of text; the desk asks for each member's name and role on its own line, because the inspector checks them one by one | The supplier's analyst, after the head of the registration desk confirms the list of roles | 26 November 2026 |
| 4 | The failure "PNIA does not answer at sign-in" tells the applicant nothing about when to try again. What may the screen say? | The head of the ICT unit of PHEQA, after asking PNIA for its published service hours | 3 December 2026 |
| 5 | The builder cannot tell from S5 whether the confirmation can be printed. Is a printed confirmation asked for anywhere? | The Registrar of PHEQA, against the register of requirements | 26 November 2026 |

Every line has a name and a date. Had the room been unable to name an owner for a line, it would have been recorded as "owner to be named by the Registrar", and she would have named one before accepting the screens.

## What happens next

The owner changes the screens for lines 1 and 3, and the walk-through is produced again from the changed screens. Lines 2, 4 and 5 are answered by their owners, and each answer is written into the document that owns it: the shared groundwork for line 2, the architecture for line 4, the register of requirements for line 5. The Registrar accepts the screens when every line is closed, or when each line still open is one she decides may stay open into the next document, with its owner.

## Working together

PAERA gives a section to digital co-creation (section 4.5); what it says there is checked against the published text in the third production round before any script cites it. The review is where the officials who will use a service and the people who build it shape one goal together, in one hour, with a record anyone can check afterwards.

**What to look for in the record of a review.** Count the open lines and the owners. A line with no owner is a decision that will be taken by whoever meets it first, usually the builder, under deadline. A record with no open lines at all usually means the review was not held, or nobody spoke.
