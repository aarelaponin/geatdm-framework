**Public anchor:** None; the content is the SDD method's own: an assistant writes and a person approves, and the review with three people

**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name, date and figure in it is invented.

# E1.5 — Who writes, checks and accepts each document of PHEQA's registration project

**What it shows.** The table of who writes, checks and accepts each of the twelve documents for PHEQA's registration project: the supplier's analyst and an AI assistant write; a second analyst checks; the Registrar of PHEQA or the head of its ICT unit accepts; the head of the registration desk sits at every review.

**Form of this example.** The four columns are the team's blank instrument and name no country. The names of roles are filled for Progressa.

## The rule behind the table

An AI assistant may draft any document a person would otherwise draft. It proposes; it never decides. A named person accepts, amends or sets aside each thing it proposes, and only what that person accepted stands. No document has the assistant as its acceptor. And at every review of something an official will use, the person who will live with the result sits beside the owner and the builder.

## The table

| # | Document | Writes | Checks | Accepts | Sits at the review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PHEQA's own documents | PHEQA (the regulations, the mandate, the registration desk's requests) | — | The Registrar of PHEQA hands them over | — |
| 2 | The register of requirements | An AI assistant extracts the entries; the supplier's analyst rules on each | A second analyst of the supplier | The Registrar of PHEQA | The head of the registration desk |
| 3 | The records, with their glossary and rules | The supplier's analyst | A second analyst | The Registrar of PHEQA, after the read-back | The head of the registration desk, who hears the read-back |
| 4 | The list of goals | The supplier's named architect | A second analyst | The Registrar of PHEQA | The head of the registration desk |
| 5 | The architecture | The supplier's solution architect | A second architect of the supplier | The head of the ICT unit of PHEQA, after the head of the ICT unit of MoEYS confirms the crossing to MoEYS | — |
| 6 | The rest of the shared groundwork | The supplier's analyst, with an AI assistant | A second analyst | The owner of each setting: the finance officer for the fee, the Registrar for the code lists | — |
| 7 | One goal written out in full | An AI assistant drafts; the supplier's analyst rules on the draft | A second analyst | The Registrar of PHEQA, at the review of the screens | The head of the registration desk |
| 8 | The screens of that goal | The analyst who wrote the goal | A second analyst | The review of three people: the screens' owner, who wrote them, the builder, the head of the registration desk; the writer never accepts alone | The head of the registration desk |
| 9 | The walk-through | Produced by a program | Its version stamp is compared with the screens | Not accepted on its own: it is clicked at the review of the screens | The head of the registration desk |
| 10 | The interaction design | One analyst of the supplier | A second analyst | The Registrar of PHEQA | The head of the registration desk |
| 11 | The application model | An AI assistant writes it and must refuse to guess | The supplier's lead reads what it assumed and could not express; a program checks it | The head of the ICT unit of PHEQA | — |
| 12 | The working application | Generated from the accepted model | The read-back against the model; the acceptance journeys | The Registrar of PHEQA, once a registration officer has finished a real task on it | The head of the registration desk |

## Three things the manager checks in the table

1. **No row ends with the assistant.** In rows 2, 6, 7 and 11 an AI assistant writes, and in each of them a named person rules on what it wrote and a different named person accepts it.
2. **The writer never accepts alone.** In no row does the writer accept alone: where a writer sits at a review, as the owner of the screens does in row 8, two other people must agree.
3. **The person who will live with it is in the room.** The head of the registration desk sits at every review of something an officer will use: the register, the records, the goals, the screens, the interaction design and the working application.

**What to look for in your own project.** Ask your supplier for this table before the first document is written. If a cell in the column Accepts names the supplier, or names nobody, the decision in that row has not been given to anyone in your organisation.
