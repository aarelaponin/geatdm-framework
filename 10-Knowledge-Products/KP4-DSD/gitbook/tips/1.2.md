## Example input

**[the service]:** the registration and licensing of private institutions.

**[the ministry or agency]:** PHEQA, the Progressa Higher Education Quality Authority.

**[paste the proposal, or its sections on design approval, requirements and testing]:** the proposal's contents page, which lists sections 1 to 9 and "Annex C. User acceptance plan", and three sections in full:

> "Section 4. Design sign-off. The supplier will deliver a data dictionary of 140 fields covering the application, the institution and the licence. PHEQA's officials will sign each page of the dictionary to confirm the design."
>
> "Section 5. Traceability. The supplier's analysts will prepare a requirements list from the approved design, and every requirement will be traced to a screen."
>
> "Section 7. Testing. Testing is complete when all test scripts pass on the supplier's test server."

## Example output

| Rule | What the proposal says | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Review a story, not a list | "PHEQA's officials will sign each page of the dictionary to confirm the design." (section 4) | Falls short | Officials approve 140 fields, not what a person does and sees step by step. |
| 2. Measure against a list the design did not write | "The supplier's analysts will prepare a requirements list from the approved design" (section 5) | Falls short | The list is written from the design, so the design will always match it. |
| 3. End one check on the running system | "Testing is complete when all test scripts pass on the supplier's test server." (section 7) | Falls short | The supplier's server is not the system the service will run on, and no real user finishes a task. |

**Shortfalls to raise with the supplier:**

1. Replace the signed data dictionary with a story for each goal, with its screens and a walk-through officials can click.
2. Write a register of what PHEQA asked, from its regulations and requests, before any design, and measure the design against it.
3. The proposal offers no acceptance by PHEQA's own users: require that a registration officer finishes a real task on PHEQA's own installation.

## Reading the output

### 1. Each verdict rests on the proposal's own words

All three rows quote the section and the sentence they judge. A supplier cannot answer "we never said that". Rules 1 and 2 fall short on the face of the text, and the reasons given are the page's own.

### 2. Shortfall 3 says more than the text allows

The contents page lists "Annex C. User acceptance plan", and the annex was not pasted. Section 7 shows where testing ends, not whether PHEQA's users accept anything afterwards. "The proposal offers no acceptance by PHEQA's own users" is a claim about a document the assistant never saw.

### 3. The safeguard sends it to the supplier first

This is why every shortfall is confirmed before it enters a decision. Ask the supplier what Annex C says. If it ends with a registration officer on PHEQA's installation, rule 3 is met and the shortfall is dropped. If it ends on the test server, the clause stands, now with the annex quoted.
