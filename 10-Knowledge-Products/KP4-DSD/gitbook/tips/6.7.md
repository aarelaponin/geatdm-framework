## Example input

**[paste the statement]:** the seven limits and what the method still lacks, as the written page gives them, in short:

1. It does not claim that the standards it rests on work well together; no analyst has yet been handed all of them and asked to produce a description from them.
2. It does not claim that two of its standards are more than drafts, or that four standards in force have been tested by use.
3. It does not claim that any existing body of work conforms to anything.
4. It does not claim that the order of its documents is the only defensible one.
5. It does not claim that everything is checked; several of the most important handovers are not checked by a machine.
6. It does not claim that the work written before its present version has been brought into its present shape.
7. It does not claim to be cheap; twelve documents mean nine places a correction can land.

Still lacking: a standard of its own for accessibility, and one for delivery in more than one language.

**[paste the description]:** MoEYS's next service: a person aggrieved by a decision of PHEQA asks the minister to review it, online. The service starts from new documents, must serve people with disabilities, and must be offered in more than one language.

## Example output

**Risks.** The method states its own limits. It does not claim that its standards work well together, since no analyst has yet used them all to produce a description; our team will be among the first, so we keep the review of each document. Two of its standards are drafts and four are not tested by use; we read those documents most closely. Several handovers are not checked by a machine; we name a reviewer for each. It does not claim to be cheap: twelve documents mean twelve places a correction can land; we budget for each as a deliverable. Limits 3, 4 and 6 do not bear on a service that starts from new documents. The method also depends on staff trained in it, which the ministry does not yet have; we plan training before the work starts. Accessibility and more than one language have no standard of their own; we write both into the register of what was asked.

| 2 | "two of its standards are more than drafts" |
| 5 | "not checked by a machine" |

## Reading the output

### 1. The limits are tied to the service

Each limit that bears on the review service gets one concrete answer: a kept review, a closer reading, a named reviewer, a budget line. Accessibility and languages go into the register of what was asked, which is where the page says they belong.

### 2. Limit 7 is misquoted

The method says twelve documents mean "nine places a correction can land". The output says "twelve places", in its paragraph and again in quotation marks in the list. Comparing each quotation with the statement, as the safeguard asks, catches it before the note goes to the minister.

### 3. A limit of the assistant's own

"Depends on staff trained in it" is not in the method's statement, yet the list gives it as the method's own words. The point may be fair, but it belongs elsewhere in the note, signed as the ministry's view. Limits 3, 4 and 6 are also not quoted: they are named only by number, so the minister cannot read them.
