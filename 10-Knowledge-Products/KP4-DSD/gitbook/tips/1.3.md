## Example input

**[the service]:** an institution's appeal against a decision of PHEQA.

**[the ministry or agency]:** PHEQA, the Progressa Higher Education Quality Authority.

**[paste the draft]:** five lines of the supplier's draft of the appeal goal.

1. "An institution may appeal within 30 days of the date of PHEQA's decision."
2. "The appeal is heard by PHEQA's appeals panel of three members."
3. "PHEQA decides the appeal within 60 days of receiving it."
4. "An appeal carries the application fee of 1,200, as set by PHEQA's schedule of fees (article 7)."
5. "A person aggrieved by a decision of PHEQA may instead ask the minister to review it (article 22)."

**[paste them]:** the Higher Education Regulations of 2025, articles 7 and 22, and the registration desk's written request for the appeal.

- Article 7: "PHEQA shall issue a schedule of fees for the services it provides."
- Article 22: "A person aggrieved by a decision of PHEQA may ask the minister to review it."
- The desk's request: "Institutions should be able to lodge an appeal through the self-service and see where it stands."

## Example output

| Line of the draft | What looks guessed | Rewritten as a named question | Proposed owner | Date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. "… within 30 days of the date of PHEQA's decision." | The period of 30 days | The appeal period: to be confirmed by the Registrar of PHEQA by [date] | The Registrar of PHEQA | |
| 2. "… PHEQA's appeals panel of three members." | The panel and its size | Who hears an appeal, and how many: to be confirmed by the Registrar of PHEQA by [date] | The Registrar of PHEQA | |
| 3. "… within 60 days of receiving it." | The period of 60 days | The time PHEQA has to decide an appeal: to be confirmed by the Registrar of PHEQA by [date] | The Registrar of PHEQA | |

**Lines treated as sourced:**

- Line 4: article 7, the schedule of fees.
- Line 5: article 22, word for word.

**Open questions the document will carry:** 3.

## Reading the output

### 1. The three periods and the panel are caught

Lines 1 to 3 hold exactly the kind of figure the page warns about: they look certain and nobody gave them. Each comes back as a question in the form the prompt asks for, and the dates are left blank for the manager. The assistant answered none of them.

### 2. Line 4 points to a source that does not say it

Article 7 says PHEQA issues a schedule of fees. It does not say what the fee is, and it does not say an appeal carries one. The schedule itself was not pasted. The assistant saw an article number beside the line and treated the whole line as sourced. Both the amount and the rule that an appeal pays the application fee are unproven.

### 3. The safeguard at work

The safeguard says to check every line treated as sourced against the source it names. Read against article 7, line 4 fails and becomes a fourth question: whether an appeal carries a fee, and how much, owned by PHEQA's finance officer, who owns the fee. The count rises from 3 to 4. Line 5 passes the same check: article 22 says it word for word.

### 4. The owners are still proposals

Every question went to the Registrar. That is a reasonable start, but the time PHEQA takes to decide may rest with whoever sets PHEQA's working rules. The manager assigns each question to a person and sets its date.
