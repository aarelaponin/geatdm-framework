## Example input

**[the service]:** the registration and licensing of private institutions.

**[the ministry or agency]:** PHEQA, the Progressa Higher Education Quality Authority.

**[paste the list]:** the team's roles, with the body each belongs to.

- Our own staff: the Registrar of PHEQA; the head of the registration desk; the finance officer; the head of the ICT unit of PHEQA.
- The supplier: the supplier's analyst; a second analyst; the solution architect; the builder; the supplier's lead.
- Another body: the head of the ICT unit of MoEYS.

## Reading the output

### 1. Most rows follow the three rules

The writer never accepts alone: in row 8 the analyst who wrote the screens accepts only with the builder and the head of the registration desk. The head of the desk sits at every review of something an officer will use. And the gaps are marked, not filled: only one architect is in the list, so nobody can check the architecture.

### 2. Row 11 breaks the rule the summary says it keeps

The application model is written by the assistant and "accepted" by the assistant once a program says the file is valid. A program's check is a check, not an acceptance, and nobody in PHEQA says yes. Yet the line under the table states that no document has the assistant as its acceptor.

### 3. The safeguard catches it

The safeguard says to check, before the table is used, that no document is left with the AI assistant as its acceptor. Read row by row, not from the summary, row 11 fails. It goes to the head of the ICT unit of PHEQA, who approves the model after reading what it assumed and could not express.

### 4. Row 12 names a role the list does not hold

"A registration officer" is not in the pasted list, yet it appears in row 12. It is the right person, but the prompt said to use only the listed roles. Add the role to the list, and let the head of the unit confirm it with the rest.
