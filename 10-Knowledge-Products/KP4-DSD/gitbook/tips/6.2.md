## Example input

**[paste the request]:** From the head of PHEQA's registration desk, 9 February 2027: "On the request from Harbourview University College to become Coastland University College, the officer's screen already offers to write the new name into the register. The minister has not approved it yet. Please take that button off the screen until the approval comes back from MoEYS."

**[paste the list]:**

- PHEQA's records, with their glossary and business rules. Holds the rule: "When an institution applies to change its name, the registration officer writes the new name into the register of institutions." Made from: the register of what was asked.
- PHEQA's shared groundwork. Holds the states of a request to change a name and the moves between them. Made from: the records.
- PHEQA's goal: apply to change an institution's name. Made from: the records, the shared groundwork.
- PHEQA's goal: write an approved new name into the register. Made from: the records, the shared groundwork.
- The screens and walk-throughs of those two goals. Made from: the goals.
- PHEQA's interaction design. Settles how a request moves between states. Made from: the records, the screens.
- PHEQA's application model. Made from: all of the above.
- PHEQA's working application. Generated from: the application model.

## Example output

**The owning document:** PHEQA's records, with their business rules.

**Reason.** The screen only offers what the rules allow. The rule says today: "When an institution applies to change its name, the registration officer writes the new name into the register of institutions." It names no approval, so the screen is right to offer the button.

**Corrected rule (proposed).** The new name is written into the register only on the minister's approval, received from MoEYS. Until then the register keeps the present name.

**Documents to produce again:**

1. PHEQA's shared groundwork: the move that writes the name gains a condition.
2. The goal: apply to change an institution's name.
3. The goal: write an approved new name into the register.
4. The screens and walk-throughs of both goals.
5. PHEQA's application model.
6. PHEQA's working application, generated again.

**Does it touch behaviour?** Yes. The story of the "write an approved new name" goal changes first, then its screens, then the walk-through, then what was built.

**Interim measure.** Until the documents are produced again, the button can be hidden in the running application, so that no officer writes a name too early.

**Questions:** none.

## Reading the output

### 1. The right document, with the line quoted

The request asks for a screen change; the output goes past the screen to the business rule, and quotes the line that states the fact today. The corrected wording matches what article 26(1) of the regulations requires. That is the whole point of this subtopic, and the run gets it.

### 2. The interim measure is placed wrongly

Hiding the button "in the running application" is a hand edit to a generated system. The safeguard says such a placement is wrong whatever reason it gives: the running system would then disagree with its documents, and the next generation would bring the button back. The rule is confirmed by the Registrar of PHEQA, who owns it, and nothing is changed until then.

### 3. The interaction design is missing from the list

The input says the interaction design settles how a request moves between states, and it was made from the records. It is stale too, and the list leaves it out. The program that lists stale documents would have caught it; a list written by hand, or by an assistant, is checked against that program's list.
