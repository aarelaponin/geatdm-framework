## Example input

**[country X]:** Progressa.

**[paste the screen]:** "Record an inspection visit", used by PHEQA's inspectors.

- Institution: a drop-down of all 214 institutions in the register, in the order they were registered.
- Address of the premises: typed.
- Date of the visit: picked from a calendar.
- Outcome: a free-text box.
- Number of inspectors who must sign: a box already filled with "2".
- The visit can be saved with any date.

**[paste them]:**

- The register of institutions: register number, name, kind, address of the premises, licence and standing.
- The outcomes of a visit allowed by PHEQA's standards: meets the standards; meets them with conditions; does not meet them.
- PHEQA's standards for new institutions, owned by the Registrar: a recommendation to the minister may rest only on a visit made within 90 days before it.

## Reading the output

### 1. The first four rules land where expected

The search, the address read from the register, the three outcomes and the 90-day rule match the corrected screen of the worked example almost line for line. Each change names what it needs from the system, which is what the builder needs to know.

### 2. The "2" is caught, but the owner is left blank

The draft saw that nothing pasted supports two signatures and refused to keep the value. It did not name an owner, because none was pasted. The rule asks for one: at PHEQA the question went to the Registrar, who answered "one inspector and the head of the inspectorate".

### 3. The inspector sets aside the date breach

The second row for the date says the system knows when the visit happened. It does not: an inspector records a visit when she is back at her desk, often days later, and today's date would be wrong. Shown the list, an inspector does not recognise this breach. Under the safeguard it is set aside, not built.
