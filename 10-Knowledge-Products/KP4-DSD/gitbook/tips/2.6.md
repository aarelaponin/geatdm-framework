## Example input

**[country X]:** Progressa.

**[paste the description]:**

1. PHEQA's application runs on PHEQA's own installation of the low-code platform and keeps the register of institutions.
2. Applicants and PHEQA's officers sign in through PNIA.
3. The applicant pays the application fee through the Payments block.
4. The registration officer sends PHEQA's recommendation, with the inspection's findings, to the minister.
5. The minister's decision on a licence is recorded in PHEQA's application.
6. MoEYS uses the register of institutions to approve changes of name and to publish the list in the Gazette.
7. Every exchange between PHEQA and MoEYS goes through Linkup, operated by PDGA.

## Reading the output

### 1. Most rows stand on a sentence

X-1 to X-4 each quote the sentence they rest on and match Progressa's table. X-6 is rightly marked "inferred": the description says MoEYS approves changes of name, not that the approval comes back to PHEQA. The registration desk can confirm that it does.

### 2. X-5 turns a reading into a copy

Sentence 6 says MoEYS uses the register; it does not say PHEQA sends it anything. The assistant filled the gap with updates pushed to MoEYS "so its list stays current". That gives MoEYS a copy of the register, the very thing the project exists to remove. The row should be an answer to MoEYS's reading, each time MoEYS needs an institution. It should have been marked "inferred: question", and it was not.

### 3. The confirmation catches it

The safeguard is confirmation by the body on the other side. When the Director of Higher Education reads X-5, MoEYS has not agreed to keep or update any copy, and the row goes back as a question. Without that check, PHEQA's ICT unit would have accepted a promise made on MoEYS's behalf.
