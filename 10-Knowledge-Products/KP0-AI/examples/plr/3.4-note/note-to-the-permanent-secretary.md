# The learner registry prototype: what it shows and does not

*From the head of the planning unit, Ministry of Education, Youth and Skills, to the Permanent Secretary*

The prototype shows a learner's one record, under the registry's number, through enrolment, a move of school, the examinations reading and the planning figures. Its search misses a registered child whose name is spelt differently. It is a prototype for agreement, built by assistant sessions under the Orchestrator, and a service a government runs is specified and generated the way KP4 teaches; it is not the registry itself.

## What it shows

As the planning unit decided, the registry issues a learner's number at first enrolment and holds the birth registration number where one exists.

The registry is searched first: a child it finds is not entered again, and a child it misses is still found if Civil Registration returns a birth registration number a registered learner holds. If Civil Registration cannot be reached, the enrolment goes on, flagged "identity to be confirmed", and the child is referred; if the National ID Authority cannot confirm the parent, no number is issued until it does.

A learner moving school keeps the same number and history.

The National Examinations Authority, reading a learner, receives the six fields its basis allows and is refused the other seven, each refusal recorded, on two learners, with simulated faults off and on.

The planning unit's three figures are counted from the records whenever the view opens.

## What it does not show

All data is invented and looks it; every screen carries the banner "Invented data, Progressa is fictional".

The search matches family name, given names and date of birth exactly, capitals aside. Of the 40 invented children on more than one list, it finds 20 from every list, and misses the other 20 on the one list where their family name is spelt differently. Both numbers are set in the invented data. Whether the birth record look-up finds those 20 is not shown; it needs a birth record.

Learners counted without a birth record include children whose birth record could not be reached.

Every basis for sharing a learner's or parent's data is the prototype's assumption, for the Data Protection Commission to confirm. No invented learner holds a national number, so its refusal is not shown against a value.

Civil Registration, the National ID Authority and Linkup, which the Ministry has not joined, are simulated, not connected. The birth record look-up depends on the Civil Registration Department answering across Linkup, which the Ministry cannot oblige. The prototype runs on one computer, unpublished.

## What is asked

This note decides nothing. Open are whether the search should find names spelt differently, and two questions the paper on what identifies a learner puts to the planning unit: whether the Ministry asks the Civil Registration Department to answer across Linkup, and whether it asks the National ID Authority and the Department, separately, to issue a national number at birth registration.
