# 2.1 Build the context once

> **Single message.** Write down once, with dates and sources, what every task needs to know about your country and your unit, and give each task only the part it needs.

## The concept

Every AI usage tip in this knowledge base begins 'Below is…'. It needs your material: your country's strategy, your sector's programmes, the laws that bind you. Without it, the assistant fills the gap from what it learned in training, which is general, undated and often wrong about your country. With it, the same prompt gives an answer about your own situation.

Most people build that material again for every task, a little differently each time. Build it once instead. Write a context pack: a few short sections that describe your country and your unit, each dated, each claim with its source. Then every task starts from the same checked facts. This is the same idea as a building block that a government plans once and every programme re-uses: the work is done once, and done properly, because many things depend on it.

More context is not better context. The engineers who build assistants [call the discipline context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents), and state its rule simply: give the assistant the smallest set of information that makes the result you want most likely. A pack of twenty pages pasted into a task that needs one section buries that section. The assistant gives each sentence some weight, so pages it does not need make the answer worse. [Independent research shows it too](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/): models use information placed in the middle of a long context less well than information at its start or end. Keep the whole pack, and give each task only its part.

A context pack goes stale. A strategy is approved, a body is renamed, a programme is cancelled. Date every section, and the pack tells you when it needs a second look.

## In the tool

Shown in Claude, and the same in any assistant that can search the web.

Building it. The research prompts of [Play 0](../../start-here/play-0.md) build the country context pack, called A0 in the other courses' workbooks, section by section: the digital landscape, the programmes, how the ministry works, the roles, the country's characteristics, the public bodies and systems, and the laws. Run them with web search on, one per chat, and ask for text in the chat, not a file.

Keeping it. Copy each answer into one document of your own, under its section heading, with the date you ran it. Open the sources on anything you will carry into a briefing, and mark any claim you could not check. Keep posts, not names: take out the names of office-holders before the pack is shared.

Using it. Paste the section a tip names, or attach the pack as a file and say which section the task uses. [Module 2's next page](2-2.md) shows how to keep the pack in a project, so that it no longer needs pasting.

**Where this is in each assistant:** [Claude: web search](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10684626-enable-and-use-web-search) · [ChatGPT: search](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search) · [Gemini: sources](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/14143489) · [Microsoft 365 Copilot: web search](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/manage-public-web-access)

## Worked example — Progressa's context pack, trimmed for one task

*Progressa is the fictional country every course in this knowledge base uses; every body, figure and date in the example is invented. MoEYS is its Ministry of Education, Youth and Skills; PNEA its National Examinations Authority; PNIA its National ID Authority; PDGA its Digital Government Authority; and the PLR the Progressa Learner Registry it plans.*

Progressa's context pack has seven sections. They are on [the Progressa page](../../start-here/progressa.md) of this knowledge base, ready to paste. The planning unit of MoEYS had a task that needed only one of them: draft the legal-basis paragraph of a data-sharing request to the examinations authority.

The first run pasted the whole pack. The paragraph that came back talked about connectivity, donors and the Digital Transformation Roadmap, and named the Data Protection Act only in passing. The second run pasted only section 7, the laws and policy, with its date. The paragraph named the two obligations of the Data Protection Act 2023 for a minor's data, a legal basis and parental consent for any use the law does not require, and noted that Progressa has no access-to-information act.

The AI usage tip under [Your turn](#your-turn-trim-a-context-pack-to-what-one-task-needs) does the trimming. Run on this task, it kept section 7 and one sentence of section 3, the question the ministry faces, which restates the consent rule, and it flagged one thing as stale: section 7 does not say whether the Data Protection Commission has issued guidance since the pack was dated.

## Your turn — Trim a context pack to what one task needs

{% hint style="success" %}
**💡 AI usage tip** · Trim a context pack to what one task needs

**Bring:** your context pack and the task.
**Get:** the trimmed pack, the sections dropped with the reason, and what the pack lacks or may have let go stale.
**Time:** ~10 min · **Assistant:** any; the tips are tool-neutral.
**Watch for:** A law or a limit dropped without a word.
{% endhint %}

**The problem it solves.** A context pack is written once and is long; a task needs only part of it. Pasting the whole pack buries what matters, and choosing by hand each time is slow and misses things.

Copy the prompt, replace the bracketed parts with your own material, and run it in the assistant your organisation has approved. Never paste personal data or unpublished documents: see [1.4](../module-1/1-4.md).

Two routes: **practise** on the Progressa material in the second tab and check your result against the answer key, or **bring your own** material.

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Prompt" %}
```text
Below is my context pack, dated [the date], in sections [paste the pack]. Below that is the task I am about to give an AI assistant [paste the task, or the prompt you will run]. Select only what the task needs. For each section, say: (a) whether the task needs it: all of it, part of it, or none; (b) if part, the sentences to keep, quoted word for word; (c) one line on why. Then list anything the task needs that the pack does not hold, and anything in the kept part that may be out of date, with the reason. Do not rewrite or summarise the kept sentences. Output: the trimmed pack, as the kept sentences under their section headings; then the table of sections; then the two lists.
```
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Practise on Progressa" %}
*Invented for this course: every name, number and date below is fictional. Paste it where the prompt asks for your material.*

Paste the seven sections of the Progressa page of this knowledge base as the pack, dated 12 October 2026, with one change: replace the data-exchange paragraph of section 1 with this older copy, dated March 2024. 'Data exchange: Linkup, the planned national data-exchange layer, is in procurement; no ministry exchanges data through it yet.'

The task: 'Draft a one-page note for the Permanent Secretary on what MoEYS must do to exchange learner data with PNIA through Linkup.'

{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Answer key" %}
*Read this after your own run. Your result will be worded differently; check that it finds the same things.*

Kept: the section 1 data-exchange paragraph; from section 3, that Linkup exists and MoEYS is not a member; from section 7, the Data Protection Act row and the Civil Registration Act row; from section 6, that Linkup has a documented onboarding procedure for members. Dropped: sections 2, 4 and 5, with a line each.

Out of date: the section 1 paragraph. It is dated March 2024 and says Linkup is in procurement, but section 6 says it went live as a pilot in 2025, and section 3 that it exists. A good run names the contradiction; a weak one keeps the paragraph without a word.

Not in the pack: what Linkup's onboarding procedure asks of a new member, and who in PDGA approves it.

{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Safeguard.** The assistant can drop a constraint the task needs without saying so: a law, a limit on data, a body that must agree. Before you use the trimmed pack, read the list of dropped sections and put back anything that binds the task.
{% endhint %}
