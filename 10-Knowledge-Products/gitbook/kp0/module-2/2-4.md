# 2.4 Judgement: what never goes into the setup

> **Single message.** A setup is used by every later chat, so nothing confidential goes into it, every file carries a date, and the intent is written down.

## The concept

What goes into one chat is used once. What goes into a project, a memory, a skill or a connector is used by every later chat, and often by every colleague the setup is shared with. So [the rules of Module 1](../module-1/1-4.md) bind harder here. And a setup can bring in text that steers the assistant: a skill or a connected source written by someone else can carry instructions, so know who wrote each one.

Nothing confidential goes into a setup: no records of citizens or learners, not even a sample, no unpublished cabinet papers, and no names of office-holders (posts, not names). A connector to a source that holds such data puts it within reach of every chat that uses the connector. And an assistant that remembers across chats can carry a detail from one conversation into another.

Context goes stale. A file added in March is still read in October, and the assistant cannot tell that it has been replaced. Date every file, replace rather than add, and review the setup on a date written in the brief.

Write the intent down. The researcher [Margaret-Anne Storey](https://margaretstorey.com/blog/2026/02/09/cognitive-debt/) names three kinds of debt that work with assistants piles up. Technical debt lives in the code. Cognitive debt lives in people: work accepted that nobody in the team can explain. Intent debt lives in the documents: the goals and limits that were never written down, so neither the next colleague nor the assistant can follow them. A project brief that says what the unit is for, what it must never do and why, pays off intent debt. Without one, a setup runs on whatever the person who made it remembered.

## In the tool

Shown in Claude.

Checking a project. Open the project and read its instructions and its list of files, as a stranger would. For each file, ask what it is, its date, and whether it may be shown to everyone the project is shared with.

Checking memory. If memory is on, the settings show what the assistant has kept about you from earlier chats. Read it, correct what is wrong, and delete what should not be kept. For a chat that must leave nothing behind, use an incognito chat, which is not saved and not remembered.

Checking connectors and skills. The settings list the connectors and skills in use. Remove any that no current task needs, and any whose author you cannot name.

**Where this is in each assistant:** [Claude: memory](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273-use-claude-s-chat-search-and-memory-to-build-on-previous-context) · [Claude: incognito chat](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12260368-use-incognito-chats) · [ChatGPT: memory](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-faq) · [Gemini: memory](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16598623) · [Microsoft 365 Copilot: memory](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/manage-copilot-memory-in-microsoft-365-copilot)

## Worked example — A project audited

*Progressa is the fictional country every course in this knowledge base uses; every body, figure and date in the example is invented. MoEYS is its Ministry of Education, Youth and Skills; PNEA its National Examinations Authority; PNIA its National ID Authority; PDGA its Digital Government Authority; and the PLR the Progressa Learner Registry it plans.*

On the first working day of the quarter, the head of the planning unit of MoEYS audited the project for the Progressa Learner Registry (PLR), as its brief requires. The AI usage tip under [Your turn](#your-turn-audit-what-a-project-holds) read the brief and the list of files.

Since the brief in 2.2 was written, the unit had added three files, and the audit found three things. The roles register was last year's, and named two office-holders: it was replaced by the current one, by post. There were two versions of the Digital Transformation Roadmap 2024–2030, the draft now awaiting cabinet and an earlier working draft, and some answers had quoted the Roadmap as if it were in force: the working draft was removed, and the brief now says the Roadmap is a draft and not binding. And the brief never said why the Registry needs parental consent, so drafts kept treating consent as optional: one line was added under standing rules, quoting the Data Protection Act 2023.

Memory held one item worth removing: the name of a head teacher mentioned in an earlier chat. The head of the unit deleted it, and recorded the audit and its date at the foot of the brief.

## Your turn — Audit what a project holds

{% hint style="success" %}
**💡 AI usage tip** · Audit what a project holds

**Bring:** the project's standing instructions and the list of its files with titles and dates.
**Get:** an audit of what the project holds, with an action for each item and the lines of intent to add.
**Time:** ~15–20 min · **Assistant:** any; the tips are tool-neutral.
**Watch for:** A harmless title can hide what a file holds.
{% endhint %}

**The problem it solves.** A project grows by addition. Files are added and rarely removed, and the brief drifts from the work. Nobody checks what every chat in it is now reading, or whether it should.

Copy the prompt, replace the bracketed parts with your own material, and run it in the assistant your organisation has approved. Never paste personal data or unpublished documents: see [1.4](../module-1/1-4.md).

Two routes: **practise** on the Progressa material in the second tab and check your result against the answer key, or **bring your own** material.

```text
Below are the standing instructions of a project my team shares in an AI assistant [paste the instructions], and the list of its files, each with its title and date [paste the list]. Audit the project. For the instructions and for each file, produce one row with: (a) the item; (b) its date, or 'no date'; (c) any risk: personal data or names, unpublished or confidential material, out of date, contradicted by another item, or two versions of the same document; (d) the action: keep, replace, remove, or ask the owner. For each skill or connector the project uses, add a row that says who wrote it and whether the team has read it. Then list the goals, limits and reasons the work depends on that the instructions do not state, as lines to add. Judge only the titles, dates and text given; where a risk can only be seen in a file's content, say 'read the file'. Output: the table, then the lines to add, then the number of items marked for each action.
```

Three planted problems, and one more. (1) The brief names an office-holder: replace 'Ms R. Phiri' with the post. (2) Two copies of the Education Sector Plan: keep one.
