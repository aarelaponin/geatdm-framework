# 3.3 The report and the review

> **Single message.** The work chat reports with no verdict; a second chat that never saw it checks the report by its own route and says accepted or not accepted.

## Try it first — The 20 that slipped through

*From the record of the registry prototype the course team built for Progressa with the cycle this section teaches. The children's data in it is made up.*

The work chat's report on the duplicates count opened with its answer. 40 children appear on more than one list: 22 on two and 18 on three. 20 of them have one entry spelt differently. The prototype's search catches 20 of the 40 from every entry. It does not catch the other 20, and those are exactly the 20 with a spelling variant: each is missed from the one entry spelt differently, and found from every other. No entry returned the wrong child.

The review chat had never seen the work chat. It counted again from the three lists alone, without the key to the made-up data and without the prototype's search. Its own rule was that two entries belong to the same child when the date of birth, the school and the given names are the same, and the family names are the same or differ by one letter. It got the same figures, entry by entry, for all 98 entries. The verdict was accepted.

**Your question.** The review agreed with the report. Why does the review's route matter more than its agreement?

<details>

<summary>Answer key: open it after you have tried</summary>

A review that repeats the report's own steps will agree with it, mistakes included: it would have used the same key and the same search, and an empty registry would have looked clean twice. Agreement by the same route proves only that the steps were followed.

This review reached the figures from different material by a different method. Two routes that start apart and arrive at the same figures are evidence; one route walked twice is not. The review also altered one entry in its own count and saw the figures move, which showed that its count could fail.

</details>

## The concept

The report gives the answer first, in one paragraph. Then it goes through the tests one by one, each with its evidence. It says what it found and did not fix, and what it could not establish. It carries no verdict: the work chat does not judge its own work.

Watch the report for one fault above all: a limit of one step stated as a limit of the whole. 'The search misses a child spelt differently' is what the duplicates count showed. 'The registry does not recognise the child' is wider, because a later step, the look-up at the birth register, may find the child, and that was not tested.

The review is a check, not a reading. It goes through the instruction's tests and looks at the evidence for each one itself. It works out at least one figure again, by a route the report did not use. A check that cannot be run counts as not met. And it ends with one of two verdicts, accepted or not accepted, with no third word. 'Accepted with reservations' is not accepted.

Be wary of a review with zero findings on work that matters: it may not have checked anything. Ask which figure it worked out again, and how. And remember what a check can and cannot see. A check sees that a field is filled, not that it is true; that a citation exists, not that it says what the sentence claims. One check should end at the real thing: the consent check of the prototype sent a learner's record to the examinations authority through the running system, and saw it refuse all seven fields the authority has no basis for and pass all six it has.

Research on using models as judges gives one more reason to keep the reviewer separate: [a model tends to favour answers that it wrote itself](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685).

## In the tool

By hand, in any assistant. The work chat: open a new chat with memory off. Paste the work-chat prompt from [the starter folder](../starter-folder/README.md), the report form, your instruction and the material. Save its report in the reports folder under the instruction's name, and write a log row: 'report back'.

The review chat: open another new chat. Never use the work chat, and never a chat that helped write the instruction. Paste the review-chat prompt, the review form, the instruction, the report and the material. Save its review in the reviews folder, and write a log row with its verdict.

Memory must be off before the review chat starts. A review chat that remembers the work chat is no longer independent. The setting is in your assistant's settings; Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot each have one under their own name. An incognito chat, which is not saved and not remembered, also serves.

If your assistant can read your folder, through the desktop app and a file tool, let the work chat write its report into the reports folder and the review chat read it from there. There is then one copy of each document, and nothing is lost in pasting.

## On your work — Run your review chat

{% hint style="success" %}
**💡 AI usage tip** · Run your review chat

**Bring:** the instruction, the report and the material, in a new chat that never saw the work chat.
**Get:** each test checked, one figure worked out again, a verdict, and the gaps for version 2.
**Time:** ~20 min · **Assistant:** any; the tips are tool-neutral.
**Watch for:** A review that only re-reads the report.
{% endhint %}

**The problem it solves.** A review that reads the report and agrees with it has checked nothing. The review must test the report against the instruction and work out a figure again by its own route.

Copy the prompt, replace the bracketed parts with your own material, and run it in the assistant your organisation has approved. Never paste personal data or unpublished documents: see [1.4](../module-1/1-4.md).

Two routes: **practise** on the Progressa material in the second tab and check your result against the answer key, or **bring your own** material.

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Prompt" %}
```text
You are a review chat. You did not write this instruction and you did not do this work. Below are the instruction [paste it], the report [paste it] and the material [paste it or attach it]. Check the report against the instruction, test by test: for each, say met or not met, and point to the evidence you looked at yourself. Work out at least one figure of the report again by a route the report did not use, and say what your route was and what you got. A check that cannot run counts as not met. Look for any sentence that states a limit of one step as a limit of the whole. Fix nothing. End with exactly one verdict, accepted or not accepted. If not accepted, list the gaps, numbered, each one written so that it can be added to the instruction as version 2.
```
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Practise on Progressa" %}
*Invented for this course: every name, number and date below is fictional. Paste it where the prompt asks for your material.*

A report from MoEYS's planning unit: 'Answer: 37 schools did not report enrolment in the 2026 census. Test T1 met: counted from the census file. Found, not fixed: none.' The census file lists 412 schools; 31 have no return at all and 6 have a return with the enrolment field empty.

{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Answer key" %}
*Read this after your own run. Your result will be worded differently; check that it finds the same things.*

Not accepted. The figure 37 is 31 plus 6, so the report has counted two different things as one without saying so; the instruction needs to define 'did not report'. A good review works the figure out again by its own route, for example by listing the schools with no return separately from those with an empty field, and names the gap. A review that says 'T1 met, accepted' has only re-read the report.

{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Safeguard.** A review chat can agree for the wrong reason. Before you accept, find the figure it worked out again and check that its route really differs from the report's. If the review found nothing, treat that as a warning.
{% endhint %}
