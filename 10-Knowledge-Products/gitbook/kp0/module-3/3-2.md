# 3.2 Write the instruction

> **Single message.** Write the instruction for a stranger: what it serves, the tests, how this work can fail while seeming to succeed, and how the review will check it, all before the work starts.

## Try it first — The duplicates count

*From the record of the registry prototype the course team built for Progressa with the cycle this section teaches. The children's data in it is made up.*

The registry must not register the same child twice. The prototype holds made-up data for 300 children spread over three lists, and some children are on more than one list, some with their name spelt differently on one of them. A note to the Permanent Secretary was going to say how well the prototype avoids a second record, so the figure had to be checked.

The instruction asked two questions: how many children appear on more than one list, and how many of them the prototype's search catches. It defined 'caught' before the work started: a child is caught only when the search finds it from every one of its entries. And it said how the review would check the figure: by counting again from the three lists alone, without the prototype's search.

Its section on how the work could fail named the faults of checking (from the page of faults) and then six ways particular to this task. Before you open the key, write down the ways you would have named.

**Your question.** Which of the six ways it could fail would you have missed?

<details>

<summary>Answer key: open it after you have tried</summary>

The six, as the instruction named them: (1) quoting the planned figures instead of counting; (2) matching the names with a method of the chat's own instead of the prototype's search; (3) counting a child as caught from one entry while another of its entries was missed; (4) searching from only one list; (5) searching an empty registry, so that nothing is found and the result looks clean; (6) the data changing between the count and the catch.

(5) and (6) are the easiest to miss, because they give a clean-looking wrong answer. Each failure named in advance is one the work chat is told to avoid and the review is told to look for.

</details>

## The concept

An instruction is written for a stranger: a chat that knows nothing of your conversations, your unit or what you meant. If a stranger could not carry it out from the page and the material, it is not finished.

It says what the work serves: who will use the result, for what, and by when. It lists the material, by name, and says that nothing outside the list is evidence. It sets out the task, and defines each figure, as the duplicates count defined 'caught'. It gives the tests, numbered, each one a stranger could run. Write the tests before the work starts: a measure written after the thing it measures is not a measure.

It names how this work can fail while seeming to succeed. First come the faults of its kind, from the page of faults in [the starter folder](../starter-folder/README.md). Then come the two or three ways particular to this task, the ones that give a clean-looking wrong answer. It says what is out of scope, and which questions stay with you. And it carries one line every instruction carries: if something you need is not in the material, say so and name who could supply it, and do not supply it.

Last, it names the review's route: the figure the review will work out again, and the different way it will do it. Name it now, so that the work chat cannot borrow it.

Choose the assistant's model by the kind of work, never by how important the subject is. A count from a table needs care, not the largest model; a judgement between two positions may need the strongest one you have.

## In the tool

By hand, in any assistant. Set up first, as the starter folder's START-HERE says: turn memory off, and decide whether you will paste files or let the assistant read the folder.

Copy the instruction form into a new file in the instructions folder. Name it by date, piece of work and version, for example '2026-10-30 duplicates v1'. Fill its eight sections. Copy the faults from the page for your kind of work into section 6, then add the ways particular to your task.

A chat may help you draft the instruction. If it does, that chat does no work afterwards and no review: it helped to commission the work.

Write a row in the log: the date, the piece of work, version 1, 'instruction issued', and the file.

## On your work — Test your instruction on a stranger

{% hint style="success" %}
**💡 AI usage tip** · Test your instruction on a stranger

**Bring:** your instruction, in a new chat with memory off.
**Get:** the work as the chat understands it, and a numbered list of questions, each tied to a line of the instruction.
**Time:** ~15 min · **Assistant:** any; the tips are tool-neutral.
**Watch for:** A chat that answers its own questions instead of asking.
{% endhint %}

**The problem it solves.** An instruction always seems complete to the person who wrote it, because they fill its gaps from what they know. A fresh chat knows nothing, so it shows the gaps.

Copy the prompt, replace the bracketed parts with your own material, and run it in the assistant your organisation has approved. Never paste personal data or unpublished documents: see [1.4](../module-1/1-4.md).

Two routes: **practise** on the Progressa material in the second tab and check your result against the answer key, or **bring your own** material.

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Prompt" %}
```text
Below is an instruction for a piece of work [paste the instruction]. Do not carry it out. You know nothing about this work except what the instruction and its material list say. First, say back in five sentences what you would do. Then list every question you would need answered before you could do it well: a term not defined, a figure with no source, a test you could not run, a choice left open, a document named but not given. Number the questions. For each, quote the line of the instruction it comes from. Do not guess the answers. Finally, say which of the instruction's tests you could not check, and why.
```
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Practise on Progressa" %}
*Invented for this course: every name, number and date below is fictional. Paste it where the prompt asks for your material.*

An instruction from MoEYS's planning unit, version 1: 'Count the schools that did not report enrolment in the 2026 census and write a short paragraph for the Permanent Secretary. Material: the census file. Tests: the count is correct. Out of scope: none.'

{% endtab %}

{% tab title="Answer key" %}
*Read this after your own run. Your result will be worded differently; check that it finds the same things.*

A good run asks at least these: what 'did not report' means (no return at all, or a return with the enrolment field empty?); which census file, and which version; how 'correct' will be tested, and by whom; who the paragraph is for and how long it may be; whether schools closed during the year count; what to do if a school appears twice. Each is a line the instruction should carry.

{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Safeguard.** Each question is a gap in the instruction; fill it in the instruction, not in the chat. Use a new chat for the work itself: the chat that tested the instruction has now helped to write it.
{% endhint %}
