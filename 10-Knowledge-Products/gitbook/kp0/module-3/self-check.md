# Self-check — Module 3

**Module 3 — Orchestrate: one piece of work, start to finish.** Answer each question before you read the answer beside it.

Five questions, drawn from the four pages. Questions 1 and 3 are multiple choice. Questions 2, 4 and 5 ask for a short answer, which is right if it makes the point of the model answer. Four or five right means Ready; below four, read again the page named beside each question you missed.

| # | Question | Model answer | Drawn from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | You ask a chat to draft a two-paragraph reply to a citizen's letter, from the letter alone. Do you need the cycle? (a) Yes: every piece of work should be reviewed. (b) No: none of the five conditions holds, so just ask, and read the reply yourself. (c) Yes, because the reply goes outside. (d) Only if the chat is not sure. | (b). One short text from one document, which you read in full before it goes out, meets none of the five conditions. The cycle costs a second chat and a log row; spend it where a mistake would be quoted, built on or kept. | [3.1](3-1.md) Two failures |
| 2 | The work chat writes: 'the system does not recognise duplicates', from a test of the search alone. What is wrong, and what do you ask for? | A limit of one step is stated as a limit of the whole. Ask what else could find the child, and have the sentence say only what was tested: 'the search misses a child spelt differently; whether a later step catches the child is not shown'. | [3.3](3-3.md) The report and the review |
| 3 | Your review chat answers 'accepted, no issues found' on a 20-page draft. What do you do? (a) Accept: the review found nothing. (b) Ask the work chat whether the review is right. (c) Treat zero findings as a warning, and check that the review worked out at least one figure by a route of its own. (d) Run the same review again in the same chat. | (c). A review with zero findings on work that matters may not have checked anything. The work chat cannot judge its own review, and the same chat would repeat itself. | [3.3](3-3.md) The report and the review |
| 4 | The review says 'accepted'. The draft recommends that MoEYS publish the figure in its annual report. Who decides whether it is published, and why? | You do, or whoever owns the annual report. A verdict says the work meets its instruction. What is delivered, cut, spent or said outside is always the person's decision, and accepted work can still be the wrong thing to publish. | [3.4](3-4.md) Judgement: you decide |
| 5 | Your instruction for a count of duplicates says: 'Test: the count is correct. The review checks the count.' What is wrong, and what do you write instead? | Only the work chat could pass that test by its own say-so, and the review has no route of its own. Define each figure before the work starts, such as when a child counts as caught; give tests a stranger could run; and name the review's route, such as counting again from the three lists alone, without the search. | [3.2](3-2.md) Write the instruction |
