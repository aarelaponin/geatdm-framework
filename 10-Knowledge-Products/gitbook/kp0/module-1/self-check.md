# Self-check — Module 1

**Module 1 — Chat: get good answers in one chat.** Answer each question before you read the answer beside it.

Four questions, drawn from the single messages of the four pages, to be answered after reading them. Questions 1 and 3 are multiple choice. Questions 2 and 4 ask for a short answer, which is right if it makes the point of the model answer. Three or four right means the module's point has landed; below three, read again the page named beside each question you missed. The threshold is a proposal, to be tested with the first learners.

| # | Question | Model answer | Drawn from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | An assistant gives you a clear, well-written summary of your country's data-protection law, with section numbers. You did not paste the law. What do you do? (a) Use it: it is clear and specific. (b) Ask the same assistant whether it is sure. (c) Treat every section number and obligation as a claim to check against the law itself before it goes anywhere. (d) Ask it to shorten the summary. | (c). Fluency is not evidence: an assistant writes an invented section number in the same tone as a real one. (b) does not help, because an assistant tends to agree with the framing of your question, including 'are you sure?'. | [1.1](1-1.md) A drafting partner, not an oracle |
| 2 | A colleague's prompt reads: 'Write a briefing on why our learner data is fragmented.' Name the four parts it lacks, and write the safeguard line you would add. | It lacks a named input (what is pasted), named outputs, a fixed format, and a safeguard line. A safeguard line for it: 'Name a cause only if the pasted documents state it; list any cause you inferred separately, as a question.' A generic line such as 'AI can make mistakes' does not count. | [1.2](1-2.md) The four-part prompt |
| 3 | A draft note from an assistant has no open questions at all, on a subject your unit has never settled. What does that most likely mean? (a) The subject is simpler than you thought. (b) The assistant answered the hard questions itself, so the draft needs a second reading. (c) The prompt was well written. (d) The source documents were complete. | (b). A draft on an unsettled subject with no open questions usually means the gaps were filled with plausible answers. Ask it to mark what it inferred and what it should have asked. | [1.3](1-3.md) Read the output before you use it |
| 4 | Your unit wants an assistant to find learners who appear in all three learner lists. What may go into the chat, and who answers for the result? | No names, numbers or records of real learners: only the column headings and a few invented rows, so the assistant can propose a matching method. The matching itself runs inside the ministry's own systems, and the head of the data unit, not the assistant, answers for the method and the result. | [1.4](1-4.md) Judgement: what stays out of the chat, and what stays yours |
