# Working with AI in Government

Five modules · 21 written pages · an AI usage tip on every page · a self-check per module · self-paced · free and open

This course teaches a government team to work with an AI assistant, from one good chat to work handed over for days and checked by someone other than the one who did it. It is written for public servants, most of them not developers, and assumes no programming.

## What you will learn

- **[Module 1 — one good chat](module-1/README.md).** Ask so that you get a usable answer, and check it before you use it.
- **[Module 2 — set the assistant up once](module-2/README.md).** Give it your context, standing instructions and skills, so that every chat starts from them.
- **[Module 3 — one piece of work, start to finish](module-3/README.md).** Take one piece of your own work through the cycle once, by hand: you write the instruction, one chat does the work, a second chat checks it, and you decide.
- **[Module 4 — many pieces of work, for weeks](module-4/README.md).** Keep the cycle going, still by hand: a log that says where the work stands, a list of the faults you have learned, and handovers to a fresh chat.
- **[Module 5 — the same method, with a plugin](module-5/README.md).** Let a plugin do the bookkeeping while you build a small practice prototype, a toy learner registry, and you still make every decision.

Every module ends with a judgement page: what stays out of the assistant, and what stays yours.

**Your way through.** If you commission and accept AI-assisted work, as a director or a head of unit, take Modules 1 to 3: enough to run any AI usage tip in this knowledge base, and to accept work done this way. If you run the work, as the head of an ICT unit, a project lead or an analyst, take all of them. Modules 1 to 4 need nothing installed; Module 5 needs the plugin.

## How to use this guide

Each page has two halves. **The concept** says why something works, in plain words, with its source. It works in any assistant, including the one your ministry has approved. **In the tool** says what to click, type or set, and is shown in Claude. Other assistants a ministry may have approved, such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft 365 Copilot, have the same settings under their own names, and their help pages say where. The pages name each setting by what it does, so you can find it in any of them.

Each page also carries a worked example set in [Progressa](../start-here/progressa.md), the fictional country every course here uses, an AI usage tip you can run on your own work, and its sources with their links. Every tip says what to bring, what you get, how long it takes and what to watch for before you use the result.

## Prerequisites

**No technical background is assumed.** No programming, no command line, no version control. Module 5 asks you to install the plugin and what it runs on; [With the plugin](starter-folder/with-the-plugin.md) lists them.

**What you do need:**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **A piece of your own work** | A briefing, a draft, a note you are about to write. The tips act on your work, not on a case study. Nothing to hand? Follow everything on [Progressa](../start-here/progressa.md) instead. |
| **An AI assistant** | The one your ministry has approved, or any general assistant. The *In the tool* halves are shown in Claude. |
| **Nothing to install, up to Module 4** | The optional [ea-plays kit](../start-here/ea-plays-kit.md) packages some tips as skills; [2.3](module-2/2-3.md) shows how to install one. Module 5 needs the orchestrator plugin and Desktop Commander; see [With the plugin](starter-folder/with-the-plugin.md). |

Time: each page is a short read and a tip of five to twenty minutes. A module is an afternoon.
