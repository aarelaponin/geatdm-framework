# 4.5 Reviewing the model: what it assumed, and what it could not express

{% hint style="info" %}
🎬 **Video in production:** *Reviewing the model: what it assumed, and what it could not express* (~5 min).
The page below does not depend on the video: it carries what the video will say. The [video index](../../start-here/video-index.md) is the page to watch for links.
{% endhint %}

> **Single message.** Every assumption and every loss is named for a person to rule on.

## The concept

An AI assistant writes the file your application is generated from. It will meet things nobody wrote down, and things the platform cannot do. The question for you is simple: does it tell you, or does it quietly decide?

Everything agreed reaches the file in one of four ways. Most things are placed directly, because there is only one place they can go. Some need a judgement, and the judgement is written down as an assumption: what was decided, and on what ground. Some cannot be placed at all, because the platform has no way to express them. These are written down as losses. A few stay in their own document, and nothing is owed for them in the file.

An assumption is a decision the writer had to make because the documents were silent. A loss is something you agreed that the file cannot express, with what was done instead, if anything. Both are normal. What is not acceptable is a gap filled by invention, with nobody told. That is why the assistant must refuse to guess. A gap it names can be ruled on. A gap it fills in silently becomes a fault in the running service.

You do not read the file. You read cards: one for each assumption and each loss. Each card says, in plain words, what the application will do because of it, and which document it rests on. The owner rules yes or no on each card, and the ruling is recorded with a name and a date. A 'no' is never typed into the file. It goes back to the document that owns the fact, and the file is written again from the corrected document.

Here are two cards from the review of the file of MoEYS, Progressa's ministry of education. The first is an assumption: a change of name takes effect on the day the minister approves it, because the goal's story did not say. The second is a loss: the story asks that the institution is told, and the platform cannot send that notice by itself, so it is kept as a task for an officer. The Director of Higher Education rules yes on both, and her rulings are recorded.

Suppose she had said no to the first card, because a change of name should take effect only when it is published in the Gazette. Her answer would not be typed into the file. It would go back to the goal's story, the one document that owns that fact. The story is corrected, the file is written again, and the cards are read again. The head of the ICT unit approves the file only when every card carries a ruling.

## Your turn — Turn the losses into yes-or-no questions

{% hint style="success" %}
**💡 AI usage tip** · Turn the losses into yes-or-no questions

**Bring:** The list of losses, each with what was asked, how it was handled and its document.
**Get:** A list of yes-or-no questions, one for each loss, in the loss's own words, each with what yes and no would mean.
**Time:** ~10–15 min · **Assistant:** any; the tips are tool-neutral.
**With the kit:** `application-model` — helps the team write the one file a program reads, and the review of what it assumed and could not express; `decision-cards` — one page of decision cards that the person who rules clicks through, with the same cards as a numbered list in the chat. Optional: the tip runs bare; see [the kit](../../start-here/ea-plays-kit.md).
**Watch for:** A question must keep the loss's own words; a question that softens or rewords what was asked can draw a 'yes' the owner did not mean.
{% endhint %}

**The problem it solves.** The review of an application model produces a list of losses written for builders. The owner who must rule on them needs each one as a question that can be answered yes or no, in words that say what the running service will do.

Copy the prompt, replace the bracketed parts with your own context, and run it in the AI assistant of your choice. Never paste personal data or unpublished documents — see [How to use the plays](../../start-here/how-to-use-the-plays.md).

```text
Below is the list of losses recorded for the application model of [the service] in [your ministry]: for each, what was asked, how it was handled instead, and the document it comes from [paste the list]. For each loss, write one question the owner of the service can answer yes or no. Keep the loss's own words for what was asked, in quotation marks. Then say in one plain sentence what the running service will do if the answer is yes, and in one sentence what must happen if the answer is no, naming the document that would be corrected. Do not answer any question, and do not merge two losses into one question. Output: a list of yes-or-no questions, one for each loss, in the loss's own words, each with its two consequences and the document it rests on.
```

**Q1.** The story asks that "the institution is told of the minister's decision". Do you accept that an officer sends this notice by hand, from a task the application gives her?
- *Yes:* each approval creates a task; an officer sends the notice and closes the task.
- *No:* the story of "approve a change of an institution's name" is corrected, and the model is written again.

**Q2.** Do you accept that the application prepares the list of registered institutions for publication?
- *Yes:* the application produces the notice as a file, ready for the Gazette.
- *No:* the story of "publish the list of registered institutions" is corrected.

**Q3.** The story asks that "the person who asked for the review is told the outcome". Do you accept that an officer tells them, from a task the application gives her?
- *Yes:* each recorded outcome creates a task; an officer tells the person and closes the task.
- *No:* the story of "review a decision of PHEQA" is corrected.

{% tab title="Reading the output" %}
This is the teaching part of the tip. Work through the output the way a chief architect would: what is earned, what is invented, what is missing.

<details>

<summary>1. One question per loss, each with its document</summary>

Three losses, three questions, none merged. Each "no" names the story that would be corrected, so a "no" goes back to the document that owns the fact and never into the file.

</details>

<details>

<summary>2. Q2 lost the loss's own words</summary>

The loss says "the minister publishes the list in the Gazette". Q2 asks whether the application "prepares the list for publication", which sounds complete. A "yes" to Q2 would not say who sends the file to the Gazette, or that nothing is published until someone does. Rewrite Q2 with the quotation, as Q1 and Q3 have it.

</details>

<details>

<summary>3. Yes leaves work on someone's desk</summary>

Two "yes" answers create tasks for officers. Before the Director of Higher Education rules, ask who at MoEYS will take them. Record each ruling with her name and the date.

</details>

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Safeguard.** A question must keep the loss's own words; a question that softens or rewords what was asked can draw a 'yes' the owner did not mean. Record each answer with the owner's name and the date before the model is approved.
{% endhint %}
