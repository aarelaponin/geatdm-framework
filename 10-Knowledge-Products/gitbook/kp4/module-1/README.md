# Module 1 — Why digital services go wrong, and the method that prevents it

**Written for:** the team that commissions the service and makes the case to the minister (Strategist register). **Videos:** 5.

**What it teaches.** The three places an agreed service gets lost; three rules to hold a supplier to; named questions; the twelve documents; who writes, checks and accepts.

**What you can do afterwards.** Explain to the minister why an agreed service gets lost on the way and what the twelve documents of the method put in its way.

**What it uses of the worked example.** The story of an institution that registered at the quality authority and filled in the same particulars again at the ministry.

## Subtopics

| # | Subtopic | Class | Demonstration | Single message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [1.1](1-1.md) | Where an agreed service gets lost | Core | — | An agreed service is lost at three places: a question nobody decided, an answer nobody passed on, a rule nobody enforced. |
| [1.2](1-2.md) | Three rules you can hold a supplier to | Core | — | Review a story, not a list; measure against a list the design did not write; end one check on the running system. |
| [1.3](1-3.md) | A question with a name on it is part of the specification | Core | — | An open question with an owner and a date is acceptable; a question answered by a guess is a fault. |
| [1.4](1-4.md) | Twelve documents from the request to the running service | Core | — | Each document has a writer, an input, an output, a person who accepts it and a check, in a fixed order. |
| [1.5](1-5.md) | Who writes, who checks, who accepts | Core | — | The AI assistant may draft; a named person accepts, amends or sets aside each proposal, and only what was accepted stands. |
