# E2.2 — Five entries of PHEQA's register of requirements

**Subtopic:** 2.2 — Write down what was asked before anyone designs

**Public anchor:** None; the content is the SDD method's own: the register of requirements

**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name, date and figure in it is invented. Progressa's Higher Education Regulations are invented for these examples.

**What it shows.** Five entries of PHEQA's register drawn from Progressa's regulations on higher education, each carrying the words of the regulation and the article it comes from, with two open questions the text leaves recorded with an owner.

**Form of this example.** The lines of an entry are the team's blank instrument and name no country. The entries are filled for Progressa from its invented Higher Education Regulations of 2025.

## What the register is

The register lists every separate thing the customer asked for, one entry each, in the customer's own words, with where it was said. It is written before anyone designs anything. An AI assistant may extract the entries from the text; the analyst rules on every one. Later, every goal of the service must serve at least one entry, and every entry must be served by a goal (subtopic 2.4). The register is the list the design did not write (subtopic 1.2).

## The five entries

| Entry | What was asked, in the regulation's words | Where it was said | What would show it is met | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | "An application for a licence shall be made to the Authority through its electronic self-service by the person who intends to operate the institution." | Higher Education Regulations 2025, article 6(1) | An applicant signed in through PNIA lodges an application in PHEQA's self-service, and the registration officer sees it with the applicant's name and the date received | A function |
| R-02 | "The Authority shall inspect the premises and facilities of the proposed institution before it makes a recommendation to the Minister." | Article 9(2) | No recommendation can be sent to the minister for an application that has no recorded inspection | A function |
| R-03 | "The Minister shall decide whether to grant the licence, having regard to the recommendation of the Authority." | Article 11(1) | The licence is granted only on a decision of the minister, received from MoEYS; PHEQA's application records the decision and does not take it | A constraint |
| R-04 | "The register of institutions shall be open to the public." | Article 14(3) | Any person can look up an institution in the register by its name or its register number, without signing in | A function |
| R-05 | "A person aggrieved by a decision of the Authority may request the Minister to review the decision." | Article 22(1) | A request for review reaches MoEYS with the decision it concerns, and the minister's decision on it returns to PHEQA | A function |

Each entry keeps the regulation's own words in quotation marks. The column "What would show it is met" is written by the analyst in plain words, so that the Registrar can tell whether a later design keeps the promise.

## The two questions the text leaves open

Reading the five articles, the analyst found two things the regulations do not say. Neither is answered by guess (subtopic 1.3); each is recorded with an owner and a date.

| Question | Arises from | Why it matters | Owner | Answer needed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q-01 Within how many days of a decision of PHEQA may a person ask the minister to review it? | R-05: article 22 gives the right and no period | The self-service must know when to stop offering the request | The Director of Higher Education of MoEYS | 30 October 2026 |
| Q-02 Does "open to the public" in article 14 include the name of the person who operates an institution, or only the institution's own particulars? | R-04 | The public register must not show a fact the law does not allow to be shown | The Registrar of PHEQA | 30 October 2026 |

## What the Registrar does with it

The Registrar of PHEQA reads each entry against the article it cites. She accepts it, amends it or sets it aside, and for each one set aside the register keeps the entry with the reason, the date and her name. An entry the assistant extracted with no article beside it is struck out unless somebody can say where it was asked. She names an owner for each open question before she accepts the register.

**What to look for before you accept a register.** Pick three entries at random and open the article each cites. Are the words in quotation marks the words in the article? Is there one thing asked per entry, or three things in one sentence? Is every open question owned by a person in your organisation or a partner body, and not by the supplier?
