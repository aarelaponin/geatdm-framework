# E3.5 — The walk-through of "apply for a provisional licence"

**Subtopic:** 3.5 — The walk-through your officials click

**Public anchor:** None; the content is the SDD method's own: the walk-through produced by a program from the written screens

**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name, date and figure in it is invented.

**What it shows.** The walk-through of the provisional-licence goal, one page for each screen, each stamped with the version of the screen record it was produced from, which a registrar of PHEQA and the supplier's builder click through together and agree on the same pages.

**Form of this example.** The walk-through and its version stamp are the method's and name no country. The pages are those of the screens of E3.3, for Progressa.

## What the walk-through is

The walk-through is a set of simple web pages, one for each screen, linked in the order the applicant meets them, with the failures as side paths. Nobody draws it. A program produces it from the written screens, so it shows exactly what the screens say and nothing else. If the screens change, the program produces the pages again. Nobody corrects a page by hand, because a page corrected by hand would no longer show what the builder will build.

## The pages

Every page carries the same stamp at its top: **"Screens of 'apply for a provisional licence', version 1.2, 14 November 2026."**

| Page | The screen it shows | What the person clicking sees | Where it leads |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S1 — Ask to apply for a provisional licence | The applicant's name from PNIA; the applicant's earlier applications; the button *Apply for a provisional licence* | Page 2 |
| 2 | S2 — Read PHEQA's standards and state the kind of institution | The standards, with their version; the three kinds of institution | Page 3; or *Stop without applying*, to the failure ending |
| 3 | S3 — Give the particulars | The proposed name, the address and each particular; the evidence of funding | Page 4; side pages 3a (a document left out) and 3b (the name already held) |
| 4 | S4 — Confirm and pay | Everything given, shown back; the fee due; *Confirm and pay* | Page 5; side page 4a (the fee not recorded) |
| 5 | S5 — See the application received | The application's number, the date received, the institution and its particulars | The success ending |
| — | The two endings | "The application is received" and "No application is made, and nothing is recorded" | — |

## The session

The Registrar of PHEQA and the supplier's builder sat in front of one screen for forty minutes, with the head of the registration desk beside them, and clicked from page 1 to the success ending, then down each side path.

Three things happened that a list of fields would never have shown.

1. On page 2 the head of the registration desk asked: "Where does the applicant see the fee before it starts?" It does not, until page 4. The Registrar asked for the fee to be shown on page 2 as well, beside the standards, so that nobody types every particular before learning the price. This is a change to the screens; the walk-through will be produced again from the changed screens.
2. On side page 3b the Registrar asked: "If the name is held by an institution whose licence was cancelled, is it still held?" Nobody in the room knew. It became an open line for the review (E3.6), owned by the Registrar.
3. On page 5 the builder asked: "Is the application's number the same as the institution's register number?" The head of the desk said no: the institution receives its register number only when it is registered. The builder had assumed the opposite. Both now agree on the same page.

## A shared picture

This is where the policy side and the technical side of the service meet. The Registrar and the head of the registration desk recognised their counter in the pages; the builder saw exactly what to build. They pointed at the same page, with the same version stamp, and agreed or disagreed about the same thing. A walk-through drawn by hand in a presentation tool cannot do this: it may show what the designer hoped, not what the screens say, and nobody can tell which version of the screens it shows.

**What to look for at a walk-through.** Check that every page carries the same version as the screens you are being asked to accept. Ask the official, not the builder, to hold the mouse. Every question the pages cannot answer is written down as an open line, not answered by the builder from memory.
