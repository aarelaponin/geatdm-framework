# E1.1 — One institution, two counters: where Progressa's service was lost

**Subtopic:** 1.1 — Where an agreed service gets lost

**Public anchor:** None; the content is the SDD method's own: an agreed service is lost at three places, and a silence that is named, owned and counted is a specification

**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name, date and figure in it is invented.

**What it shows.** The story of a private institution in Progressa that registered at PHEQA and then filled in the same particulars again at MoEYS, told in the words of the institution's registrar, with each loss placed at one of the three places where an agreed service gets lost.

**Form of this example.** The three places are the method's and name no country. The story is built for Progressa.

## The three places

An agreed service is lost in one of three ways. **A question nobody decided:** the people who agreed the service never settled a point, so the builder settled it alone, or nobody did. **An answer nobody passed on:** somebody decided, but the decision never reached the document or the person who needed it. **A rule nobody enforced:** the rule was written down, but no check, no person and no program held anyone to it.

## The story, in the registrar's words

The registrar of Harbourview University College, a private institution in Progressa, tells it like this.

"We applied to PHEQA for our provisional licence in March. Their self-service asked for everything: our name, our address, our kind of institution, our governing board, our programmes, our premises. In June PHEQA entered us in the register of institutions, and we received our register number, INS-00217.

"In September the ministry wrote to us. MoEYS was preparing the list of registered institutions for the Gazette and wanted our particulars. We filled in their form: our name, our address, our kind of institution, our board. The same answers we had given PHEQA six months earlier. The ministry's form had no place for our register number, so we did not give it. Two weeks later the ministry asked why our address on their form differed from PHEQA's letter. We had moved our office in August and told PHEQA, but not the ministry. Both bodies were right about what they had been told. Neither knew what the other knew.

"Everybody had agreed, in the national strategy, that an institution gives its particulars to the state once. We gave them twice and were then asked to explain the difference."

## Each loss, placed

| What went wrong | Which of the three places | What would have prevented it |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody ever decided whether MoEYS may read PHEQA's register of institutions, so MoEYS built its own form and asked again | A question nobody decided | A named question, "May MoEYS read PHEQA's register, and under what rules?", owned by the Registrar of PHEQA and the Director of Higher Education, with a date |
| PHEQA gave the institution an identifier, INS-00217, but nobody passed it to MoEYS, so the ministry's form had no place for it and could not look the institution up | An answer nobody passed on | The identifier written into MoEYS's documents as the key by which it reads an institution, so the ministry asks for nothing it can read |
| The rule that a fact is entered once was written in the national strategy and enforced nowhere: no document of either project tested it, and no review asked about it | A rule nobody enforced | A check in the documents of both projects: every fact MoEYS uses is either kept by MoEYS or read from the body that keeps it, and never asked again |

## What the manager takes from it

None of the three losses is a programming error. Each is a decision about the service that was never made, never written into the right document, or never checked. The method's twelve documents (subtopic 1.4) exist to close these three places: an open question gets a name, an owner and a date; an answer is written in the document that owns it; and a rule is checked by a person or a program before the service is built.

**What to look for in your own project.** Take one complaint from the people your service serves. Ask which of the three places it came from. If you cannot tell, the documents of your project do not yet show who decided what.
