# E1.4 — The twelve documents, filled for PHEQA's registration of institutions

**Subtopic:** 1.4 — Twelve documents from the request to the running service

**Public anchor:** None; the content is the SDD method's own: the twelve documents and the handovers between them

**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name, date and figure in it is invented.

**What it shows.** The table of the twelve documents, filled for Progressa's registration of institutions: for each document, who in PHEQA or its supplier writes it, what it takes in, what it gives out, where a person decides and how it is checked, with the points where the manager stands marked.

**Form of this example.** The columns and the order of the twelve documents are the team's blank instrument and name no country. The rows are filled for Progressa.

## Before the twelve

Progressa's catalogue of higher-education services (subtopic 2.1) is written once for the whole sector, before the first of the twelve. It names the registration of institutions as a service PHEQA owes and the blocks it rests on. It is not one of the twelve.

## The twelve, filled

★ marks a point where the manager, the Registrar of PHEQA, stands: a document she or a person she names must accept before the next is begun.

| # | Document | Who writes it for PHEQA | What goes in | What comes out | Where a person decides | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PHEQA's own documents | PHEQA | — | Progressa's Higher Education Regulations, PHEQA's mandate, the registration desk's written requests, kept as received and never edited | ★ The Registrar, by what she hands over | Everything after is measured against them |
| 2 | The register of requirements | An AI assistant extracts it; the supplier's analyst rules on each entry | 1 | One entry for each separate thing PHEQA asked, in the regulation's words, with the article it comes from | ★ The Registrar accepts the register | The register's own checklist; no program |
| 3 | The records, with their glossary and rules | The supplier's analyst | 1, and the shared groundwork | The records PHEQA keeps (an institution, an application, a licence, a decision), what each means, and whose each fact is | ★ The read-back to the head of the registration desk | Its own checklist; no program |
| 4 | The list of goals | One named architect of the supplier | 2 and 3 | Every goal named (apply for a provisional licence, record a fee, record an inspection), each tied to the entries it serves | The list's owner at the review | Goals against entries, in both directions |
| 5 | The architecture | The supplier's solution architect | 2, 3 and 4 | PHEQA's application on its own installation; the sign-in through PNIA; MoEYS reading the register across Linkup; the fee through the Payments block | ★ The head of the ICT unit, with the bodies on the other side of each crossing | Its own checklist; no program |

## What the table shows a manager

There are nine ★ marks. At each one, somebody in PHEQA must say yes before the work goes on, and the next document is written from what was accepted, not from what was proposed. Two documents (9 and 12) are written by nobody: a program produces them, and nobody edits them by hand. Five documents (2, 3, 5, 6 and 7) are checked by a person against their own checklist; the others are checked against an earlier document or by a program.

**What to look for in your own project.** Lay your project's documents beside the twelve. A missing row is a decision nobody will take in writing. A row with no ★ where this table has one is a place where the supplier, not your organisation, decides.
