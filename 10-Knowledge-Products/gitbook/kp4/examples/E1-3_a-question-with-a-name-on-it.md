# E1.3 — The appeal period: a guess, and a named question

**Subtopic:** 1.3 — A question with a name on it is part of the specification

**Public anchor:** None; the content is the SDD method's own: the test of good enough, and gaps that are named, owned and counted

**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name, date and figure in it is invented.

**What it shows.** Two versions of the same line of PHEQA's specification: in the first, the period within which an institution may appeal is a figure nobody gave; in the second, it is a named question with an owner and a date, counted among the document's open questions.

**Form of this example.** The test of good enough is the method's and names no country. The line, the figure and the date are invented for Progressa.

## The first version: an answer by guess

The supplier's analyst was writing the goal in which an institution appeals against a decision of PHEQA. Progressa's Higher Education Regulations give the right to appeal, but the text the analyst had did not say within how long. The analyst needed a number to write the screens, so the draft read:

> "An institution may appeal within 30 days of the date of PHEQA's decision. After 30 days the self-service no longer offers the appeal."

Nobody gave the figure of 30 days. It looks like a fact. It will be built as a fact: the self-service will refuse an appeal on day 31. If the true period is longer, an institution with a lawful appeal will be turned away by the system, and nobody will know why the system says 30.

## The second version: a named question

The same line, written under the method:

> "An institution may appeal within the appeal period of the date of PHEQA's decision. **The appeal period: to be confirmed by the Registrar of PHEQA by 15 November 2026.** Open question 7 of 9 of this document."

And in the document's list of open questions:

| No. | The question | Why it matters | Owner | Answer needed by | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Within how many days of PHEQA's decision may an institution appeal? | The self-service closes the appeal when the period ends; a wrong figure turns away a lawful appeal | The Registrar of PHEQA | 15 November 2026 | Open |

The second version is not weaker than the first. It is more complete: it says exactly what is not yet known, who will settle it and when. The builder can build everything else and leave the one figure to a setting that the Registrar fills in.

## The manager's three questions before sign-off

| Question | Asked of the appeal goal | What a good answer looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Can someone build from it without asking anything? | Could the supplier's builder make the appeal screens from this document alone? | Yes, except for the named open questions, each of which has an owner |
| Was the review held? | Did the owner of the goal, the builder and the head of the registration desk go through it together? | A dated record of the review, with every open line given a name (subtopic 3.6) |
| Is every remaining gap named, owned and counted? | Does the document say how many questions are open, and who owns each? | "Open question 7 of 9", with a table that lists all nine |

## What the manager takes from it

An open question with an owner and a date is acceptable in a document you sign. A question answered by a guess is a fault, even when the guess turns out right, because nobody can tell it from a fact. When you read a draft, look for figures, periods, roles and rules that have no source beside them, and ask where each came from.
