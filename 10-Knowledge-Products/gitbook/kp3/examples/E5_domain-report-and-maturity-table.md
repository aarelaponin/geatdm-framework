# E5 — The Digital Data domain report and Progressa's maturity table

**Method step:** 5 of 9 — Score each domain and write the domain reports

**Public anchor:** PAERA version 1.0 (GovStack, 2024), section 5.3, for what is assessed; the UNDP Digital Development Compass, for the five stages and the score range of 0 to 5

**Simulated case:** This is a simulated case for Progressa, a fictional country; every institution, name and figure in it is invented.

**Form of this example.** The scoring rule and the report's section template are the team's method. The domain report and the maturity table are rebuilt for Progressa. One domain report, Digital Data, is written in full; the other four follow the same template and are shown here only through their scores and findings.

## The scoring rule

The scale is the five stages of the UNDP Digital Development Compass, under the Compass's own names: Basic, Opportunistic, Systematic, Differentiating and Transformational. The Compass places scores on a range from 0 to 5, one unit to each stage, and gives no zero scores. The way the team applies that scale to PAERA's five domains is the team's own:

1. Each sub-component (E1) has assessment criteria written at three reference levels: the lowest stage (Basic), the middle stage (Systematic) and the highest stage (Transformational).
2. The facilitator places each sub-component at one of the five stages, using only verified positions from step 4. A sub-component that meets part of the Systematic criterion is Opportunistic; one that meets part of the Transformational criterion is Differentiating.
3. Each stage scores the middle of its unit: Basic 0.5, Opportunistic 1.5, Systematic 2.5, Differentiating 3.5, Transformational 4.5.
4. A domain's score is the mean of its sub-components' scores, to one decimal place. Its stage is the unit the score falls in: below 1.0 Basic, from 1.0 Opportunistic, from 2.0 Systematic, from 3.0 Differentiating, from 4.0 Transformational.

**The criteria for one sub-component, D2 State registers:**

| Reference level | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Basic | The registers a sector needs are kept on paper or in separate files; no law names their keepers |
| Systematic | The main registers are held in systems, each with a keeper named in law; some are shared online with other bodies |
| Transformational | Every main register is digital, kept under law, keyed to shared identifiers and reused by every service that needs it |

## Part A — The Digital Data domain report (in full)

*Illustrative: every stage, score and finding in this report is invented for Progressa.*

### 1. How the domain was assessed

**Framework.** PAERA's Digital Data pillar (section 3.4.2), divided into the six sub-components of E1, scored by the rule above.

**Data sources.** Three: the automatic desk assessment of step 2 (E2); the questionnaire answers of the MoEYS statistics unit and PNEA, verified in sessions V-01 and V-04 (E3, E4); and a reading of the answers against the criteria of each sub-component.

**Scope.** The registers and data that education services depend on, as the frame page of E1 sets them. Not in scope: the internal data of universities and private schools.

### 2. The sub-components (illustrative scores)

**D1 Data governance and policy — Opportunistic (1.5).** *What exists:* the education act names PEMIS as the source of education statistics, and the statistics unit publishes a yearly yearbook. *What is missing:* no policy says who owns each education register, who may use its data, or how long records are kept.

**D2 State registers — Opportunistic (1.5).** *What exists:* the national identity register is a working system with a service on Linkup; PLR, a member of Linkup, holds enrolment records for some learners in a system; PNEA keeps examination candidates in a system. *What is missing:* PLR is not yet the authoritative learner register, because no law gives it a basis, it holds no record for most learners, and it has no quality rules and no link to the national identity; primary schools keep class registers on paper; school and teacher records are spreadsheets or payroll extracts.

**D3 Data quality and master data — Basic (0.5).** *What exists:* nothing that works as master data. *What is missing:* there is no common school identifier, so PEMIS, PNEA and the school records use different codes for about one school in ten; no data-quality rules; no named data steward; changes to PEMIS totals are not logged.

**D4 Data sharing and reuse — Opportunistic (1.5).** *What exists:* PNIA and PLR each offer one service over Linkup, which PNEA alone may call. *What is missing:* MoEYS and the schools share data with one another and with PNEA only by spreadsheet and file export; nothing in education is entered once and reused.

**D5 Data protection in practice — Opportunistic (1.5).** *What exists:* the data protection act of 2019 applies to all public bodies. *What is missing:* the act has no rule for children's data in public registers, and no education body has carried out a data protection impact assessment.

**D6 Statistics and analysis — Opportunistic (1.5).** *What exists:* PEMIS produces totals by district, grade and sex every year. *What is missing:* no learner-level data, so the ministry cannot plan by school, follow a learner from one grade to the next, or see who drops out.

**Domain score:** (1.5 + 1.5 + 0.5 + 1.5 + 1.5 + 1.5) ÷ 6 = 1.3, **Opportunistic**.

### 3. Development priorities for Digital Data

1. PLR made the one authoritative learner register, linked to the national identity and kept under law.
2. One school identifier used by PEMIS, PNEA and the school records.
3. Data-quality rules and a named data steward for each education register.
4. Learner-level data in PEMIS, drawn from the learner register instead of school returns.

### 4. Findings carried to the gap register

Digital Data contributes findings F-DAT-1 to F-DAT-4 to the findings register in Part B.

| IDN | 2.3 | Systematic | PNIA's sign-in for people, running since 2024 | Linking learners to the national identity, including children below the age of issue |

The scores above are the validated scores. In the draft, Interoperability stood at 2.3, because sub-component I2, membership of the exchange, had been placed at Systematic from the provisional stage of desk result AF-INT-01, which describes the exchange as a whole. That broke rule 2 of the scoring rule: session V-02 of step 4 had already verified that MoEYS is not a member of Linkup (E4), and that verified position places I2 at Opportunistic. PDGA pointed this out in the validation round (comment CM-02 in E9). I2 was lowered to 1.5 and the domain score to 2.1; its stage did not change. The correction is kept here because it shows why rule 2 exists: a desk result tells the team where to look, and only the verified position is scored.
