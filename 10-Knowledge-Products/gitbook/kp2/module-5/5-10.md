> **Single message.** The same four-layer framework stands up interoperability beyond education — the method is sector-portable, and the second sector is cheaper than the first.

## The concept

Everything in this knowledge product was demonstrated on education — Progressa's schools, learners and credentials. But the framework is not education-specific, and now that it runs, the most important thing a Strategist can know is exactly which parts carry to the next sector unchanged and which parts are new. Get that split right, and the second sector pays only for its own vocabularies and services.

Split the framework in two. What carries unchanged to health, or agriculture, or social protection: the four-layer model, the decree pattern, the governance — the tiers, the RACI, the Operating Authority — the standards portfolio, and the bus itself, already built and running. What is genuinely new per sector: the semantic layer, because health speaks a different vocabulary than education, and the specific exchanges and services that sector needs. That — the vocabularies and the services — is the whole of the difference. And a word on order. This knowledge product demonstrated on education because the worked case was to hand; most countries sequence the framework the other way round. The first wave is usually tax, civil registration, the business register and health — high-volume, foundational reference data that every other sector reads — with education, justice, social protection and customs in the second wave. Whichever sector goes first pays for the platform; the rule that the next is cheaper holds either way.

And this is the re-use argument at the scale of the whole framework. The first sector pays to build the bus, the governance and the legal mandate; the second sector reuses all of it and pays only for its own semantics and services; the third reuses more still. This is exactly the whole-of-government planning logic from the very first module, now visible across sectors: build the shared platform once, and every sector after consumes it. The second sector is cheaper than the first, and the third cheaper than the second — and only a framework built deliberately, for the whole of government, makes that compounding possible. A set of separate sector projects never gets cheaper; a planned framework does.

Portability is built in on purpose, and it is not a footnote — it is the reason an interoperability framework is worth its cost. A platform that served only education would be hard to justify; a platform that serves education first and then every other sector, each paying only for its own vocabularies and services, is the investment a strategist can defend for a decade. So when you take the next sector to your minister, bring the portability map: here is what we reuse, which is most of it, and here is the small, sector-specific part we build new. That map gets cheaper to make every time, and it is the business case for the next sector.

**Reused unchanged**

- The four-layer model: legal, organisational, semantic and technical questions are the same in every sector.
- The decree pattern and the governance: tiers, RACI, Operating Authority. None of it names a sector.
- The bus: Linkup, its trust anchor and PNIA's `identity-api`, already published.
- The standards portfolio's technical rows (X-Road, OpenAPI, TLS). CEDS and OneRoster are education-specific and do not carry.

**New for health**

- Vocabularies: HL7 FHIR R4 for patients, encounters and facilities; ICD-11 for diagnoses; LOINC for laboratory results. *[confirm versions]*
- Exchanges: patient identity from PNIA (a new grant and lawful basis, not a new service); a facility registry; birth notifications to civil registration; consent records.

**Business case for the minister:** "Health reuses the bus, the law and the governance we have already paid for in education. It pays only for its own vocabularies, three new services and one new permission on an existing one. So health costs a fraction of what the first sector cost."

**Wave:** health usually belongs in the first wave: foundational, high-volume reference data that other sectors read. In Progressa it comes second only because education went first. It should be the first sector added after education.

{% tab title="Reading the output" %}
This is the teaching part of the play. Work through the output the way a chief architect would — what is earned, what is invented, what is missing.

<details>

<summary>1. Reusing the identity service is earned</summary>

PNIA's `identity-api` is already on the bus, with a contract and an access list. Health needs a new grant and its own lawful basis, not a new service. That is the clearest proof in the map that the second sector is cheaper.

</details>

<details>

<summary>2. One "unchanged" claim is too broad, and the cost has no figure</summary>

The run caught the education vocabularies in the portfolio, which is the leak the safeguard warns about. But it still lists the decree pattern as unchanged. Health data is more sensitive, much of it is about children, and the Data Protection Act asks for a legal basis to share a minor's data. Health will likely need its own article. "A fraction of the cost" also has no figure behind it. Add the cost of the three new health services from your implementation plan before it reaches a minister.

</details>

<details>

<summary>3. The linking key does not carry</summary>

Education links records by NIN. In health, newborns and children under 16 have no National ID, and the patient index uses its own identifier. Matching patients is a new semantic problem, not a reused one. Settle it with the Ministry of Health's data owners and the Civil Registration Department before anyone promises the platform carries over.

</details>

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Safeguard for this play.** The 'reused unchanged' list is only true if the platform was built sector-neutrally — confirm that none of your education-sector specifics leaked into the bus, governance or standards before promising they carry over. And validate the new sector's vocabularies with that sector's data owners; assuming health maps like education is exactly the semantic error this framework exists to prevent.
