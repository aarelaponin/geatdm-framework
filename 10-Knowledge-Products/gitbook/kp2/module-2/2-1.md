> **Single message.** Without the legal layer, the bus cannot lawfully carry one cross-agency message — the decree is the on-switch, not paperwork.

## The concept

Of the four layers of interoperability, the one most often left for last is the legal layer — and it is the one that decides whether anything actually moves. You can build a perfect technical bus, agree the meaning of the data, and sign every organisational arrangement, and still carry nothing, because no agency will share data without a clear lawful basis to do so. A working bus with no legal mandate is not a working exchange. It is a standing risk that the most cautious official in any agency can stop, and be right to stop.

Without a mandate, you see one of three things. An official refuses to share, citing the data-protection law, and no one can overrule them. A project stalls for months waiting for a legal opinion that covers only that one exchange. Or — worst of all — data moves anyway, on an unclear basis, and becomes a breach waiting to surface. Each of these is a legal-layer failure wearing a technical or political disguise.

A single legal instrument fixes all three. The decree authorises cross-agency exchange — it makes the sharing lawful in the first place. It makes connecting to the bus the default rather than something each agency may decline. It protects the citizen's data by binding every exchange to a stated purpose, a basis for consent where needed, and security obligations. And it names the operating authority and the powers it holds to run the framework. With the decree in force, exchange is the rule and refusing to connect becomes the exception that has to be justified.

Here is the part that makes the decree a framework artefact and not a project document. The lawful basis is written once, for the whole of government, so that every agency reuses the same mandate instead of each negotiating its own legal opinion for every exchange. That reuse — write the rule once, apply it everywhere — exists only at the level of whole-of-government planning. A project cannot grant itself a mandate that binds other agencies. Only the framework, through the decree, can.

Country: Progressa. The bracket "paste any relevant instruments" was filled from A0 §7 and §10:

- **Data Protection Act 2023** (enacted; Data Protection Commission, 6 staff, no enforcement action yet). A legal basis is needed to share a minor's data; parental consent for any non-statutory use. - **National Identity Decree 2018.** Establishes PNIA and the National ID; public bodies may verify a person against the register. IDs are issued only at 16.
- **Civil Registration Act.** Birth registration is a child's legal identity anchor. Silent on electronic sharing.
- **Examinations Authority Act.** Candidate results are confidential to the candidate and MoEYS.
- **e-Government Interoperability Framework 2021.** Published, not applied, no legal force.
- No e-transactions act, no access-to-information act. No instrument establishes the Progressa Learner Registry (PLR).

Planned exchanges: (1) PNIA → PNEA person identity, to issue a certificate; (2) PLR → PNEA enrolment, to issue a certificate; (3) Civil Registration → PLR birth record, at enrolment; (4) PNEA → universities, exam results.

**Current-law assessment**

| Exchange | Status | Basis, block or silence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. PNIA → PNEA identity | Permitted | Identity Decree allows verification by public bodies; DPA "another law provides" |
| 2. PLR → PNEA enrolment | Unclear | PLR has no legal existence, so no body is lawfully the source |
| 3. Civil Registration → PLR birth record | Unclear | Civil Registration Act silent on electronic sharing; minors' data needs a legal basis |
| 4. PNEA → universities results | Blocked | Examinations Authority Act limits results to the candidate and MoEYS |

**What the decree must provide**

- **Mandate:** make cross-agency exchange over Linkup lawful for named exchanges and purposes.
- **Lawful basis:** a statutory basis for each catalogue exchange, so minors' data moves without consent for statutory uses.
- **Safeguards:** purpose limitation, a field list per exchange, logging, the DPA's rights preserved.
- **Mandatory connection:** PNIA, PLR and PNEA shall connect and publish their services.
- **Operating authority:** PDGA's powers to admit members, set standards and suspend access.

{% tab title="Reading the output" %}
This is the teaching part of the play. Work through the output the way a chief architect would — what is earned, what is invented, what is missing.

<details>

<summary>1. The PLR row is the real finding</summary>

The run did not mark exchange 2 as permitted just because it is the one the minister wants. A registry with no founding instrument cannot be the lawful source of anything. That points to a question for the Ministry of Justice: can a decree establish the PLR, or does it need its own act?

</details>

<details>

<summary>2. "Permitted" for PNIA rests on one line of summary</summary>

The run read "may verify a person" as permission to send PNEA a person's details. Verifying a match and returning fields are not the same act. This is exactly the "already permitted" finding the safeguard warns about. It also missed that many exam candidates are under 16 and hold no National ID, so for them the identity data comes from the birth record.

</details>

<details>

<summary>3. The Data Protection Act clause was never found</summary>

The whole table leans on "where another law provides for it", and the input admits that section was not found. Get the text of the Act before anything else. Then ask the Data Protection Commission how it reads that clause.

</details>
