## The concept

The first two components of the decree are the ones a non-lawyer can lead on, because they are about the case and the context rather than the binding rules. The Explanatory Memorandum makes the case for the decree. The Preamble sets out the legal authority and the recitals. Both can be drafted from a document you already have — the Strategic Foundation Document from Module 1 — and both are exactly the kind of focused, well-bounded drafting task an AI assistant does well, with you reviewing.

The Explanatory Memorandum is the case, written for the minister and the cabinet, not the lawyer. It states the problem the decree solves — fragmentation, the citizen asked the same thing many times. It says what the decree does, in plain language. It says why now — the cost of another year without a mandate. And it names the expected benefit. If you wrote a good Strategic Foundation Document in Module 1, the Memorandum is largely a translation of it into the form a Ministry of Justice expects. That is a task you can hand to Claude with the foundation document as input.

The Preamble is different. It sets out the recitals — the formal findings the decree rests on — and, critically, the legal authority: the constitutional or statutory power under which the decree is made. Here the AI is useful for structure and for a first draft of the recitals, but every citation of authority is a placeholder — a [confirm] — until a qualified lawyer verifies that the cited power actually exists and actually supports a decree of this kind in your country. An invented or wrong authority citation is the fastest way to have the whole decree sent back. So the Preamble is generated as a scaffold, then verified line by line.

This is the pattern for the whole Decree Drafting Kit, and it appears first here. The AI prompt for this video takes your Strategic Foundation Document and produces a Memorandum draft and a Preamble scaffold in minutes. That is the easy ninety per cent. The hard, essential ten per cent is yours: confirm every legal citation against a real statute, soften every claim the evidence does not support, and mark anything unverified as a [confirm] for the lawyer. Generate fast; confirm carefully. The AI accelerates the drafting; it does not get the law right for you.

**Explanatory Memorandum (shortened)**

Progressa registers the same learner three times, in lists that do not agree, and asks parents to prove a child's identity on paper at every counter. This decree makes it lawful for PNEA to obtain a learner's identity from PNIA and enrolment from the PLR over Linkup, and requires the three bodies to connect. The exchange layer is already running in pilot, so the remaining obstacle is legal. Each year about 180,000 certificates could be issued without paper proof. [evidence]

**Preamble scaffold**

- Having regard to [confirm: the constitutional or statutory power of the issuing authority to make a decree on public administration];
- Having regard to the Data Protection Act 2023, in particular [confirm: the provision allowing sharing where another law provides];
- Having regard to [confirm: the instrument establishing PNIA];
- Whereas the Education Sector Plan 2023–2028 calls for a single learner registry;
- Whereas citizens are asked repeatedly for data the state already holds;
- [confirm: the enacting formula used for decrees in Progressa] —

**Items a lawyer must resolve**

1. Power to issue the decree.
2. The Data Protection Act's sharing provision.
3. PNIA's founding instrument.
4. The enacting formula.
5. Whether the PLR can be referred to before it legally exists.

{% tab title="Reading the output" %}
This is the teaching part of the play. Work through the output the way a chief architect would — what is earned, what is invented, what is missing.

<details>

<summary>1. The problem statement is earned</summary>

Both sentences of the problem come from the foundation document, almost word for word. A minister can check them against what they already believe. That is the part of the Memorandum you can keep.

</details>

<details>

<summary>2. The 180,000 figure is invented</summary>

Nothing in the input gives a number of certificates. The run flagged it, which is right, but a flagged invented number still tends to survive into the final draft. Delete it, or replace it with PNEA's own count.

</details>

<details>

<summary>3. "The remaining obstacle is legal" overstates</summary>

The PLR is planned, not started. A minister who reads this will expect the exchange to run the day the decree is signed. Say that the decree is one of two conditions, the other being a working PLR.

</details>

<details>

<summary>4. The data-protection reader is missing</summary>

The Memorandum never mentions minors' data. The Data Protection Commission will read for that first. Add one sentence on the statutory basis and the safeguards, so the case answers its first question.

</details>

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Safeguard for this play.** Never let a [confirm] placeholder reach the Ministry of Justice as if it were a real citation. The Memorandum may be near-final; the Preamble is a scaffold whose every legal authority must be supplied and verified by a qualified lawyer in the jurisdiction.
