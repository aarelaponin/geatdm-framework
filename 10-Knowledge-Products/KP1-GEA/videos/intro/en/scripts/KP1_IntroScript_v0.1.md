## Compliance note — read before filming

This video is a **deliberate exception to rule 4.3 of the module bundles, "No individuals on screen".** The rule allows only an AI avatar or a screen-only voice-over. A named person on camera breaks it, intentionally and once, at the entrance to the course; every one of the thirty-five videos after it stays without a person on screen, so the rule holds for all of the teaching content.

The video does **not** break the rule that each video stands alone with no introduction inside it: the introduction is a video of its own, and 1.1 to 5.6 remain self-contained and can each be found and watched alone.

**It belongs among the course's open calibration items**, beside item 5.4(b) of the Module 1 bundle (whether a short introductory entry heads the playlist). If the exception is declined, **the same words are recorded as a voice-over on a text-only title slide** (ITU template, background #E5F5FB, Arial), and nothing else in this script changes: no line depends on the face.

## Script

> _Frame: the presenter on camera, medium close-up, plain background, eyes to the lens. No slide. Two seconds of silence before the first word. Lower third at 0:02–0:07 — 'Aare Lapõnin · FiscalAdmin OÜ' (Arial 18pt, white on #003D5B, lower left)._

> _Position 1 — the situation the viewer recognises._

In many governments, each programme builds its own systems. The country pays several times for one thing, and citizens repeat themselves at every counter.

> _Position 2 — nobody is failing. Beat before it; say it plainly, not as a concession._

Nobody is failing. Each ministry follows its own law, budget and deadline. Inside one project, building separately is quicker, and reasonable.

> _Position 3 — the plan for the whole government, and the shared language. This is the turn of the video; slow down here._

That changes when someone sees the whole government at once. That view is a plan your government commissions: an enterprise architecture, one agreed picture of how it works. Then re-use is the sensible choice, and policy and technical people decide together, in one language.

> _Position 4 — not from nothing, and still in charge. The whole position is cut in the one-minute version._

⟦You do not start from nothing. What other governments have learned is written down, public and free to use. The main reference is PAERA, a published model of how a public administration can organise this work, and in what order. It comes from GovStack, an international initiative that describes shared components any government can use. None of it is imposed. Your government adapts it to its own laws and keeps every decision.⟧

> _Beat. Position 5 — who the course is for, and what each will be able to do._

This course serves two people. No technical background is needed. ⟦This kind of planning is rarely taught, and a small team is enough.⟧ If you brief a minister, you learn to make the case and what to ask for. If you do the planning, you learn to build the plan around shared components.

> _Position 6 — where to begin. In the long version the exercise comes first._

⟦Each video ends with an exercise. You give an AI assistant a prepared instruction and material from your own country, and you get a first draft to correct.⟧ Five modules, thirty-five videos, each standing alone. Briefing a minister? Start with 1.1. Doing the planning? Start with 2.1.

> _Hold two seconds after the last word. Do not smile out. Cut on the still frame._

### The one-minute cut

Drop the three ⟦bracketed⟧ passages and nothing else. What is left is 148 spoken words, about 1 min 05 sec, with every sentence whole. The cut removes position 4 entirely, and from positions 5 and 6 only the two optional half-sentences the message analysis names: the sentence that planning is rarely taught and a small team is enough, and the sentence on the exercise. Positions 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 survive as written.

Film the long version even if the short one is published; the bracketed passages can be cut in the edit, but not added.

## Why the script reads as it does

**The order.** It follows the accepted message analysis, section 7.1: the situation, nobody failing, the plan and the shared language, not from nothing and still in charge, who it is for and what each can do, where to begin. It does not follow the earlier introduction's order (who I am, the question, who it is for, the exercise, what you will be able to do, where to begin). The presenter is named by the lower third, not by a spoken self-introduction, so that the first words a viewer hears are about the viewer's own work.

**The reason for separate building is never a fault.** Position 2 says that a ministry follows its own law, budget and deadline, and that building separately is the reasonable choice inside one project. Position 3 says what changes that choice: a view of the whole government. Nothing in the script says that officials lack incentives, prefer the easy path, or lack skills.

**The two names are said once each, and only where they can be cut.** PAERA and GovStack appear only in position 4, each with its meaning in plain words. The authorship of PAERA belongs in the written description under the video.

**No speed, no figures.** The script promises nothing about time or money beyond what the sources allow; the only numbers spoken are the counts of modules and videos and the two video numbers at the end.

## Delivery notes

- **Register.** A senior adviser speaking to a colleague: collegial, unhurried, certain. Not a presenter and not a pitch. If it sounds like selling the course, it is one notch too bright.
- **Pace.** About 135 words a minute, slower than natural speech. The beats marked in the cues buy the time; take them rather than slowing every word.
- **Position 2 is the line that keeps the viewer.** "Nobody is failing" is said as a plain fact, not as a kindness. The viewer must hear that the presenter knows how a ministry works.
- **Position 3 is the turn.** Slow down at "That changes". "Enterprise architecture" is the one technical term the video uses; its meaning follows it at once, so do not pause between them.
- **The two names.** "PAERA" and "GovStack" come in position 4 only, each followed at once by what it is; if that position is cut, neither is heard. Say each the way the course's narrated videos say it, so the series sounds as one.
- **"1.1" and "2.1".** Say "one point one" and "two point one", not "video one dot one". They are the last thing heard, and they are instructions.
- **Direct address.** "Your government", "you" — never "one might". If a line drifts away from the viewer, it is the wrong line.
- **Not on camera.** No organisation that commissioned the course, no contract number, no named AI product ("an AI assistant"), no figure promising speed or saving. Attribution belongs in the description and on the end card, where it can be corrected without filming again.
