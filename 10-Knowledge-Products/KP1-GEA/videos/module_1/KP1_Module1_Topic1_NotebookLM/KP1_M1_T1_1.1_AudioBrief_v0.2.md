# AUDIO BRIEF — KP1 · Module 1 · Topic 1 · Video 1.1
## "Why your country needs a national EA"

**This document is the sole authority for the audio.** Everything the hosts say must come from
this file. Do not add examples, statistics, institutions, countries, analogies, or closing
questions that do not appear below.

---

## 0. Production spec

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | Two hosts: **Host A** (interviewer) and **Host B** (subject-matter expert) |
| Total runtime | **4 minutes 00 seconds (±10s). Hard ceiling 4:15.** |
| Audience | Government Chief Digital Officers, Directors-General, sector ministers, and their senior advisers — in low- and middle-income countries, many working in English as a second or third language |
| Register | Expert policy briefing. Collegial but professional — two senior advisers preparing a minister, not two podcasters reacting to news |
| Companion deck | 6 slides; audio must align to the slide budget in §2 |

---

## 1. Framing rule — the single most important instruction

**The listener is the government official, not the frustrated citizen.**

Address the listener as the person who *runs* these systems and can *fix* them. Use
"in your ministry", "your programme", "your vendor", "your minister".

- ❌ Do **not** open with, or return to, the listener standing at a counter filling in a form.
- ❌ Do **not** use "we've all been there" / consumer-complaint framing.
- ✅ The citizen appears only as the person **your** systems burden — third person: "the citizen
  who gave her ID number to the health ministry gives it again, on paper, at the school."

---

## 2. Slide-by-slide time budget and content

Cover the slides **in order**. Finish each slide's content before moving to the next. End each
slide's segment on a complete sentence — do not let a thought straddle a slide boundary.

### Slide 1 — Title card · 0:00–0:15 (15 s)

Cold open, no music bed, no "welcome to the show". Host A states the topic in one or two
sentences, in this spirit:

> "Module 1, video 1.1 — why your country needs a national enterprise architecture. Without a
> shared plan for your government's digital systems, every new programme rebuilds what others
> have already built."

Host B adds one sentence naming the stake: the country pays, the citizen pays, and the minister
cannot deliver what they promised. Then move on. **No preamble about the sources.**

### Slide 2 — The pattern · 0:15–1:00 (45 s)

Content, all of it, nothing more:

- One programme builds a system to register citizens. A second programme builds another system
  to register the same citizens for a different service. A third builds a third.
- Each takes years. Each is funded separately — often by a different donor: the World Bank, the
  African Development Bank, a bilateral partner.
- None of them work together.
- **The key insight, stated plainly by Host B:** you cannot fix this inside any one programme.
  Each programme is doing exactly what it was funded to do. The systems are not broken — they
  are behaving exactly as they were designed and financed to behave.

Host A may ask **one** short clarifying question here. Not more.

### Slide 3 — Four signs your government has no shared plan · 1:00–2:20 (80 s)

Host A opens with the retrieval question, then a one-beat pause:

> "Before we go through them — how many separate registers of the same citizens does your
> government keep? Hold that number."

Then **number the signs out loud — "sign one", "sign two", "sign three", "sign four"** — roughly
20 seconds each, in this order:

1. **More than one register of the same people.** The school list has one. The national ID
   register has another. The social register has a third. None of them agree.
2. **Every system-to-system link is built from scratch.** Last year your team connected the tax
   office to the business register. This year the same work again, for the health ministry.
   Built again, from zero.
3. **Vendor lock-in on systems built years ago.** One vendor built it ten years ago. To change
   anything you call that vendor and pay what they ask. Nobody else knows how it works. The
   vendor knows this, and prices accordingly.
4. **Ministries that do not connect across each other.** The citizen who gave her ID number to
   the health ministry gives it again, on paper, when she enrols her child in school. The
   **once-only principle** exists on paper. In practice it is impossible.

### Slide 4 — The country pays in four directions at once · 2:20–3:00 (40 s)

Name all four, in this order, ~10 seconds each:

- **Money** — the same systems built many times over.
- **Time** — every new programme waits for cross-system work nobody planned for.
- **Citizens** — the same form, filled in five places.
- **Your minister** — flagship cross-ministry programmes cannot land, because the systems will
  not talk to each other.

**Land the fourth one hardest.** It is the cost that moves this audience. Host B should slow
down and let it sit for a beat before the next slide.

### Slide 5 — One root cause: there is no shared plan · 3:00–3:50 (50 s)

This is the most important slide. Give it room; do not rush it.

- All four signs come from one root cause: **there is no shared plan.**
- No one has written down what your government's digital systems are, who owns them, and how
  they should fit together.
- **A national Enterprise Architecture is that plan.**
- One permitted analogy, used once and briefly: you cannot rewire a building you have never
  mapped. Do not extend it into a running joke.
- **Close on the series handoff, spoken by Host B:** the rest of this knowledge product shows
  you how to commission a national EA, what it will deliver, and what you will need from your
  minister to make it work.

### Slide 6 — Sources · 3:50–4:00 (10 s)

One line only, then stop: "Sources are in the video description." **Do not read URLs. Do not
name section numbers. Do not summarise the sources.**

## 3. Hard prohibitions

The following all appeared in the previous draft and must not recur.

- ❌ **No podcast outro.** No "that raises a fascinating question for you to consider", no "look
  around at the other institutions in your life", no invitation to reflect on hospitals or
  university campuses. The audio ends on the series handoff in §2, Slide 5, plus the one-line
  sources note.
- ❌ **No "deep dive" self-reference.** Never say "deep dive", "today we're unpacking", "welcome
  to", "our sources", "the sources say", "here's where it gets interesting".
- ❌ **No invented specifics.** No maternal-health registry, no named country, no cost figures,
  no percentages, no dates other than the ones in §2 and §4.
- ❌ **No filler.** Remove "you know", "like", "I mean", "basically", "totally", "sort of",
  "right?", "wow", "oh absolutely", "man". Reaction interjections should be rare and short.
- ❌ **No consumer-outrage vocabulary.** Not "broken", "chaos", "held hostage", "extortionate",
  "hostage", "nightmare". Say: fragmented, duplicated, costly to change, locked in.
- ❌ **No idioms or metaphors beyond the one permitted in Slide 5.** Drop the plumbing /
  contractors / server-room / tangled-wires imagery. Much of this audience listens in English as
  an additional language and the audio is subtitled and translated.
- ❌ **No crosstalk or interruption.** One speaker finishes, the other begins. The audio is cut
  to slide transitions, so overlapping speech breaks the edit.

## 4. Terminology and pronunciation — say these exactly

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| the **PAERA** framework — the Public Administration Ecosystem Reference Architecture (spell "P-A-E-R-A" the first time only) | "the PRA framework", "Paira" |
| the **European Interoperability Framework** | "the EU-European Interoperability Framework" |
| the **once-only principle** | "the ask-once principle" |
| **national Enterprise Architecture**; abbreviate to **"EA"** only after saying it in full once | "an EA" on first use |
| **register** (a list of people or entities) | "registry" — use *register*, consistently |
| **six months to a first roadmap** | any other duration |
| **four sign-offs** | any other number |

Named sources may be mentioned **once each, in passing, in Slide 2 or 5 only** — never as a
recurring "according to the sources".

---

## 5. Definition of done

The audio is correct when:

1. Total runtime is 3:50–4:10.
2. A listener can tell, without seeing the slides, where each of the six slides begins.
3. The four signs are numbered aloud and appear in the deck's order.
4. The four costs are named aloud and appear in the deck's order, minister last.
5. Nothing is said that is not in this brief.
6. The final words are the series handoff plus the one-line sources note.
