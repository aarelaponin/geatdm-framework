**Description:**

```text
An EA is the picture everyone agrees describes your government — minister,
ministry CIO, donor, vendor. With it, you can lead the conversation. Without it,

KP1 · Module 1 · 1.2 — What an EA actually is, in one breath

An Enterprise Architecture isn't software and isn't a vendor product — it's a

Application, Technology), and why writing them down turns a list of projects into
```

### 1.3 — Why projects can't do this themselves

**File:** `video/KP1_M1_1.3_Video_v0.10.mp4` · **Subtitles:** `audio/KP1_M1_1.3_Audio_v0.10.srt`

**Title:** Why digital procurement rules don't fix fragmentation — and what an EA actually does

**Description:**

```text
Procurement rules can require interoperability. They cannot deliver it. Only
planning at the level of the whole government, supported by reference
architectures, can.

KP1 · Module 1 · 1.3 — Why projects can't do this themselves

Procurement rules can require open APIs. Strategies can mandate interoperability.
But projects, optimising rationally for their own delivery, will not re-use across
ministries — and won't deliver sustainability or complexity reduction. Only an EA,
anchored on a reference architecture, brings those properties to the front.
```

### 1.5 — Why PAERA-anchored

**File:** `video/KP1_M1_1.5_Video_v0.4.mp4` · **Subtitles:** `audio/KP1_M1_1.5_Audio_v0.4.srt`

**Title:** Why anchor your national EA on PAERA? Five things you don't have to build from scratch

**Description:**

```text
PAERA gives your team five years of head start. Adopt it, and the architecture
work begins on day one. Do not adopt it, and your first year is spent inventing
what others have already published.

KP1 · Module 1 · 1.5 — Why PAERA-anchored: the head start you do not pay for twice

Designing a country-specific EA framework from scratch takes 12 to 18 months
before any architecture work begins. Anchoring on PAERA — the Public

metamodel, a pattern library, principles and a methodology on day one.
Sector-portable, and connected to GovStack.
```
