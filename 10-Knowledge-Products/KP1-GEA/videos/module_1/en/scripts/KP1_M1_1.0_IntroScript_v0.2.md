## ⚠ Compliance note — read before filming

This video is a **deliberate exception to two rules** in the bundle, and ITU should be told rather than left to notice:

1. **§4.3 "No individuals on screen."** The Guide §3 Note allows only an AI avatar or a screen-only voice-over. A named human on camera breaks that — intentionally, once, at the front of the course. The argument for the exception: a knowledge product commissioned from a named contractor benefits from a named human sponsor at the entrance; everything after 1.0 stays faceless, so the rule holds across 100% of the substantive content.
2. **§3.i "Each video stands alone — no in-video introduction."** Honoured, not broken: the intro is its own video, so 1.1–1.8 remain self-contained and individually discoverable. This is exactly the option left open in **§5.4(b)** — *"whether to include a Topic 1 'trailer' subtopic (1.0, ~60 sec) as a playlist entry."*

**Add to §5.4 as a calibration item before the Tuesday call.** If ITU declines the exception, the same script re-records as voice-over on a text-only title slide with no other change — the words don't depend on the face.

> **Note on §5.4(b) sizing.** That item says *~60 sec*. This draft runs ~1:55. Either the calibration item moves to "~2 min," or you take the 60-second cut below. Don't let the bundle and the video disagree in front of ITU.

## Runtime — what changed and why

v0.1 was 136 words / ~58 sec. Your three notes each asked for more: the audience line to include non-Strategists, the play to be explained rather than named, and the four-things line to be understandable to someone who has not read the bundle. Those are all *good* asks and none of them can be met in the words v0.1 had. The result is 272 words / **~1:55**.

That is a real trade, so make it deliberately:

- **Keep ~1:55** if the intro's job is to make someone commit to nine videos. It now earns that — it explains the play, which is the actual differentiator of this series, and it says what the viewer will be able to *do*.
- **Take the 60-second cut** (below) if the intro's job is to get out of the way. The deletions are marked so you can film the long version and cut back later — record it once, cut it twice.

## Notes on your three edits

**The audience line.** The inclusion is aimed at a specific person, not at "everyone" — Modules 2–4 of this same knowledge product are Architect-facing, so the architects and advisers watching 1.0 are not strangers wandering in, they are the audience for the next three modules. "The same words in the same room" is a deliberate echo of 1.4's *"business and IT in the same room, using the same words"* — the intro plants the phrase, 1.4 pays it off.

**The play.** Expanded to *what it is, what you feed it, who runs it, what comes back.* Two choices worth your sign-off:

- **"An AI assistant," not "Claude."** Your open calibration item on tool-neutral phrasing applies here more than anywhere — a spoken tool name in the first minute of the course dates the whole series the moment ITU switches vendors. The GitBook play pages can name a tool; the video shouldn't.
- **"You do not watch me run it. You run it."** This is your own objection to demo-the-tooltip videos, said out loud to the viewer, in the first minute. It sets the expectation that this series asks something of them.

**The four things.** v0.1 listed the *artefacts* — "the case, the definition, the lifecycle, the four asks." That is the table of contents as an author sees it, and it is meaningless to someone who has not read the bundle. v0.2 lists what the viewer will be able to **do**, in their own words, in the order the videos deliver it. Note what is deliberately *not* said: the four asks are named but not enumerated, because enumerating them is 1.7's job and the intro should create the question, not answer it. The ~2% budget figure stays out for the same reason plus one more — §5.1 lists it as an unverified claim.

## Delivery notes

- **Register.** The same one the series is voiced in: *a senior adviser briefing a minister — collegial, unhurried, certain.* Not a presenter, not a pitch. If you sound like you are selling the course, you are one notch too bright.
- **Pace.** 272 words over ~1:55 is about 140 words a minute — slower than you speak naturally. The three marked beats are what buy the time; take them rather than slowing every word.
- **The play passage is the one to rehearse.** It is the longest unbroken stretch and the only genuinely new idea in the video. Everything else a viewer can guess; this they cannot.
- **"One point one."** Say it, don't say "video one dot one." It is the last thing they hear and it is an instruction.
- **Direct address.** "Your country," "your minister" — per §4.4. If a line drifts into "one might," it is the wrong line.
- **Do not** name ITU, Giga or the contract number on camera. Attribution belongs in the description and the end card, where it can be corrected without a re-shoot.
