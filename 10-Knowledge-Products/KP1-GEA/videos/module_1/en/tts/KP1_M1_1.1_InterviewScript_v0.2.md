<!-- slide: 1 — Title card | words: 43 -->
**Nadia:** Module one, video one point one. Why your country needs a national Enterprise Architecture.
**Daniel:** Without a shared plan for your government's digital systems, every new programme rebuilds what others have already built. The country pays. And your minister cannot deliver what they promised.

<!-- slide: 2 — How the silos get created | words: 125 -->
**Nadia:** Start with the pattern itself. What does it look like from inside a ministry?
**Daniel:** You have probably seen it. One programme builds a system to register citizens. Another programme builds a second system to register the same citizens, for a different service. A third programme builds a third. Each takes years. Each is funded separately, and often each is funded by a different donor — the World Bank, the African Development Bank, a bilateral partner. None of them work together. And your citizen still fills the same form, five times, at five different counters.
**Nadia:** So which of those programmes is the one to fix?
**Daniel:** None of them. You cannot fix this inside any one programme. Each programme is doing exactly what it was funded to do.

<!-- slide: 3 — Four signs your government has no shared plan | words: 217 -->
**Nadia:** Before we go further — how many separate registers of the same citizens does your government keep? Hold that number.
**Daniel:** There are four signs that this pattern is happening in your government.
**Nadia:** What is the first?
**Daniel:** Sign one. Your ministry has more than one register of the same people. The school list has one. The national ID register has another. The social register has a third. None of them agree.
**Nadia:** And the second?
**Daniel:** Sign two. Every time two systems need to share data, you build a new connection from zero. Last year your team connected the tax office to the business register. This year you are doing the same work again for the health ministry. The connection is built from scratch, again.
**Nadia:** The third?
**Daniel:** Sign three. A vendor built one of your systems ten years ago. To change anything in it, you must call that vendor and pay what they ask. Nobody else knows how it works. The vendor knows this, and prices accordingly.
**Nadia:** And the last?
**Daniel:** Sign four. Each ministry has its own digital systems, and they do not connect across ministries. The citizen who gave her identity number to the health ministry gives it again, on paper, when she enrols her child in school. The once-only principle exists on paper. In practice, it is impossible.

<!-- slide: 4 — The country pays in four directions at once | words: 90 -->
**Nadia:** Put a cost on that. Where does the money actually go?
**Daniel:** The cost runs in four directions at once. You pay more, because you build the same things many times over. The country moves more slowly, because every new programme waits for cross-system work that nobody planned for. Your citizens carry the burden, because they fill the same form in five places. And your minister cannot deliver a flagship cross-ministry programme, because the systems will not talk to each other. That last one is the direction that reaches the minister's desk first.

<!-- slide: 5 — One root cause: there is no shared plan | words: 100 -->
**Nadia:** Four signs, four costs. One cause?
**Daniel:** One cause. There is no shared plan. No one has written down what your government's digital systems are, who owns them, and how they should fit together.
**Nadia:** And that written-down plan has a name.
**Daniel:** That shared plan is what a national Enterprise Architecture provides. The rest of this knowledge product shows you how to commission one, what it will deliver, and what you will need from your minister to make it work. The next video answers the question your minister will ask first — what an EA actually is.
