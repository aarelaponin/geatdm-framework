# AUDIO BRIEF — KP1 · Module 1 · Topic 1 · Video 1.5
## "Why PAERA-anchored"

**This document is the sole authority for the audio.** Everything the hosts say must come from
this file. Do not add examples, statistics, institutions, countries, analogies, or closing
questions that do not appear below.

---

## 0. Production spec

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | Two hosts: **Host A** (interviewer) and **Host B** (subject-matter expert) |
| Total runtime | **4 minutes 00 seconds (±10s). Hard ceiling 4:15.** |
| Audience | Government Chief Digital Officers, Directors-General, sector ministers, and their senior advisers — in low- and middle-income countries, many working in English as a second or third language |
| Register | Expert policy briefing. Collegial but professional — two senior advisers preparing a minister, not two podcasters reacting to news |
| Companion deck | 8 slides; audio must align to the slide budget in §2 |

---

## 1. Framing rule — the single most important instruction

**The listener is the government official, not the citizen being served.**

Address the listener as the person who *runs* these systems and can *fix* them. Use
"in your ministry", "your programme", "your vendor", "your minister".

- ❌ Do **not** open with, or return to, the listener standing at a counter filling in a form,
  waiting in a queue, or being frustrated by a service.
- ❌ Do **not** use "we've all been there" / consumer-complaint framing.
- ✅ The citizen appears only as the person **your** systems burden — third person, and only if
  needed; this video is about your team and your minister.

---

## 2. Slide-by-slide time budget and content

Cover the slides **in order**. Finish each slide's content before moving to the next. End each
slide's segment on a complete sentence — do not let a thought straddle a slide boundary.

**One permitted expression: "head start"** — as in the deck's "five years of head start". Use it
at most twice, exactly as the deck uses it. No other idiom or metaphor is permitted.

**Tone rule for this video: factual, not promotional.** PAERA's assets are real things a country
gets access to, not buzzwords. No sales register.

### Slide 1 — Title card · 0:00–0:15 (15 s)

Cold open, no music bed, no "welcome to the show". Host A states the topic in one or two
sentences, in this spirit:

> "Module 1, video 1.5 — why PAERA-anchored. PAERA gives your team five years of head start:
> adopt it, and the architecture work begins on day one."

Host B adds one sentence naming the stake: do not adopt it, and your first year is spent
inventing what others have already published. Then move on. **No preamble about the sources.**

### Slide 2 — The next question · 0:15–0:30 (15 s)

Host B, briefly — this is a bridge, not a segment to linger on:

- An Enterprise Architecture is the agreed picture of your government. The next question is:
  which framework do you use to draw that picture?
- You have two paths.

### Slide 3 — Two paths — one costs you a year before work begins · 0:30–1:10 (40 s)

Present the two paths in order:

1. **Design your own framework.** Hire consultants to design an EA framework specifically for
   your country — a new way of organising ministries, a new set of terms, new principles, a new
   method. This takes **twelve to eighteen months** before any architect has drawn a single
   picture of any ministry. You have paid for groundwork, not for architecture.
2. **Anchor on PAERA.** The Public Administration Ecosystem Reference Architecture — published
   in 2024 under GovStack, built for the public-sector use case, with the groundwork already
   done. Work begins on day one.

Host A may ask **one** short clarifying question here. Not more.

### Slide 4 — Five things you do not pay to invent · 1:10–2:25 (75 s)

**This is the substantive payload of the video. Number the five things out loud — "first" …
"fifth" — roughly 15 seconds each, in this order.**

1. **A way to organise ministries and authorities.** Every body is a policy unit, a regulatory
   agency or a service-delivery authority — the classification is published, not argued over for
   three months.
2. **Plain words for the basic terms.** Capability. Service. Application. Data domain. Defined
   on day one.
3. **A library of common building blocks.** Identity, payments, information sharing, registers —
   published specifications with working examples.
4. **Ten architectural principles.** From rule of law to user-centred design — already debated
   across many countries. You extend them; you do not draft them.
5. **A way to run the work.** The lifecycle — Discover, Assess, Adapt, Plan, Execute & Govern —
   with roles, decisions and sign-offs per phase.

### Slide 5 — One framework — every sector reuses the investment · 2:25–2:55 (30 s)

- PAERA works across sectors: the same framework applies to education, to health, to social
  protection, to agriculture.
- Once your country builds the EA muscle for one sector, every next sector reuses the same
  investment. The framework does not get re-bought, re-trained, re-customised.
- **The second sector is cheaper than the first. The third is cheaper still.**

### Slide 6 — PAERA connects your team to a working network · 2:55–3:20 (25 s)

Factual list, no promotion:

- PAERA is not a standalone document. It connects to a working network — building block
  specifications, **GovMarket** (a marketplace of vendor-built implementations checked for
  compliance), a certification programme, a sandbox you can test in, a shared knowledge base.
- Adopting PAERA means joining a network of countries and partners actively building these
  resources together.

### Slide 7 — In one sentence · 3:20–3:50 (30 s)

Host B delivers the close, unhurried:

- When you make the case for PAERA to your minister, this is the case. **It is not a vendor
  choice. It is the choice to start with the work other countries have already done** — so your
  team spends its time on what is specific to your country.
- That is the head start. That is what your minister is being asked to commit to.

**Close on the series handoff, spoken by Host B:** the fifth thing on that list — the lifecycle —
is the one picture to keep. The next video walks it, on one page: five phases, four sign-offs,
six months to a roadmap.

### Slide 8 — Sources · 3:50–4:00 (10 s)

One line only, then stop: "Sources are in the video description." **Do not read URLs. Do not
name section numbers. Do not summarise the sources.**

## 3. Hard prohibitions

- ❌ **No podcast outro.** No "that raises a fascinating question for you to consider", no "look
  around at the other institutions in your life", no invitation to reflect on other sectors. The
  audio ends on the series handoff in §2, Slide 7, plus the one-line sources note.
- ❌ **No "deep dive" self-reference.** Never say "deep dive", "today we're unpacking", "welcome
  to", "our sources", "the sources say", "here's where it gets interesting".
- ❌ **No invented specifics.** No named country, no cost figures, no percentages, no
  institutions beyond those in §2. The only durations permitted are the ones in §2 and §4: five
  years of head start, twelve to eighteen months, 2024, day one.
- ❌ **No sales register.** Not "game-changer", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "revolutionary".
  The network assets are stated as facts.
- ❌ **No filler.** Remove "you know", "like", "I mean", "basically", "totally", "sort of",
  "right?", "wow", "oh absolutely", "man". Reaction interjections should be rare and short.
- ❌ **No consumer-outrage vocabulary.** Not "broken", "chaos", "held hostage", "extortionate",
  "nightmare". Say: fragmented, duplicated, costly to change, locked in.
- ❌ **No idioms or metaphors beyond "head start" as permitted in §2.** Much of this audience
  listens in English as an additional language and the audio is subtitled and translated.
- ❌ **No crosstalk or interruption.** One speaker finishes, the other begins. The audio is cut
  to slide transitions, so overlapping speech breaks the edit.
- ❌ **No in-video intro or outro branding.** ITU compliance rule — the video opens on content
  and closes on the sources line.

| **building block** | "module", "component" |
| **GovStack** | "Gov Stack", "the GovStack platform" |
| **six months to a first roadmap** · **four sign-offs** | any other duration or count |
| **published in 2024 under GovStack** | any other date or sponsor |
| **twelve to eighteen months** (the own-framework path) | any other duration |
| **GovMarket** — a marketplace of compliance-checked implementations | "the marketplace", "an app store" |

Named sources may be mentioned **once each, in passing**, in a content slide only — never as a
recurring "according to the sources".

## 5. Definition of done

The audio is correct when:

1. Total runtime is 3:50–4:10.
2. A listener can tell, without seeing the slides, where each of the eight slides begins.
3. The five things are numbered aloud — first to fifth — in the deck's order, with roughly equal
   time each.
4. The two paths on Slide 3 are presented in order, own-framework first, PAERA second.
5. Nothing is said that is not in this brief.
6. The final words are the series handoff plus the one-line sources note.
7. There are audible pauses at the slide boundaries, long enough to cut against.
