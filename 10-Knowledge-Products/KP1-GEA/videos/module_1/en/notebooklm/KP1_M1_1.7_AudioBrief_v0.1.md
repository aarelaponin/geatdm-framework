# AUDIO BRIEF — KP1 · Module 1 · Topic 1 · Video 1.7
## "What you will need from your minister"

**This document is the sole authority for the audio.** Everything the hosts say must come from
this file. Do not add examples, statistics, institutions, countries, analogies, or closing
questions that do not appear below.

---

## 0. Production spec

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Format | Two hosts: **Host A** (interviewer) and **Host B** (subject-matter expert) |
| Total runtime | **4 minutes 30 seconds (±10s). Hard ceiling 4:50.** |
| Audience | Government Chief Digital Officers, Directors-General, sector ministers, and their senior advisers — in low- and middle-income countries, many working in English as a second or third language |
| Register | Expert policy briefing. Collegial but professional — two senior advisers preparing a minister, not two podcasters reacting to news |
| Companion deck | 9 slides; audio must align to the slide budget in §2 |

---

## 1. Framing rule — the single most important instruction

**The listener is the government official, not the citizen being served.**

Address the listener as the person who *runs* these systems and can *fix* them. This video is
the most direct of the module: the listener is preparing to walk into their minister's office.
Use "your minister", "tell your minister", "your CDO", "your team".

- ❌ Do **not** open with, or return to, the listener standing at a counter filling in a form,
  waiting in a queue, or being frustrated by a service.
- ❌ Do **not** use "we've all been there" / consumer-complaint framing.
- ✅ The citizen does not need to appear in this video at all.

---

## 2. Slide-by-slide time budget and content

Cover the slides **in order**. Finish each slide's content before moving to the next. End each
slide's segment on a complete sentence — do not let a thought straddle a slide boundary.

**One permitted expression: "permanent muscle"** — as in the deck's "your country's permanent
muscle for cross-cutting digital decisions". Use it once, on Slide 3 only. No other idiom or
metaphor is permitted.

**Rhythm rule:** slides 3 to 6 are the four asks. Announce each as "First ask", "Second ask",
"Third ask", "Fourth ask", so the listener hears the count build.

### Slide 1 — Title card · 0:00–0:15 (15 s)

Cold open, no music bed, no "welcome to the show". Host A states the topic in one or two
sentences, in this spirit:

> "Module 1, video 1.7 — what you will need from your minister. Four asks: a team, a Board, a
> budget, and one promise."

Host B adds one sentence naming the stake: each one is necessary — without any one of them, the
EA programme will struggle to deliver. Then move on. **No preamble about the sources.**

### Slide 2 — Suppose you have made the case · 0:15–0:40 (25 s)

- Suppose you have made the case. Your minister is convinced an EA is the right work.
- Now the harder part: agreeing the four specific things the minister must commit to.
- Each one is necessary. Without any one of them, the EA programme will struggle to deliver what
  it could.

### Slide 3 — Ask 1 — A small permanent EA team · 0:40–1:10 (30 s)

**"First ask."**

- A small permanent EA team: **two to four senior architects.** Permanent. Reporting to your CDO
  or its equivalent.
- Typically a chief architect, domain architects, a methodology lead. In smaller countries: two
  people, each carrying several domains.
- Not consultants who arrive and leave. Not a temporary unit.
- **Tell your minister: this team will exist whether or not any single programme is running.
  That is the point.** It is your country's permanent muscle for cross-cutting digital
  decisions.

### Slide 4 — Ask 2 — An EA Board with binding authority · 1:10–1:50 (40 s)

**"Second ask."**

- An EA Board with real authority. Chaired by your CDO — or by the minister directly.
- Members: sector ministry CIOs, owners of the major state registers, and where useful an
  external advisor.
- Cadence: quarterly main meetings, with ad-hoc sessions for urgent decisions.
- **The hardest part, and Host B lands it:** the mandate must be **binding, not advisory.** The
  Board reviews new digital projects against the architecture, approves cross-ministry
  integrations, enforces boundaries between architectural domains. Without binding authority,
  the EA becomes documentation nobody reads.

Host A may ask **one** short clarifying question here. Not more.

### Slide 5 — Ask 3 — About two per cent of digital budget, for five years · 1:50–2:30 (40 s)

**"Third ask."**

- A sustained budget envelope, in three parts: the initial six-month engagement that runs the
  first four phases — about **ten to fifteen senior person-months**; the permanent practice —
  your two to four architects, ongoing; and the governance overhead — Board time and occasional
  external review.
- As a share of your digital-government budget, the whole thing is typically **about two per
  cent.**
- The framing: a **five-year envelope**, not an annual line that disappears when ministerial
  priorities shift.
- **Tell the minister: this is the leverage decision.** Every other digital programme runs more
  efficiently when this two per cent is in place.

### Slide 6 — Ask 4 — One promise: the team stays protected · 2:30–3:15 (45 s)

**"Fourth ask." This is the emotional peak of the video. Give it room; slow down.**

- One promise: **the EA team will not be pulled onto the urgent project of the week.** Not by
  you. Not by the minister. Not by anyone in cabinet.
- This is the most commonly broken promise — and the one that quietly kills EA programmes in
  their second year, when the team is moved onto a flagship delivery and the architecture work
  stops.
- Get it in writing if you can. The promise must be explicit, must be visible, and must be
  recommitted whenever the minister changes.

**Host B lets this segment breathe — a clear beat before the next slide.**

### Slide 7 — Be honest about the time horizon · 3:15–3:55 (40 s)

Put the horizon to your minister honestly, in three steps:

- **Six months** to an approved roadmap. **Eighteen to twenty-four months** to a fully operating
  practice. **Five years** to mature governance.
- The minister who launches this work will not be the one who completes it. That is
  uncomfortable, but it is the truth.
- **Host B lands the distinction:** a minister who hears that as a problem is sponsoring a
  deliverable, not an EA. A minister who hears it as a feature is the right minister to
  commission this work.

### Slide 8 — In one sentence · 3:55–4:20 (25 s)

Host B delivers the close, unhurried:

- Four asks. **A team. A Board. A budget envelope. A promise.**
- Put them on a single page. Bring them to the meeting. Ask for all four together — not in
  pieces.
- With all four committed, you have what you need. Without any one of them, the work will
  struggle.

**Close on the series handoff, spoken by Host B:** and if your minister asks whether any country
has actually done this — the next video gives you four to point to.

### Slide 9 — Sources · 4:20–4:30 (10 s)

One line only, then stop: "Sources are in the video description." **Do not read URLs. Do not
name section numbers. Do not summarise the sources.**

## 3. Hard prohibitions

- ❌ **No podcast outro.** No "that raises a fascinating question for you to consider", no "look
  around at the other institutions in your life", no invitation to reflect on other sectors. The
  audio ends on the series handoff in §2, Slide 8, plus the one-line sources note.
- ❌ **No "deep dive" self-reference.** Never say "deep dive", "today we're unpacking", "welcome
  to", "our sources", "the sources say", "here's where it gets interesting".
- ❌ **No invented specifics.** No named country, no institutions beyond those in §2, no figures
  beyond the ones this brief states: two to four architects, about two per cent, ten to fifteen
  senior person-months, five-year envelope, six months, eighteen to twenty-four months, five
  years, quarterly.
- ❌ **No filler.** Remove "you know", "like", "I mean", "basically", "totally", "sort of",
  "right?", "wow", "oh absolutely", "man". Reaction interjections should be rare and short.
- ❌ **No consumer-outrage vocabulary.** Not "broken", "chaos", "held hostage", "extortionate",
  "nightmare". Say: fragmented, duplicated, costly to change, locked in.
- ❌ **No idioms or metaphors beyond "permanent muscle" as permitted in §2.** Much of this
  audience listens in English as an additional language and the audio is subtitled and
  translated.
- ❌ **No crosstalk or interruption.** One speaker finishes, the other begins. The audio is cut
  to slide transitions, so overlapping speech breaks the edit.
- ❌ **No in-video intro or outro branding.** ITU compliance rule — the video opens on content
  and closes on the sources line.

## 5. Definition of done

The audio is correct when:

1. Total runtime is 4:20–4:40.
2. A listener can tell, without seeing the slides, where each of the nine slides begins.
3. The four asks are announced aloud — "First ask" through "Fourth ask" — in the deck's order:
   team, Board, budget, promise.
4. The fourth ask lands as the peak, with a clear beat after "recommitted whenever the minister
   changes".
5. The closing recap names all four — a team, a Board, a budget envelope, a promise — and says
   to ask for all four together.
6. Nothing is said that is not in this brief.
7. The final words are the series handoff plus the one-line sources note.
8. There are audible pauses at the slide boundaries, long enough to cut against.
