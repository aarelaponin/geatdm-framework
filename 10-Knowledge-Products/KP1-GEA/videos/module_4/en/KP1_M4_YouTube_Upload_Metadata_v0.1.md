```text
Now you judge. Score the capabilities and "things are bad" becomes a map — and the

KP1 · Module 4 · 4.3 — Phase 2, Assess: find the gaps and rank them
```

### 4.4 — Phase 3, Adapt

**File:** `video/KP1_M4_4.4_Video_v0.10.mp4` · **Subtitles:** `audio/KP1_M4_4.4_Audio_v0.10.srt`

**Title:** Phase 3, Adapt — build, buy, share or sandbox, decided one block at a time

**Description:**

```text
PAERA is a starting point, not a constraint. Adapt fits it to one sector —
localised principles and a deliberate sourcing call for every building block, with
reuse as the default that build has to justify itself against.

KP1 · Module 4 · 4.4 — Phase 3, Adapt: fit PAERA and decide build, buy or share

Progressa does not draft principles from scratch. It adopts the baseline and
points each principle at its own laws — once-only against the data-protection act,
reuse-before-build against the procurement rules — and adds one its own context
needs, about offline access for rural schools. Then four kinds of decision, made on
purpose, one building block at a time: build the learner registry because nobody
else can own it, share identity and data exchange, buy exam management, sandbox the
analytics nobody is sure about yet. A procurement rule cannot make these calls. An
architect looking across the sector can.
```

**Description:**

```text
In what order, and at what cost? The deliverable is a roadmap in waves with
investment estimates — the document the minister takes to cabinet.

KP1 · Module 4 · 4.6 — Phase 4, Plan: sequence the roadmap and cost it

Four waves, each delivering something the minister can show rather than
groundwork alone: inception, the high-priority use case, the shared backbone, mass
scale. Why the flagship lands in Wave 2, inside the first year, and not after the
plumbing is finished — politically, waiting is fatal. Honest directional costing,
not quotations, because directional is enough for cabinet to commit a five-year
envelope rather than an annual line that vanishes. And the roadmap as the one page
both sides point at: the minister reads outcomes and dates, the architect reads the
sequence and its dependencies.
```

**File:** `video/KP1_M4_4.7_Video_v0.18.mp4` · **Subtitles:** `audio/KP1_M4_4.7_Audio_v0.18.srt`

**Title:** Phase 5, Execute & Govern — the review gate that stops a fourth duplicate list

**Description:**

```text
The phase that never ends. An approved roadmap becomes a project pipeline, and a
small permanent team turns it into a living practice.

KP1 · Module 4 · 4.7 — Phase 5, Execute & Govern: stand up the living EA

Three things make it real: the repository, the Board, the review gate. Watch them
work on one case — a funded scholarship programme in a hurry, whose plan is to
build its own small learner list, because integrating with the registry would mean
learning it and waiting on its team. For the project, building is faster. Every
project makes that same rational choice. The gate catches it while it is still a
line in a project plan, and the Board rules: consume the registry, do not build a
fourth list. A procurement rule could not have done this — only the
whole-of-government view, plus a Board that can say no.
```
