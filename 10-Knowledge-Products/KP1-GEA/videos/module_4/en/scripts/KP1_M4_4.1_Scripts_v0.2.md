## 4.1 Meet Progressa — a real sector with a real fragmentation problem (~3 min)

> *Progressa is a demonstration education sector with the fragmentation problem most countries share — the same learner registered in several places, identity proven on paper. Meet its institutions and its problem, because the rest of this topic runs the full method on exactly this canvas.*

### Slide — Title (4.1)

To see the method work, you need a realistic place to run it. Progressa is a demonstration country with an education sector like many across the continent — real institutions, a real fragmentation problem, and a minister who wants results.

### Slide — Five bodies: a policy unit, a service authority, two registries, a platform

Progressa's education sector has five bodies that matter. The Ministry of Education, Youth and Sport sets policy and funds schools. The National Examination Authority runs examinations and certifies results. The Learner Registry is meant to be the one list of who is a learner. The National Identity Authority owns the person identity every sector reuses. And the Digital Government Authority runs the shared data-exchange backbone and payments. Five bodies — a policy unit, a service authority, two registries, and a shared-platform provider.

### Slide — One learner, three registrations, and the three lists do not agree

Here is the problem the minister feels. A learner is registered three times — once in the school census, once by the Examination Authority for exams, once by a social grant programme. None of the three lists agree. A parent proves the child's identity on paper at every counter, because no system trusts another's. The minister has promised a single learner record that follows the child from primary school to university — and it cannot be delivered, because the systems do not fit together. This is not a Progressa problem. It is the problem most education sectors share.

### Slide — Fictional on purpose — so every step can be shown in full detail

Progressa is fictional on purpose: it shows every step without exposing any real country, and its shapes — duplicate registries, paper re-entry, a stalled flagship — are the ones you will recognise. At each step, ask what the equivalent would be in your own sector.

### Slide — Five phases, six deliverables — this is what you will watch being built

Running the method on Progressa produces six things across the five phases: a picture of what exists today, a ranked gap analysis, a localised framework with build, buy or share calls, a target architecture, a sequenced costed roadmap, and a living, governed architecture — six deliverables, produced in that order.

### Slide — In one sentence

So this is Progressa. A realistic education sector, five institutions, and the fragmentation problem most countries live with — the canvas the whole method runs on.
