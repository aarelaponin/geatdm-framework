# Health sector — a worked example of a ministry of health target architecture and roadmap

This folder holds a complete worked example of the method applied to a ministry of health: the e-health reference architecture it starts from, the target architecture of the ministry (deliverable D6), its implementation roadmap (deliverable D7), and the instruments used to validate both with the ministry. It is the worked case that stands behind the general guide `../GEATDM-Sector-Health-v1.0.md`.

**The example is set in Progressa**, the fictional demonstration country used across this repository. The material was drawn from a real engagement and made neutral:

- The country, its institutions, places, hospitals, vendors, regional bodies, people's names, projects, contract and grant references, web addresses and file paths have been replaced by Progressa equivalents or by general descriptions.
- The material that belonged to the customer itself — interviews, questionnaires, current-state findings, the customer's comments, workshop agendas and slides, and third-party documents — has been left out.
- Statistics that describe the country — its population, registrations and enrolments, facility counts, coverage percentages and programme envelopes — are given to one significant figure or as a round range: a population of about 2–3 million, about 90 health facilities, 15,000–20,000 insurance beneficiaries, a health programme of about USD 100–150 million. Every total, percentage and chart value that depends on one of them has been changed with it. The roadmap's own cost estimates are the author's, not the country's, and stand as written. Read all these figures as illustrative of Progressa.
- National laws, policies and plans are named by their kind, without numbers, years or quoted targets: the Personal Data Protection Act, recently enacted; the national digital master plan (ten-year); the national health sector strategic plan; the WHO country cooperation strategy.
- The names of the real engagement's method, vendors and utility have been replaced by general descriptions, and the product used for immunisation tracking is described as scanned paper tracking (SPT).

The e-health reference architecture in `01-reference-and-research` was drawn from a different engagement and was made neutral in the same way: its consultants, funder, integrator, national standard, national laws and decrees, and language are replaced by general descriptions or by French and Portuguese stand-ins. Its own country is Progressa too, used there as a larger illustrative country of about 35 million people, so its figures are independent of the D6 and D7 case. Published experience of other countries (Estonia, Rwanda, Kenya, Ghana, Tanzania and others), cited as precedent, is kept, with the titles and years of their own laws and strategies.

The build folders show the deliverables as they were first drafted (version 1.0) and the prompts and working notes that produced them. Where they differ from version 1.2 — for example, they still treat the data-protection law as not yet enacted — version 1.2 is the current text. The build folders and the deliverables also keep the drafts' and the customer's own inconsistency on the data-protection law — enacted in one place, pending in another — as it was carried from the source, not caused by making the material neutral.
